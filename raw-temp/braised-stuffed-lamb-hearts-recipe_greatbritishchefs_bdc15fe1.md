As [Shakespeare](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/features/shakespeare-forgotten-food-plays) once said, whilst mulling over a pint of watery ale in some tavern somewhere, the course of true love never did run smooth. And I should know, as my romantic record is littered with calamity, especially with regards to [Valentine’s Day](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/valentines-day-romantic-recipes). Take a teenage trip to the cinema for instance, to watch Alive, a story of a plane crash where the survivors kept alive by eating the dead. I paid for the tickets, I bought the [popcorn](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/popcorn-recipes) and everything was going so well until my date announced quietly during the credits at the end that she was a [vegetarian](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/vegetarian-recipes).

Another time, I spent an evening with a girlfriend penned in at rather packed local [Italian](https://www.greatitalianchefs.com/). Halfway through the meal, feeling lightheaded and a tad squiffy, I popped to the toilet to freshen up and returned to my table and carried on with conversation; eyes down, focussing on my plate. When I finally looked up, my girlfriend had changed from a blonde to a bemused brunette, which was very confusing but a piercing burn from across the room soon signalled that I was sitting at the wrong table. The boyfriend who was hovering over me didn’t look too happy either.

And then there was the night spent down at casualty with a suspected broken toe. A Valentine’s spent indoors this time with much sweat, tears and blood lavished on preparing a sumptuous meal. Boy, the girl that I was cooking for was going to be so lucky. However, when it came to plating up the [starter](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/starter-recipes), a piece of [chicken](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/chicken-recipes) had the nerve to fall on the floor and so I lashed out at it in anger and planted my foot firmly in the dishwasher. The lucky girl who came running into the kitchen after hearing my howling became my wife soon after, so in some ways that Valentine’s Day was quite fortuitous but given past history, I try not to pay too much attention to the commercial love-fest these days.

That said, if I don’t make any sort of effort for this coming Friday, I might just get it in the eye from Cupid’s arrow so the game plan I am drawing up at present is to serve up a delicious and tasty plateful of lambs’ [heart](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/heart-recipes).

Now, you might consider dishing up some glistening heart for a [romantic supper](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/romantic-main-recipes) to be another recipe for disaster, especially if you are squeamish. But with some care and attention, this ball of muscle, the most life-giving of [edible organs](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/offal-recipes) can make for excellent eating indeed. I would even go so far as to say that lambs’ hearts are offally good. _Badoom-tish._

The key to cooking heart in my opinion, as with all tough, well exercised meat, is to cook it [low and slow](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/braising-slow-cooking-recipes). With [ox heart](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/recipes/stuffed-ox-heart-recipe), which is huge in comparison to lamb, you can go down the route of marinating slices in herbs, oil and vinegar and then flash frying, treating it like [steak](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/steak-recipes). However, l prefer the warm cosseted mouthful of heart that has been braised for a couple of hours at least and this is where lambs’ heart comes into its own. The flavour is strong, there is no denying that, and the accompanying [red wine](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/red-wine-recipes) sauce is quite rich but you can lighten things up easily enough. With this recipe, I use plenty of lemon thyme and parsley in the [sausage](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/sausage-recipes) stuffing mix and I like to sprinkle gremolata (namely finely chopped parsley, lemon zest and garlic) over afterwards for an extra fresh citrus kick. And because [comfort eating](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/comfort-food-recipes) is on the agenda here, I like to serve up heart on [mashed potato](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mash-potato-recipes), although [polenta](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/polenta-recipes) or any other mashed root vegetable would also be nice.

In the preparation, there is the slightly grisly business of trimming out any nasty tubes or gristle but you can always ask your butcher to do that for you. Furthermore, by wrapping the heart in streaky [bacon](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/bacon-recipes) you do sort of hide the fact of what you are eating and bacon does deliver a sort of all-round win to a dish don’t you think?

If the suggestion of eating heart on Valentine’s Day does still wrinkles noses though, try this recipe out anyway and try blindfolding your partner before sliding a forkful of tenderness into their mouth.

Who knows where it might lead.
