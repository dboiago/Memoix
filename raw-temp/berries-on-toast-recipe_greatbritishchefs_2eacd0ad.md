Wimberries, or bilberries, are a close relative of blueberries but with a fuller flavour and deep red flesh. [Shaun Hill](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/chefs/shaun-hill) makes the most of these elusive [foraged](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/foraging-recipes) berries in this beautiful [breakfast](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/breakfast-recipes-brunch-recipes). Shaun recommends using a rich-dough bread like brioche for an extravagant finish.

First published in 2015

1

Whisk the cream, eggs, egg yolks, sugar and cinnamon together in a bowl. Remove the crusts from the bread then dip the slices into the cream mixture

-   150ml of double cream
-   2 [eggs](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/egg-recipes)
-   1 [egg yolk](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/egg-recipes)
-   1 tbsp of caster sugar
-   1/2 tsp [ground cinnamon](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/cinnamon-recipes)
-   8 slices of [bread](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/easy-bread-recipes)

2

Heat the butter in a frying pan and when bubbling, fry the bread until golden on each side. Drain on kitchen paper and sprinkle over the icing sugar

-   1 tbsp of [butter](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/butter-recipes), for frying
-   1 tbsp of icing sugar, for dusting

3

Stir together the wimberries and jam and spoon over the toast. Serve immediately

-   100g of wimberries
-   2 tbsp of [blueberry jam](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/jam-recipes)
