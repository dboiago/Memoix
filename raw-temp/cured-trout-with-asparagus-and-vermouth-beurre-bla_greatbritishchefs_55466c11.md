-   6
    
-   40 minutes plus 48 hours curing time
    

Noilly Prat’s Original Dry vermouth adds a floral, balanced complexity to sauces – in this case a beurre blanc [sauce](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/sauce-recipes) made from a shallot, wine and vinegar reduction. Paired in typical Jeremy Lee fashion with strikingly elegant and simple fare that lets the ingredients sing; dill and sugar-[cured](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/how-to-cook/how-to-cure) [trout](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/trout-recipes) and tender asparagus. Try this with Quo Vadis’s mixologist Luca Harchay's [Sweet pea martini](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/recipes/sweet-pea-martini-recipe "Sweet pea martini").

#### BEURRE BLANC

-   2 [shallots](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/shallot-recipes), finely chopped
-   100ml of [Noilly Prat Original Dry](https://www.noillyprat.com/original-dry-vermouth/)
-   50ml of white wine vinegar
-   100ml of double cream
-   200g of [unsalted butter](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/butter-recipes), finely chopped, at room temperature
-   1/2 [lemon](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/lemon-recipes), juiced

1

Cure the trout 48 hours before you want to make the dish. Mix together the sea salt, sugar and chopped dill

-   900g of [trout](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/trout-recipes), in a single piece, trimmed and pin-boned
-   50g of [sea salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes)
-   100g of caster sugar
-   2 bunches of [dill](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/dill-recipes), finely chopped

2

Rub this mixture into the trout, then transfer to a dish. Cover well and refrigerate for 48 hours, turning once halfway through

3

Lift the trout from the cure and wipe well with a dry cloth. Discard the cure. Cut the trout into 6 equal-sized fillets and lay side by side in an ovenproof dish. Set aside

4

Preheat the oven to 200°C

5

Place the shallot, vermouth and vinegar in a pan and simmer until only a teaspoon of liquid remains

-   2 [shallots](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/shallot-recipes), finely chopped
-   100ml of [Noilly Prat Original Dry](https://www.noillyprat.com/original-dry-vermouth/)
-   50ml of white wine vinegar

6

Add the cream and bring to a simmer. Remove the pan from the heat and whisking all the while, gradually add all the butter

-   100ml of double cream
-   200g of [unsalted butter](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/butter-recipes), finely chopped, at room temperature

7

Pass the sauce through a sieve (discarding the shallots), season with lemon, to taste, and reserve until needed – but not too long

-   1/2 [lemon](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/lemon-recipes), juiced

8

Add the olive oil and 6 tablespoons of water to the fish, cover and bake for 8-10 minutes depending on how pink you want it

-   4 tbsp of olive oil

9

Meanwhile, boil the asparagus in lightly salted water until just tender (about 3-4 minutes)

-   30 [asparagus spears](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/asparagus-recipes), trimmed and the bottom 3cm of the spears peeled with a paring knife or vegetable peeler

10

Place each piece of trout on a warm plate, skin-side up. Divide the asparagus evenly between the plates then pour the beurre blanc onto each plate. Serve swiftly with a [Sweet pea martini](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/recipes/sweet-pea-martini-recipe "Sweet pea martini")
