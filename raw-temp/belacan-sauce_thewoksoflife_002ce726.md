Belacan sauce is a Malaysian-style sambal with dried shrimp, fresh and dried chili, and belacan, a fermented shrimp paste. With strong notes of garlic and shallots, this Malaysian-Chinese condiment is as addictive as some of our best sauces: [chili oil](https://thewoksoflife.com/how-to-make-chili-oil/), [XO sauce](https://thewoksoflife.com/xo-sauce-recipe/), and [ginger scallion oil](https://thewoksoflife.com/raw-ginger-scallion-oil/). It may just join the ranks of your must-have condiments!

## How To Use Belacan Sauce

We used the [belacan sauce](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-sauces-vinegars-oils/#shrimp-paste) most often as a condiment with rice or congee as it’s quite salty. We would also sometimes use it as an add-in for cooking certain dishes. If you can’t find belacan, [shrimp sauce](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-sauces-vinegars-oils/#shrimp-sauce) also works as a substitute.

I remember having a tablespoon or two with some warm white rice, and I personally think that is the best way to eat it. 

![Belacan sauce on white rice](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-18.jpg)

You can also mix a healthy dollop in with noodles, which has recently become a favorite of mine for a quick meal or snack! 

## A Recipe Created from Memory

I grew up eating this dried shrimp belacan sauce. We call it _ma lai zan_ in Cantonese, or _mǎ lā zhǎn_ (马拉盞) in Mandarin.  

We used to buy it from a very small Malaysian restaurant on Doyers Street in [NYC’s Chinatown](https://thewoksoflife.com/our-favorite-chinatown-classics/). Picking up a small pint of this belacan sauce from the anonymous soda fridge in the corner of the cramped restaurant was a predictable stop after our monthly drive from the Catskills to Chinatown for fresh produce, Chinese packaged ingredients, and [dim sum](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-dim-sum-recipes/). 

Recently, I missed this belacan sauce so much that I finally decided to recreate it from memory. As I set out to develop this recipe, the tricky thing was that sometimes it was made with [dried shrimp](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-dried-preserved-ingredients/#dried-shrimp), and sometimes it was with small dried anchovies. I’ve opted for the traditional shrimp. 

It was also cooked, so it definitely shouldn’t be mistaken for a raw sambal belacan, which is usually made by pounding raw ingredients together in a mortar and pestle. It was also saucier than dried shrimp sambal (known as _hai bee hiam_ in Cantonese). 

That said, I think this shrimp belacan sauce recipe is very close to what I had growing up. I will probably continue to tweak the recipe into the future, as little taste and food memories might come back to me. For now, this recipe will be preserved for the family archives! 

![Belacan Sauce](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-17.jpg)

That said, please let us know in the comments if you have ever had something similar, and how it was made! Or if, like us, you were a fan of that little Malaysian restaurant on Doyers Street! 

## Belacan Sauce: Recipe Instructions

![Fresh and dried chili, belacan, dried shrimp, shallots, and garlic](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce.jpg)

Soak the de-seeded [dried chili peppers](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-spices-condiments/#red-dried-chili-peppers) in 1 cup of warm water for 15-20 minutes.  

While the dried peppers are soaking, de-stem and de-seed the [Holland peppers](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-chives-scallions-aromatics-peppers/#red-holland-hot-peppers), removing the membranes as well. You can leave the seeds in if you like your sauce very spicy. Cut each pepper into 3 pieces. If using, chop the Thai chilies. 

Take half of your shallots and finely chop them. With the other half, thinly slice them. (You can also chop them, but I think slices add another textural dimension to the final sauce. 

Drain the dried chili peppers, and set aside the soaking water. To the bowl of a food processor or blender, add the dried peppers, Holland peppers, [Thai chilies](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-chives-scallions-aromatics-peppers/#Thai-chili-peppers) (if using), half of the shallots (i.e. your chopped shallots, setting aside the slices for later), and garlic.

![Chilies, garlic, and shallots in food processor](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-2.jpg)

Pulse until the mixture is smooth, or if you prefer, a coarse paste. Use a rubber spatula to scrape down the sides of the bowl to ensure the mixture is evenly processed. 

![Paste of chili, shallots, and garlic in food processor](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-3.jpg)

Heat a frying pan or wok over medium heat. Add the oil and chopped dried shrimp.

![Frying chopped dried shrimp in pan with oil](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-4.jpg)

Fry over medium to medium low heat for 3-5 minutes.

![Dried shrimp after frying for 3-5 minutes](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-5.jpg)

Put the belacan in a mortar and pestle to break it up (you can also put it in a small bowl and break it up with a fork). Add it to the pan.

![Adding belacan to dried shrimp](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-6.jpg)

Cook for 3-4 minutes, until the belacan is melted into the sauce. 

![Melting belacan into oil with dried shrimp](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-8.jpg)

Add the sliced shallots.

![Adding shallots to shrimp sauce](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-9.jpg)

Cook for 3-4 minutes, until translucent.

![Shallots melted into shrimp sauce](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-10.jpg)

Next, add the chili, garlic and shallot paste.

![Adding chili, shallot and garlic paste to shrimp mixture](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-11.jpg)

Stir to combine, and add ½ cup of the reserved pepper soaking water. Cook for 5 minutes, stirring occasionally. Stir in the fish sauce and brown sugar.

![Adding fish sauce and brown sugar to belacan sauce](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-12.jpg)

Continue to cook for another 15 minutes.

![Belacan sauce simmering](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20width='600'%20height='396'%20viewBox='0%200%20600%20396'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

Let the mixture cool.

![Belacan sauce in saucepan](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-13.jpg)

And transfer to a clean jar or other airtight container.

![Spoonful of Malaysian Chinese Belacan Sauce (Ma Lai Zan)](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-14.jpg)

Refrigerate for up to 4 weeks, or freeze (it should last up to 1 year frozen). If you’re looking for other ways to use this sauce, it goes great with stir fried vegetables like [water spinach](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-water-spinach/) in our [Kang Kung Belacan recipe](https://thewoksoflife.com/kangkung-belacan-water-spinach-with-shrimp-paste/).

![Malaysian Chinese Belacan Sauce](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/belacan-sauce-15.jpg)

## Recipe

Prep: 30 minutes

Cook: 40 minutes

Total: 1 hour 10 minutes

-   Food processor
    
-   Mortar & pestle
    

-   ▢ 16 [dried Sichuan chili peppers](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-spices-condiments/#red-dried-chili-peppers) (16 dried peppers = 10g; deseeded)
-   ▢ 7 [holland chilies](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-chives-scallions-aromatics-peppers/#red-holland-hot-peppers)
-   ▢ 3 [Thai chili peppers](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-chives-scallions-aromatics-peppers/#Thai-chili-peppers) (chopped, optional for added heat)
-   ▢ 1 1/4 cup [sliced shallots](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-chives-scallions-aromatics-peppers/#shallots) (about 6 large, divided)
-   ▢ 8 cloves [garlic](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-chives-scallions-aromatics-peppers/#garlic) (8 cloves = approx. 40g)
-   ▢ 2/3 cup canola oil (or peanut oil)
-   ▢ 1 cup [dried shrimp](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-dried-preserved-ingredients/) (chopped)
-   ▢ 3 tablespoons [belacan shrimp paste](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-sauces-vinegars-oils/#shrimp-paste)
-   ▢ 1 tablespoon [fish sauce](https://thewoksoflife.com/fish-sauce/) (optional)
-   ▢ 2 tablespoons light brown sugar

-   Soak the de-seeded dried chili peppers in 1 cup of warm water for 15-20 minutes.
    
-   While the dried peppers are soaking, de-stem and de-seed the Holland peppers, removing the membranes as well. You can leave the seeds in if you like your sauce very spicy. Cut each pepper into 3 pieces. If using, chop the Thai chilies.
    
-   Take half of your shallots and finely chop them. With the other half, thinly slice them. (You can also chop them, but I think slices add another textural dimension to the final sauce.
    
-   Drain the dried chili peppers, and set aside the soaking water. To the bowl of a food processor or blender, add the dried peppers, Holland peppers, Thai chilies (if using), half of the shallots (i.e. your chopped shallots, setting aside the slices for later), and garlic. Pulse until the mixture is smooth, or if you prefer, a coarse paste. Use a rubber spatula to scrape down the sides of the bowl to ensure the mixture is evenly processed.
    
-   Heat a frying pan or wok over medium heat. Add the oil and chopped dried shrimp. Fry over medium to medium low heat for 3-5 minutes.
    
-   Put the belacan in a mortar and pestle to break it up (you can also put it in a small bowl and break it up with a fork). Add it to the pan. Cook for 3-4 minutes, until the belacan is melted into the sauce.
    
-   Add the sliced shallots, and cook for 3-4 minutes, until translucent.
    
-   Next, add the chili, garlic and shallot paste. Stir to combine, and add ½ cup of the reserved pepper soaking water. Cook for 5 minutes, stirring occasionally. Stir in the fish sauce and brown sugar. Continue to cook for another 15 minutes.
    
-   Let the mixture cool, and transfer to a clean jar or other airtight container. Refrigerate for up to 4 weeks, or freeze (it should last up to 1 year frozen).
    

Makes about 2 cups, or 32 servings—1 tablespoon per serving.
