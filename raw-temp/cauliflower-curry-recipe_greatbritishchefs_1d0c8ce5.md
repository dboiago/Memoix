When I am not in the mood for a fiery [curry](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/curry-recipes), than a pasanda always fits the bill; a pasanda is a mild, creamy curry made with yoghurt, almonds and fragrant spices such as [garam masala](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/garam-masala-recipes), [ginger](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/ginger-recipes), [cumin](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/cumin-recipes) and [coriander](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/coriander-seed-recipes). I’ve also seen recipes for pasanda with tomatoes in the base too, but the creamy almond version is my favourite. It’s a popular dish in northern India and Pakistan, with the title being a variation on the Urdu word for 'favourite' which is _pasande_ which is thought to refer to the cut of meat that is usually used in the curry, which will be a prime cut, or a favourite cut.

My version of a cauliflower pasanda is based on the meat version, but is [gluten-free](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/gluten-free-recipes) and [vegetarian](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/vegetarian-recipes), as well as being low in calories and healthy. I love the humble [cauliflower](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/cauliflower-recipes) and often cook with it – favourite recipes that often find their way to my kitchen table, are cauliflower steaks, cauliflower pizza and a beautiful whole roasted cauliflower in a spicy sauce. I also love cauliflower soup and spiced cauliflower too... I find that it’s such a versatile vegetable, and there’s more to serving it than dousing it in cheese sauce, nice though that may be!

The recipe below yields a large meal for four people, when served with rice and [naan bread](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/recipes/naan-bread-recipe), and it can be made the day before you plan to serve it, allowing the flavours and spices to permeate the cauliflower thus enhancing its flavour – just reheat it gently over a low heat and serve.
