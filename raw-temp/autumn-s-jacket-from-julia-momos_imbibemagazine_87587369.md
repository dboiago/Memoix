A bouquet of seasonal charm.

In _[The Way of the Cocktail](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/611572/the-way-of-the-cocktail-by-julia-momose-with-emma-janzen/),_ the new book from Chicago bar owner Julia Momosé (written with _Imbibe_ digital editor [Emma Janzen](https://imbibemagazine.com/author/emma/)), the cocktail recipes are organized by micro-season, a calendar system used in Japan to celebrate the nuances of seasonal shifts. One of the most memorable recipes from the fall chapter is the Autumn’s Jacket, a drink where the warm earthy qualities of barley shochu bring depth to a big bouquet of spice.

## Ingredients

-   1 1/2 oz. barley shochu (Kintaro Honkaku suggested)
-   1/2 oz. crème de cassis
-   1/2 oz. Cynar
-   1/4 oz. Bénédictine
-   [1/4 oz. simple syrup (1:1)](https://imbibemagazine.com/recipe/simple-syrup/)
-   1/4 oz. fresh lemon juice

## Preparation

In a shaker tin, combine the shochu, creme de cassis, Cynar, Benedictine, simple syrup, and lemon juice with ice. Shake to chill, then strain into a rocks glass over ice. Float a burning match around the tip of the rosemary sprig, to warm and awaken the herb, but not burn it. This provides woodsy aromatics without the char. Garnish with the rosemary.
