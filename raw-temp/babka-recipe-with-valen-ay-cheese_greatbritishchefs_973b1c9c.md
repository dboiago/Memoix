Pascal Aussignac serves up a [cake](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/cake-recipes) inspired by by his travels to Israel, a spin on babka. This version is much more simple than the traditional method using yeasted dough – instead he infused the half of the batter with black [sesame](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/sesame-seeds-recipes) paste and creates a marble effect by swirling the batters together before baking. The chef stays true to his [French](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/french-recipes) roots by serving the cake with Valençay cheese and a sweet fig leaf and honey dressing.

First published in 2017

### Metric

### Imperial

#### Babaka

-   60g of plain flour
-   300g of marzipan, (white)
-   30g of [honey](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/honey-recipes)
-   100g of [butter](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/butter-recipes)
-   5 [eggs](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/egg-recipes)
-   80g of [black sesame paste](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/sesame-seeds-recipes), (this can be made by roasting black sesame seeds and blending with a little vegetable oil)

#### Fig leaf dressing

-   100g of [fig leaves](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/fig-recipes), washed and ripped up
-   400g of grapeseed oil, or other neutral vegetable oil
-   300g of [runny honey](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/honey-recipes)

-   Blender
-   Cooking thermometer
-   Small loaf tin

1

Preheat the oven to 160°C/gas mark 3

2

Add all of the ingredients for the babka to a blender (except the eggs and black sesame paste) and blitz to combine

-   60g of plain flour
-   300g of marzipan, (white)
-   30g of [honey](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/honey-recipes)
-   100g of [butter](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/butter-recipes)

3

Once well-mixed, add the eggs slowly until fully combined

-   5 [eggs](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/egg-recipes)

4

Remove about half the batter and add the black sesame paste to the blender. Blitz the batter and sesame paste until well-combined

-   80g of [black sesame paste](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/sesame-seeds-recipes), (this can be made by roasting black sesame seeds and blending with a little vegetable oil)

5

Pour the white and black batters into a single jug at the same time (from opposite sides) so that each coloured batter occupies each half of the jug from top to bottom

6

Pour the batter into a greased loaf tin, swirling the batter as it comes out, to create a marbled effect

7

Bake for about 35–40 minutes, or until a knife comes out clean when inserted into the centre of the cake. Leave to cool before cutting

8

While the cakes are cooking, make the fig leaf dressing. Blend the oil and fig leaves at high speed in a blender for a minute or so until very well-chopped

-   400g of grapeseed oil, or other neutral vegetable oil
-   100g of [fig leaves](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/fig-recipes), washed and ripped up

9

Transfer to a pan. Stir and heat the mixture to about 80–90°C for a few minutes then leave it to cool to room temperature

10

Strain through a fine sieve, squeezing any excess oil out of the pulp, and discard the pulp. Whisk the honey and oil together until fully combined

-   300g of [runny honey](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/honey-recipes)

11

Serve with the sweet fig leaf dressing, a scattering of poppy seeds and some Valençay cheese (or another high quality goat's cheese). Decorate with cinnamon bark

-   1 [cinnamon stick](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/cinnamon-recipes), layers of bark separated
-   [Valençay cheese](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/goats-cheese-recipes)
-   poppy seeds
