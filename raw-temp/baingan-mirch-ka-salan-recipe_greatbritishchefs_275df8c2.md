Baingan mirch ka salan is a dish from the Hyderabad region of [India](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/indian-recipes "Indian"), consisting of fried baby [aubergines](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/aubergine-recipes "Aubergine") and green chillies simmered in a spiced [curry](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/curry-recipes "Curry") of [coconut](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/coconut-recipes "Coconut"), peanuts and sesame seeds. 

This recipe is taken from _Tarkari: Vegetarian and Vegan Indian Dishes with Heart and Soul_ by Rohit Ghai (Kyle Books, £25). Photography by Maja Smend.

First published in 2021

Not feeling this? See related recipes.

Rohit says: _'Baingan mirch ka salan is a traditional Hyderabadi curry. A combination of green chillies and aubergines cooked in a coconut, peanut and sesame seed curry, it’s usually served with Hyderabadi biryani, but it can be served with plain rice or roti, too.'_

## Method

1

Heat the oil for deep-frying to 160°C in a large pan. Cut a slit in each aubergine, lengthways and across, but not all the way through, and cut a slit down one side of each chilli. Deep-fry the aubergines and chillies until cooked through. Set aside

-   rapeseed oil, enough to deep-fry
-   10 [baby aubergines](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/aubergine-recipes)
-   3 [large green chillies](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/chilli-recipes)

3

Remove to a bowl. Once the mixture cools slightly, add enough water to grind them to a smooth paste, using a mortar and pestle or blender

4

Heat 1 tablespoon of the oil in the same pan and sauté the onions until lightly browned. Remove from the pan and grind to a smooth paste

-   1 tbsp of rapeseed oil
-   1 [large onion](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/onion-recipes), chopped

5

Heat the remaining oil in a second pan and add the curry leaves, cumin seeds, mustard seeds and nigella seeds and once they start to crackle, add the onion paste. Cook for 1–2 minutes, so that the paste absorbs the flavours

-   10 [curry leaves](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/curry-leaf-recipes)
-   1 tsp [cumin seeds](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/cumin-recipes)
-   1/2 tsp [mustard seeds](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mustard-seed-recipes)
-   1 tsp [nigella seeds](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/black-onion-seed-recipes)

6

Add the ginger-garlic paste and cook for 1 minute. Then add the masala and cook, covered, for 3–4 minutes

-   1 tsp [ginger-garlic paste](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/ginger-recipes)

7

Add the tamarind paste, turmeric, chilli powder, some salt and the sautéed aubergines and chillies along with 500–750ml of water. Mix well. Lower the heat to medium-low, then cover the pan and cook for 20–25 minutes, or until the vegetables are completely cooked through and the gravy is thick

-   3 tbsp of [tamarind paste](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/tamarind-recipes)
-   1/2 tsp [ground turmeric](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/turmeric-recipes)
-   1 tsp [red chilli powder](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/chilli-recipes)
-   [salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes)
