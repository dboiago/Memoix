Every year I make the statement that I will cook every single meal I eat, for the entire summer, on my barbecue. Not particularly practical for someone who writes about food; that 10 best summer [soups](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/soup-recipes) article is going to take an interesting turn for a start.

Anyway, I try my best to plug whatever gaps I can with lovely smoky charred things and I’m guessing you do too, so here’s a recipe for a wrap I’ve been enjoying recently. And before you ask, yes I do count wraps as [sandwiches](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/sandwich-recipes), because, well, all the filings are encased in bread. It’s like I would class a hot dog as a sandwich, or a [burger](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/burger-recipes). So there.

This wrap also allows me to make a case for grilling as many elements of a meal as possible, i.e. not just the meat or fish or whatever you have chosen as the main ingredient. The fire is lit, so maximize your usage of it. [Sides](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/side-dish-recipes), sauces, relishes, whatever, they can all be improved by a lick of [smoke](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/smoked-recipes).

Seen here, the [aubergine](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/aubergine-recipes), pierced and cooked whole until shrivelled and blackened, flesh scooped and used as a sauce. I pity people who have had a bad experience of aubergine; ‘it’s horrible and squeaky!” No, it’s not properly cooked. Treated correctly, it’s one of the greatest vegetables and one that really comes into its element on the barbecue. I find it hard not to chuck a couple on the corner of the grill every time I fire it up. It’s worth chucking [lemons](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/lemon-recipes) on too. Cut them in half and briefly caramelise - the acidity is reduced and sweetness coaxed out.

The lamb in these wraps is marinated in garlic, a shed-load of [cumin](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/cumin-recipes) and [pomegranate](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/pomegranate) molasses, then whipped over the grill very briefly, so the meat stays tender; too long and you’ll end up with something akin to a [kebab](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/kebab-recipes) made from squash balls.

Everything is whacked into a flatbread with feta, chilli, and heaps of fresh herbs, used like salad leaves, which is possible since they became affordable in decent bunches rather than sad plastic packets of scrawny hothouse runts.
