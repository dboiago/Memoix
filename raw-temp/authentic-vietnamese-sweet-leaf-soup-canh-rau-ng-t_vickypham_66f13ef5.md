## What is Rau Ngót?

_Rau ngót_ is commonly known in English as sweet leaf, katuk, or star gooseberry leaves.

It is a tropical, leafy green shrub native to Southeast Asia. The leaves are edible but the woody stems are not, so the leaves must be stripped from the stems before cooking.

![Authentic Vietnamese Sweet Leaf Soup (Canh Rau Ngót)](https://i0.wp.com/vickypham.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/canh-rau-ngot-vietnamese-sweet-leaf-soup-20.jpg?resize=1024%2C1024&ssl=1)

The term “sweet leaf” is a bit confusing because I don’t find the leaves to be sweet at all. I suspect it might be a misinterpretation of the Vietnamese word _ngọt_ and _ngót_ when written without accent marks.

_Ngọt_ (with a heavy/drop tone) means sweet while _ngót_ (with a rising tone) means to shrink or wilt.

![Authentic Vietnamese Sweet Leaf Soup (Canh Rau Ngot)](https://i0.wp.com/vickypham.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/canh-rau-ngot-vietnamese-sweet-leaf-soup-12-1.jpg?resize=1024%2C1024&ssl=1)

Done

This is exactly what happens when you cook a large pile of these leaves. They shrink down to almost nothing, just like spinach.

_Rau ngót_ is not sweet, not in the slightest. Rather, it has a mildly nutty, earthy flavor, somewhat similar to spinach, but with a firmer texture.

![Rau Ngot Vietnamese Sweet Leaf Katuk](https://i0.wp.com/vickypham.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/canh-rau-ngot-vietnamese-sweet-leaf-soup-1.jpg?resize=1024%2C706&ssl=1)

_Rau ngót_ (also known as sweet leaf)

Because of the firmer texture, it actually needs to be cooked down a bit longer than regular spinach.

This is also why Vietnamese cooks traditionally like to scrunch or aggressively massage the leaves before adding them to the pot, but I don’t find that really necessary.

![Rau Ngot Vietnamese Sweet Leaf Katuk](https://i0.wp.com/vickypham.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/canh-rau-ngot-vietnamese-sweet-leaf-soup-7.jpg?resize=1024%2C1024&ssl=1)

## What You Will Need

To make Canh Rau Ngót, gather the following ingredients:

-   **Rau Ngót (Sweet Leaf):** You’ll need about 1 bundle. You might have a hard time finding _rau ngót_ in Asian grocery stores. You might have better luck in a _Vietnamese_ grocery stores or from a Vietnamese friend who has it growing in their yard. If you can’t find it, substitute with regular spinach or [Malabar spinach (_mồng tơi_),](https://vickypham.com/blog/vietnamese-malabar-spinach-ground-beef-soup-canh-mong-toi-nau-thit-bam/) which has a similar texture. If using these substitutes, keep in mind that they don’t take nearly as long to cook as _rau ngót_ and only need a quick blanch.
-   **Protein:** I’m using small pieces of pork ribs here, but you can easily swap in ground pork for a quicker-cooking option. Ground chicken or ground beef also work great, or you can omit the meat entirely.
-   **Aromatics:** Fresh minced garlic or shallots for the soup’s fragrant base. If you don’t have shallots, the white parts of green onions work nicely.
-   **Seasonings:** A blend of fish sauce, salt, sugar, ground black pepper and chicken or pork bouillon powder (or MSG) perfectly balances the broth. If you prefer not to use bouillon powder or MSG, simply omit them and adjust with additional salt to taste. Just keep in mind it might not taste exactly like the traditional versions.

## How to Make Canh Rau Ngot

### Step 1: Prep the greens and aromatics

Strip the leaves from the stems by running your pinched fingers down the stems. Wash the leaves while gently scrunching them to help them soften faster during cooking. Drain and set aside.

![Preparing Rau Ngot for Soup - Strip the Leaves from the Woddy Stems](https://i0.wp.com/vickypham.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/canh-rau-ngot-vietnamese-sweet-leaf-soup-2.jpg?resize=1024%2C862&ssl=1)

Stripping the leaves from the stems

Peel and finely dice the garlic and shallots. Set aside as well.

![How to Make Authentic Vietnamese Sweet Leaf Soup (Canh Rau Ngót) - Preparing garlic and shallots](https://i0.wp.com/vickypham.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/canh-rau-ngot-vietnamese-sweet-leaf-soup-3-1.jpg?resize=1024%2C1024&ssl=1)

Shallots and garlic prepped and ready

### Step 2: Sauté the aromatics

Heat a little oil in a soup pot over medium heat. Add the minced garlic or shallots and sauté until fragrant (about 20 seconds).

![How to Make Authentic Vietnamese Sweet Leaf Soup (Canh Rau Ngot) - Make the Aromatic Base](https://i0.wp.com/vickypham.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/canh-rau-ngot-vietnamese-sweet-leaf-soup-4-2.jpg?resize=1024%2C1024&ssl=1)

Bring out their aroma by pan frying with a bit of oil

### Step 3: Pan fry the protein

Add the small pieces of pork ribs or your protein of choice like ground pork.

_I did one small hunk of pork ribs here but I don’t recommend because it took longer to become tender._

Toss pork ribs (or ground meat) with the toasted aromatics and cook until lightly browned on the outside.

![How to Make Authentic Vietnamese Sweet Leaf Soup (Canh Rau Ngot) - Make the Stock](https://i0.wp.com/vickypham.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/canh-rau-ngot-vietnamese-sweet-leaf-soup-5-2.jpg?resize=1024%2C1024&ssl=1)

### Step 4: Make the stock

Pour in the water and bring it to a gentle boil. Let it cook until the ribs are tender (about 30 minutes depending on size). You can test them by piercing the meat with a fork or chopstick. If it pierces easily, it’s done.

If using a quicker-cooking protein like ground meat, this boiling step will only take only 10 minutes.

![How to Make Authentic Vietnamese Sweet Leaf Soup (Canh Rau Ngót) - Make the Stock](https://i0.wp.com/vickypham.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/canh-rau-ngot-vietnamese-sweet-leaf-soup-6-1.jpg?resize=1024%2C1024&ssl=1)

Making stock

### Step 5: Season the stock

Season the stock to make it into a delicious broth. Add fish sauce, salt, sugar, bouillon powder, and ground black pepper, a little at a time to taste.

![How to Make Authentic Vietnamese Sweet Leaf Soup (Canh Rau Ngót) - Make the Broth](https://i0.wp.com/vickypham.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/canh-rau-ngot-vietnamese-sweet-leaf-soup-8.jpg?resize=1024%2C1024&ssl=1)

### Step 6: Add vegetables

Add the sweet leaf to the boiling broth. Let it cook for about 8 minutes, or until the leaves reach your preferred texture.

![How to Make Vietnamese Sweet Leaf Soup (Canh Rau Ngot)](https://i0.wp.com/vickypham.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/canh-rau-ngot-vietnamese-sweet-leaf-soup-9-1.jpg?resize=1024%2C1024&ssl=1)

Scrunch them into the boiling liquid a handful at a time (optional but that’s how it’s done traditionally)

![How to Make Vietnamese Sweet Leaf Soup (Canh Rau Ngot)](https://i0.wp.com/vickypham.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/canh-rau-ngot-vietnamese-sweet-leaf-soup-10-2.jpg?resize=1024%2C1024&ssl=1)

Looks like a lot but it will shrink. Hence the word _ngót_

### Step 7: Serve

Ladle the hot soup into a large bowl. Crack more black pepper for an aromatic finish and serve alongside steamed jasmine rice.

## Storage & Reheating

Allow the soup to cool completely to room temperature before storing. This prevents condensation, which can lead to faster spoilage.

Transfer the cooled soup into airtight containers, or simply store it in the fridge in the original pot with a lid. It will stay fresh for up to 3 days.

For longer storage, pour the soup into freezer-safe containers, leaving a little space at the top for expansion.

Reheat gently on the stovetop over medium heat, or in the microwave. Stir occasionally to ensure even heating.

### Description

Bring authentic Vietnamese comfort food to your table with this easy side soup. This recipe features hand-scrunched sweet leaves (also known as katuk) in a savory, steaming pork broth (though you can easily swap in your favorite protein). Serve it alongside steamed rice and other side dishes for a taste of home.

-   6 oz sweet leaf / rau ngot (1 bundle)
-   8 oz pork ribs cut into small segments (or choice of meat)
-   3 cloves garlic (minced)
-   1 small shallot minced (can sub with whites of green onions)
-   2 teaspoons [fish sauce](https://amzn.to/3ST164U)

-   1 teaspoon fine ground sea salt
-   1/2 teaspoon granulated sugar
-   1/2 teaspoon ground black pepper
-   1 quart (4 cups) water
-   1 tablespoon neutral oil (vegetable or canola)
