Every year I challenge myself to make a special dish that reminds me of my grandma or a traditional dish to [celebrate _Día de los Muertos_ (Day of the Dead)](https://muybuenoblog.com/dia-de-los-muertos-celebration-video/). Last year I shared a recipe for [_marranitos_ (little pigs)](https://muybuenoblog.com/mexican-marranitos-gingerbread-pigs/), because they were my grandmas favorite sweet bread, and the year before that I made [_pan de muerto_ (bread of the dead)](https://muybuenoblog.com/pan-de-muerto-bread-of-the-dead/), which is traditional bread seen at _Día de Los Muertos_ celebrations.

This year I wanted to make _conchas_, a particular type of [_pan dulce_ (Mexican sweet bread)](https://muybuenoblog.com/mexican-sweet-breads-pan-dulce/). Luckily for me my mom was visiting and she wanted to make them too. The pastry contains a sugar shell pattern on the top. _Conchas_ (shells) also called _pan de huevo_ and are one of the most famous Mexican pastries.

![two photos one of processor with dough and a bowl with ball of dough resting](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/magimix_food_processor-590x432.jpg)

Being from El Paso we are very lucky to have _panaderias_ (Mexican bakeries) nearby when we crave this sweet bread. The best time to buy sweet bread from a _panaderia_ is first thing in the morning when they are freshly made and warm.

![conchas pan dulce assembled on parchment paper](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/conchas-pan_dulce1.jpg "conchas pan dulce")

Now that I live in Colorado, _panaderias_ are not conveniently nearby, so I figured this was the perfect opportunity to try and make this sweet bread at home. My mom brought over her 1970’s Better Homes and Gardens Mexican cookbook with the _pan dulce_ recipe dog-eared and I had a Better Homes and Gardens Mexican edition magazine with the _conchas_ recipe dog-eared. We figured this was a sign and we needed to make them. The recipes were very different, and we decided to try out the newest magazine recipe.

![conchas cut on the tops to shape seashells ](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/conchas-cut-grooves1.jpg)

The process of making this sweet bread is not difficult to make, but it is time consuming. You need to be patient and allow the dough to rise. Once they are baking the smell transported me to my childhood.

![](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/baked-conchas-590x393.jpg)

The warm sweet bread out of the oven is well worth the wait. Accompany this Mexican sweet bread with a hot cup of [café de olla](https://muybuenoblog.com/cafe-de-olla-video-le-creuset-giveaway/) or [champurrado](https://muybuenoblog.com/champurrado-mexican-hot-chocolate/) and the first warm bite will melt in your mouth.

![plate of Mexican sweet bread conches with a sugar skull and marigolds](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/day_of_the_dead-conchas-4-590x885.jpg)

_Conchas_ have a sweet hardened crust, and the inside is thick, soft, and fluffy. With such amazing flavors it is no wonder it is one of Mexico’s most favorite sweet breads.

![conchas pan dulce on a teal plate with a magimix food processor ](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/10/conchas-pan_dulce-magimix.jpg)

Making the dough for these _conchas_ might have been challenging if made by hand, but we cheated a bit and made it using this dreamy and powerful [Magimix food processor](http://amzn.to/2s8DbRn). I have made dough in a food processor before and this processor did the job perfectly. The motor is sturdy, powerful, and very quiet.

-   ▢ 1 packet active dry yeast, 1/4 ounce 
-   ▢ ½ cup warm water
-   ▢ ½ cup warm evaporated milk
-   ▢ 1/3 cup sugar
-   ▢ 1/3 cup butter, melted
-   ▢ 1 egg
-   ▢ 1 teaspoon [salt](https://amzn.to/3vTupZx)
-   ▢ 4 cups [all-purpose flour](https://amzn.to/45btgZH)
-   ▢ ½ teaspoon [ground cinnamon](https://amzn.to/3HhhjaL)
-   ▢ ½ cup butter, softened
-   ▢ 2/3 cup sugar
-   ▢ 1 cup [all-purpose flour](https://amzn.to/45btgZH)
-   ▢ 2 teaspoons [ground cinnamon](https://amzn.to/3HhhjaL)
-   ▢ 1 teaspoon [vanilla extract](https://amzn.to/48NWEa6)

-   In a large bowl stir together yeast and the warm water. Let stand for 10 minutes. Stir in evaporated milk, the 1/3 cup sugar, the melted butter, egg, and salt. Stir in 2 cups of the flour. Gradually stir in another 2 cups flour and the ½ teaspoon cinnamon to make a dough.
    
-   Turn the dough onto a lightly floured surface. Knead for 3 to 5 minutes or until smooth and elastic. Shape into a ball. Transfer dough to a large greased bowl; turn to coat surface of the dough. Cover and let rise in a warm place until double in size (1 to 1 ¼ hour).
    
-   Meanwhile, for topping, in a medium bowl beat the softened butter with an electric mixer on medium speed for 30 seconds. Add the 2/3 cup sugar, beating until well mixed. Stir in the 1 cup flour, the teaspoons cinnamon, and the vanilla.
    
-   Punch down the dough. Cover and let stand for 10 minutes. Divide dough into 12 portions. Shape each portion into a smooth ball. Place balls about 3 inches apart on baking sheets lined with parchment paper. Press down on balls slightly. Divide topping into 12 balls; pat each ball flat. Place one round of topping on each dough ball.
    
-   Using a sharp paring knife or [concha cutter](https://amzn.to/2HbDQJQ), cut grooves on a scallop shell. Cover rolls and let rise in a warm place until nearly double in size (about 45 minutes).
    
-   Meanwhile, preheat oven to 375 degrees F. Bake rolls for 18 to 20 minutes or until light golden brown. Remove from baking sheets. Cool on wire racks. Serve warm or cool.
