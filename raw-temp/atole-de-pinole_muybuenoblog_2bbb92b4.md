## What is Atole de Pinole?

Atole is a warm and cozy Mexican drink made with masa-based flour, milk or water, sweetener, and canela (cinnamon). When it’s made with pinole—roasted and ground cornmeal—it adds a deep, earthy flavor that feels like a hug in a mug.

**Pinole is not [masa harina](https://muybuenoblog.com/what-is-masa-harina/)!** Masa harina, such as Maseca, is just pure ground corn used for things like tortillas and tamales. Pinole is different—it’s made from roasted corn and sometimes mixed with spices or sugar. Fun fact: the word _Pinole_ comes from the Náhuatl word _Pinolli_, which means “flour from corn.”

You can usually find pinole or atole mixes at your local Latin market or even order them [online](https://amzn.to/4llCtY7) if needed.

## How to Make Atole de Pinole

**Step 1: Steep the [cinnamon tea](https://muybuenoblog.com/mexican-cinnamon-tea-te-de-canela/).** In a saucepan, combine water and cinnamon stick. Simmer over low heat for 30 minutes to infuse the flavor.

**Step 2: Dissolve the flour.** Discard the cinnamon stick and turn off the heat. Stir in the pinole (or mesquite flour) until completely dissolved. Set aside.

**Step 3: Combine and heat.** Add milk, vanilla, and piloncillo or brown sugar to the pan. Heat over medium until it begins to boil.

**Step 4: Simmer.** Lower heat to medium-low and continue to cook for 10 minutes, stirring often to prevent sticking.

![hand-painted talavera mug on a wooden serving tray atop a pink and white striped kitchen towel with a cone of piloncillo, a small wooden spoon, a bunch of small pink flowers, and a green ceramic jug with the rest of the batch. ](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/atole-de-pinole-drink.jpeg)

**Step 5: Finish and enjoy.** Turn off the heat and serve warm. The atole will thicken slightly as it sits.

## Atole de Pinole Expert Tips & Tricks

**Whisk constantly** while heating to avoid lumps.

## Storage Instructions

Store leftover atole in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 3 days. Reheat gently on the stovetop, adding a bit of water or milk to loosen it up if needed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between atole and pinole?**

Atole is a traditional Mexican warm drink typically made with [masa harina](https://muybuenoblog.com/what-is-masa-harina/), water or milk, and sweeteners. Variations include [atole de vainilla](https://muybuenoblog.com/atole-de-avena/) and [champurrado](https://muybuenoblog.com/champurrado-mexican-hot-chocolate/), both made with corn masa—one flavored with vanilla, the other with chocolate. Other types use different thickeners, such as [atole de avena](https://muybuenoblog.com/atole-de-avena/) made with oats, or [atole de chocolate](https://muybuenoblog.com/mexican-hot-chocolate/), which can be thickened with masa or cornstarch. Pinole, a roasted and ground cornmeal often mixed with spices and sugar, is used in this recipe to create atole de pinole, a comforting drink with a toasty, lightly sweet flavor.

****What is pinole made of?****

Pinole is typically made from roasted and ground maize, sometimes mixed with sugar, cinnamon, or cocoa powder.

**What is atole de grano made of?**

Atole de grano is made with whole corn kernels, simmered in water with spices and thickened with masa or corn flour.

**What is atole made of?**

Traditional atole is made from masa harina or corn flour, milk or water, sweetener like piloncillo, and flavored with cinnamon and vanilla.
