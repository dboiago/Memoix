### Glass:

Serve in a [Coupe glass](https://www.diffordsguide.com/encyclopedia/329/cocktails/cocktail-glassware)

Ingredients:

1 1⁄4 fl oz

[Straight rye whiskey (100 proof /50% alc./vol.)](https://www.diffordsguide.com/beer-wine-spirits/6467/wild-turkey-101-rye-whiskey)

1 1⁄4 fl oz

[Strucchi Rosso Vermouth](https://www.diffordsguide.com/beer-wine-spirits/8200/-strucchi-vermouth-rosso) chilled

1⁄3 fl oz

[Amer Picon](https://www.diffordsguide.com/beer-wine-spirits/144/picon-amer) chilled

1⁄4 fl oz

[Bénédictine D.O.M. liqueur](https://www.diffordsguide.com/beer-wine-spirits/1634/benedictine-dom)

### Prepare:

1.  Select and pre-chill a [COUPE GLASS](https://www.diffordsguide.com/encyclopedia/329/cocktails/cocktail-glassware).
2.  Prepare garnish of lemon zest twist.

### How to make:

3.  [STIR](https://www.diffordsguide.com/g/1150/how-to-make-cocktails/how-to-stir-a-cocktail) all ingredients with ice.
4.  [FINE STRAIN](https://www.diffordsguide.com/g/1150/how-to-make-cocktails/fine-double-straining) into chilled glass.

### Garnish:

5.  [EXPRESS](https://www.diffordsguide.com/g/1269/cocktail-garnishes/citrus-zest-twists) lemon zest twist over the cocktail and use as garnish.

## Review:

Short and punchy and Manhattan-like. Best appreciated as an after-dinner digestivo or late-night sipper. Thanks to feedback and much refining, I'm happy this recipe balances the spirituous spice of rye with herbal aromatised wine and rich herbal liqueur notes.

## History:

Adapted from a recipe in Jacques Straub's 1913 _Manual of Mixed Drinks_. It also notably appears in [Harry Craddock](https://www.diffordsguide.com/encyclopedia/2345/people/harry-craddock)'s 1930 _The Savoy Cocktail Book_.

> **CREOLE COCKTAIL**  
> ⅓ Jigger Absinthe.  
> ⅔ Jigger Italian Vermouth.  
> Shake well.
> 
> Jacques Straub, Manual of Mixed Drinks, 1913

> **CREOLE COCKTAIL.**  
> ½ Rye or Canadian Club Whisky.  
> ½ Italian Vermouth.  
> 2 Dashes Bénédictine.  
> 2 Dashes Amer Picon.  
> Stir well and strain into cocktail glass. Twist lemon peel on top.
> 
> Harry Craddock, The Savoy Cocktail Book, 1930
