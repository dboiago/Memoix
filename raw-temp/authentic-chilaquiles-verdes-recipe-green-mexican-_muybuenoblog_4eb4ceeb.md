## What Are Chilaquiles Verdes?

Chilaquiles (pronounced CHEE-lah-key-lays) are a traditional **[Mexican breakfast dish](https://muybuenoblog.com/recipes/breakfast-recipes/)** made from [fried corn tortilla](https://muybuenoblog.com/homemade-tortilla-chips-fried-or-baked/) pieces simmered in salsa, then topped with cheese, onions, and sometimes eggs or shredded chicken.

The word _chilaquiles_ comes from the Nahuatl language, roughly meaning “chiles and greens.” There are two main styles: [chilaquiles rojos](https://muybuenoblog.com/red-chile-chilaquiles/) (made with red chile sauce) and chilaquiles verdes, which use a tangy tomatillo-based green salsa like the one here.

If you’re a fan of green chile, you’ll love this version — the salsa is slightly spicy, perfectly tangy, and tastes amazing with a fried egg on top. If you like roasted green chile, then you need to try these creamy and spicy [roasted green chile chilaquiles](https://muybuenoblog.com/roasted-green-chile-chilaquiles/).

## Ingredients & Substitutions

Authentic chilaquiles don’t require much — just tortillas, salsa, cheese, and eggs. Below you’ll find everything needed to make the homemade salsa verde and to assemble the dish.

### Roasted Tomatillo Salsa Ingredients

-   **[Tomatillos](https://muybuenoblog.com/tomatillos/):** Tangy green fruit that forms the base of the sauce.
-   **White Onion:** Adds a bit of sharpness. Yellow onions work too.
-   **Garlic Cloves:** Roast whole for a mellow flavor.
-   **Serrano Peppers:** Add heat and flavor; use jalapeños for milder salsa.
-   **Ground Cumin:** Optional — just a pinch enhances flavor. (If you dislike cumin, omit.)
-   **Cilantro:** Adds fresh, herbal brightness.
-   **Chicken Stock:** Or vegetable broth for a vegetarian version.
-   **All-Purpose Flour:** Thickens the sauce; swap with [masa harina](https://muybuenoblog.com/what-is-masa-harina/) for gluten-free.
-   **Salt:** To taste.
-   **Olive Oil:** Adds richness; any cooking oil works.

### For the Chilaquiles

-   **[Corn Tortillas](https://muybuenoblog.com/homemade-corn-tortillas/) & Oil:** Fry stale tortillas for best texture, or use sturdy store-bought chips (preferably from a Mexican market).
-   **[Oaxaca Cheese](https://muybuenoblog.com/oaxaca-cheese/):** Melty and mild; substitute with queso quesadilla, mozzarella, or Jack cheese.
-   **Red Onion:** For garnish; [pickled red onion](https://muybuenoblog.com/pickled-red-onions-cebollas-en-escabeche/)s add tang.
-   **Eggs:** Fry sunny-side-up or over medium.
-   **[Queso Fresco](https://muybuenoblog.com/queso-fresco/):** [Crumbled cotija cheese](https://muybuenoblog.com/cotija-cheese/) or feta works well too.

### To Assemble the Chilaquiles:

-   **Corn Tortillas & Canola Oil** – Making your own tortilla chips is a snap, and it’s a great way to use up store-bought stale corn tortillas that would otherwise be destined for the trash. Feel free to swap in [sturdy store-bought tortilla chips](https://www.huffpost.com/entry/best-tortilla-chips-mexican_l_6320b4e5e4b000d988557c5a) from a Mexican grocery store sold in a clear bag with no brand name. Those are most likely perfect for making chilaquiles. 
-   [**Oaxaca Cheese**](https://muybuenoblog.com/oaxaca-cheese/) – This mild, melty cheese is a staple in our fridge. If you can’t find it, queso quesadilla, low-moisture mozzarella, or Jack cheese may be used instead.
-   **Red Onions** – With their vibrant color and sweet taste, red onions are made for garnishing. Feel free to substitute with slivered green onions. For an added pop of flavor, try using [pickled red onions](https://muybuenoblog.com/pickled-red-onions-cebollas-en-escabeche/) instead.
-   **Eggs** – I prefer my eggs a little runny (e.g. over medium or sunnyside-up), but feel free to cook them however you prefer.
-   [**Crumbled Queso Fresco**](https://muybuenoblog.com/queso-fresco/) – This salty, crumbly, fresh white cheese is both beautiful and tasty. Crumbled [cotija](https://muybuenoblog.com/cotija-cheese/) or feta is an acceptable substitute. 

## How to Make Chilaquiles Verdes

Follow these simple steps to make restaurant-quality chilaquiles verdes at home.

**Step 1: Broil the vegetables.** Place tomatillos, onion, garlic, and serranos on a foil-lined baking sheet and broil for 8–10 minutes until blistered.

![Roasted tomatillos, serrano peppers, and garlic cloves on a foil-lined baking sheet ready for making salsa verde.](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/roast-ingredients.jpg)

**Step 2: Blend the salsa verde.** Add roasted vegetables, cumin, cilantro, chicken stock, flour, and salt to a blender; purée until smooth.

**Step 3: Cook the sauce.** Heat olive oil in a skillet, pour in the salsa carefully (it may splatter), bring to a boil, and simmer for 6–8 minutes until slightly thickened.

![Freshly blended green chilaquiles sauce made with roasted tomatillos, chiles, and cilantro in a blender.](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/salsa-sauce-verde-pozole-740x1110.jpeg)

![Salsa verde simmering in a skillet with a wooden spoon, the base for authentic chilaquiles verdes.](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/salsa-verde-enchiladas-740x1110.jpeg)

**Step 4: Fry the corn tortillas.** Cut tortillas into triangles. In hot oil (350–365°F), fry until golden and crisp. Drain on paper towels.

![Stack of stale corn tortillas cut into wedges, ready to be fried into crispy chips for chilaquiles verdes.](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/cut-corn-tortillas-740x493.jpg)

![Homemade pan-fried tortilla chips in a cast-iron skillet, golden and crisp for making green chilaquiles.](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/fried-corn-tortilla-chips-740x493.jpg)

**Step 5: Assemble the chilaquiles.** Add chips to the pan with 2 cups of the cooked salsa verde. Toss quickly until coated, then sprinkle with Oaxaca cheese and melt slightly.

![Chilaquiles verdes in a black cast-iron skillet topped with melted white cheese and coated in salsa verde.](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/chilaquiles-verdes.jpg)

**Step 6: Garnish and serve.** Top with red onions, crumbled queso fresco, and fried eggs. Garnish with cilantro and avocado slices.

![Overhead view of chilaquiles verdes served on gray plates topped with fried eggs, queso fresco, and red onion, surrounded by tomatillos and fresh ingredients.](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/chilaquiles-verdes-eggs.jpg)

## Optional Variations

-   **Gluten-Free:** Use [masa harina](https://muybuenoblog.com/what-is-masa-harina/) instead of flour to thicken the sauce.
-   **Add Protein:** Top with [Instant Pot Shredded Chicken](https://muybuenoblog.com/easy-shredded-chicken/) or leftover [pork carnitas](https://muybuenoblog.com/braised-crispy-pork-carnitas-dutch-oven/).
-   **Chilaquiles Divorciados:** Serve half with salsa verde and half with salsa roja for a fun twist.
-   **Vegan:** Use vegetable broth and skip the eggs and cheese, or try vegan crema.

## Chilaquiles Verdes Expert Tips & Tricks

-   Line your baking sheet for easy cleanup.
-   Use a high-powered blender for silky smooth salsa.
-   Don’t rush thickening the salsa — it should coat the chips, not soak them.
-   To save time, use store-bought salsa verde and chips, but warm them before mixing.

## Storage & Reheating

-   **Salsa:** Store in the fridge for up to 1 week or freeze up to 6 months. You can also use it for making delicious [enchiladas verdes](https://muybuenoblog.com/pulled-pork-enchiladas-in-salsa-verde/) for dinner. 
-   **Chilaquiles:** Best eaten fresh! If you have leftovers, refrigerate for up to 2 days.
-   **Reheat:** Warm gently in a skillet to revive the crunch — avoid the microwave if possible.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use store-bought tortilla chips for chilaquiles verdes?**

Yes! Use thick, sturdy chips (not thin restaurant-style ones). Toss them briefly in warm salsa to avoid sogginess.

**What’s the difference between chilaquiles verdes and chilaquiles rojos?**

_Verdes_ are made with tomatillo-based green salsa, while _rojos_ use a red chile sauce. Both are traditional Mexican breakfasts.

**Can I make chilaquiles ahead of time?**

Yes — make the salsa verde in advance and fry tortillas ahead, but combine only before serving.

**Are chilaquiles verdes spicy?**

They can be! Serrano peppers give medium heat. Use jalapeños or remove seeds for milder flavor.

**How do I keep my chilaquiles crispy?**

Toss the chips in salsa right before serving and eat immediately for the best texture.

#### Roasted Tomatillo Salsa

-   ▢ 1 pound [tomatillos](https://muybuenoblog.com/tomatillos/), husked and rinsed
-   ▢ 1 white onion, quartered
-   ▢ 4 garlic cloves, unpeeled
-   ▢ 2 serrano chiles, (or 1 jalapeño for milder heat)
-   ▢ 1/8 teaspoon [ground cumin](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B007R0QYGK/?cv_ct_id=amzn1.idea.29AV8L954R0LG&cv_ct_pg=storefront&cv_ct_wn=aip-storefront&ref=exp_cov_muybueno_dp_vv_d&tag=muybue-20), (optional)
-   ▢ 1/2 cup chopped cilantro
-   ▢ 1 cup chicken or vegetable stock, (low sodium)
-   ▢ 2 tablespoon [all-purpose flour](https://amzn.to/45btgZH), (or masa harina for gluten-free)
-   ▢ 1/8 teaspoon [salt](https://amzn.to/3vTupZx)
-   ▢ 1 tablespoon [olive oil](https://amzn.to/3sKJkUh)

#### Chilaquiles

-   ▢ 8 [corn tortillas](https://muybuenoblog.com/homemade-corn-tortillas/), cut into triangles
-   ▢ 1 tablespoon canola oil
-   ▢ 3/4 cup [shredded Oaxaca cheese](https://muybuenoblog.com/oaxaca-cheese/), (or queso quesadilla or Monterey Jack)
-   ▢ 1/2 cup [crumbled queso fresco](https://muybuenoblog.com/queso-fresco/)
-   ▢ 1/4 small red onion, finely diced or pickled
-   ▢ 2 to 3 eggs, fried or cooked to preference
-   ▢ Fresh cilantro, Mexican crema, and avocado slices for garnish

-   On a baking sheet, broil tomatillos, onion, garlic, and serranos for 8 to 10 minutes. Transfer the roasted vegetables to a blender.
    
-   Add cumin, cilantro, chicken stock, flour, and salt, and purée until smooth.
    
-   Heat olive oil in a large skillet. Pour in blended sauce carefully to avoid too much splatter and bring it to a boil. Lower heat and simmer till thickened for about 6 to 8 minutes.
    
-   Cut corn tortillas into triangles. In a large skillet heat oil and fry corn tortilla pieces until crunchy. Lower heat to low.
    
-   Add 2 cups of sauce and mix only long enough to coat all the tortillas. Top with cheese while still over a low flame and cook just long enough for the cheese to melt.
    
-   Remove from heat and sprinkle with onions. Serve with eggs any style and top with crumbled queso fresco.
    

-   _About 8 medium tomatillos equals 1 pound_ — perfect for this recipe.
-   **Make ahead:** The salsa verde can be made up to 5 days in advance or frozen for up to 6 months.
-   **Storage:** Once combined with sauce, chilaquiles are best eaten fresh. Leftovers can be refrigerated up to 2 days.
-   **Reheat:** Warm gently in a skillet over low heat to maintain crispness.
-   **Substitutions:** Use jalapeños for a milder salsa, or make it vegan by using vegetable broth and skipping the cheese and eggs.
