These fried wontons are a classic Thai street food you can find at many outdoor markets in Thailand. They are filled with a flavourful and tender pork filling and are served with a sweet and spicy chili sauce. Both the wontons and the dip are easy to make, and they make the most munchy, addictive party snacks. Want to make them even easier? Get your guests to wrap their own wontons and make it a party! They cook in literally 1 minute, so you can enjoy them fresh off the fryer!

## Cultural Context: Fried Wontons in Thailand

Fried wontons are called _giew tod_ เกี๊ยวทอด in Thai. You might think of wontons as a Chinese food, and they are, but they arrived in Thailand along with Chinese immigrants and quickly became popular amongst Thai people. In Thailand, you can find fried wontons most commonly sold by street carts or vendors at open air markets.

The filling is a little different from Chinese wontons you might have had. We flavour it with _saam gler,_ our trinity of herbs that are used in many Thai dishes.

In Thailand, they're typically served with a dip that's equal parts sweet and spicy. This can be easily replicated by combining some sriracha with store bought sweet chili sauce!

## Ingredients and Important Notes

Here are ingredients you'll need and important notes about them. For amounts, see the f[ull recipe card below](#recipe).

-   **Wonton wrapper**. When you're at the store make sure you buy wonton wrappers and not "dumpling wrapper." which are thicker. Wonton wrappers are very thin and are yellow in colour. They come in round or squares, both will work but if you want them to look like the shape in the photo you have to get the square ones. I find square ones easier to wrap into nice shapes; with the round ones you're more limited by what shapes you can turn them into.
-   **Ground pork,** preferably not lean as the fat will make the filling more juicy and tender, but if you can only get lean, it is totally fine. You can also substitute ground chicken or ground turkey with no problem.
-   **Garlic**
-   **White peppercorns**. White pepper is what's traditionally used in Thailand, though it is okay to sub black pepper if you don't have it.
-   **Cilantro roots or cilantro stems**.
-   **Soy sauce**. I use [Thai soy sauce](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0086XQ4XK?asc_item-id=amzn1.ideas.2BDCQAZK1BH7N&linkCode=ll2&tag=hotthakit-20&linkId=81a2ede7539384446b57db62897d3d1f&language=en_US&ref_=as_li_ss_tl) _(affiliate)_ for this recipe, but Japanese or Chinese soy sauce will work.
-   **Oyster sauce**. Oyster sauce varies in quality, my preference is for [Lee Kum Kee Premium](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B006R1UE52?asc_item-id=amzn1.ideas.2BDCQAZK1BH7N&th=1&linkCode=ll2&tag=hotthakit-20&linkId=a933df15871673d10256af3b246b81d6&language=en_US&ref_=as_li_ss_tl) _(affiliate)_ which I think is the best one. To learn more about how to choose good quality oyster sauce, read my [guide to oyster sauce and taste test](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/oyster-sauce-101/).
-   **Sugar**, just a touch to balance the saltiness of the soy sauce and oyster sauce. The filling will not taste sweet.
-   **Cornstarch**. This is added to help absorb moisture from the filling so it doesn't sog out the wonton wrapper too quickly. You can substitute equal amounts of all-purpose flour or another starch you have on hand.
-   **Green onion,** finely chopped.
-   **Toasted sesame oil,** optional. This adds an extra sesame aroma to the filling, but it not necessary.

#### Spicy & Sweet Chili Sauce

You can simply dip the fried wontons in store bought sweet chili sauce, but in Thailand you'll find that they are served with a dip that is spicier and less sweet. This can be easily achieved by combining two simple things:

-   **Thai sweet chili sauce**, a store bought one is perfectly fine, but I also have an easy recipe for a [homemade sweet chili](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/sweet-chili-sauce/) sauce that is even better!
-   **Sriracha**, this will add acidity and heat to the sweet chili sauce, creating a more balanced, less sweet dip. The popular rooster brand sriracha is fine, but any other brands will also work. For the DIY minded, I also have an easy recipe for [homemade Thai-style sriracha!](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/sriracha-hot-sauce/)

## How to Make Fried Wontons

Here's an overview of the steps. Be sure to [watch the full video tutorial](#recipe-video) to ensure success, and follow the detailed [recipe card below](#recipe) when ready to cook!

![Process shot for how to make fried wontons, step 1](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/1-1.jpg)

1.  Pound the garlic, peppercorns and cilantro stems or roots into a paste. This mixture of these 3 herbs is called _saam gler_ and is a basic herb paste in Thai cuisine.

![Process shot for how to make fried wontons, step 3](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/3.jpg)

2.  To the ground pork, add the _saam gler_ herb paste, soy sauce, oyster sauce, sugar, cornstarch, and sesame oil.

![Process shot for how to make fried wontons, step 4](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/4.jpg)

3.  With a gloved hand, knead the seasoning into the pork. Once mixed, gently mix in the green onions.

![Process shot for how to make fried wontons, step 5](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/5.jpg)

4.  Wrap the wontons, add about 1 teaspoon of filling into the middle of the wonton wrapper. Then dip your finger into water and wet two adjacent edges of the wrapper. This water will act as glue to seal the wrapper. (Tip: it is best to see the wrapping in [the video](#recipe-video).)

![Process shot for how to make fried wontons, step 6](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/6.jpg)

5.  Close up the wonton wrapper into a triangle, squeezing out as much air as possible so you do not have a big air bubble trapped inside. You can stop here and make simple wonton triangles, or proceed with the next step for a "fancier" shape.

![Process shot for how to make fried wontons, step 7](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/7.jpg)

6.  Wet the two outside tips of the triangle with water, then fold them towards the center and press them together.

![Process shot for how to make fried wontons, step 8](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/8.jpg)

7.  Heat frying oil to 325 °F (160 °C) and fry the wontons for about 1 minute only, just until golden brown. Keep flipping them over so they are evenly golden. and the bubbling subsides. (The stopping of bubbling indicates that the wrapper is crispy.)

![Process shot for how to make fried wontons, step 9](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/9.jpg)

8.  When done, the bubbling of the oil should start to subside, which tells you they're crispy. The wontons should be golden brown, keeping in mind they will darken a little bit further out of the oil. Fish them out using tongs or a wire skimmer and drain on paper towel.

![Process shot for how to make fried wontons, step 10](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/10.jpg)

9.  Let the wontons cool slightly before eating. In the mean time mix up the dipping sauce by stirring the sweet chili sauce and the sriracha together.

![Finished image of fried wontons on a plate](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/11.jpg)

10.  Enjoy while they are fresh and crispy!

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

-   **_D_o not overstuff**. The most common mistake is for people to overstuff the wontons. Don't put anymore than 1 teaspoon of filling in; I know it doesn't look like enough, but trust me, it is. Put too much and you will have a hard time wrapping, and more importantly, the wrappers might burn before your big ball of filling has enough time to cook through.
-   **Press out all the air.** When you fold wontons, get out as much air as possible before sealing them. Any trapped air will expand when you fry, and since the air is sealed inside, it'll create a big bubble in your wontons. In the [video tutorial](#recipe-video) I show you an example of this happening.
-   **Measure oil temperature.** While this isn't strictly necessary, if you're not used to deep frying "by feel," it can be difficult to guage the correct oil temperature. And since wontons cook so quickly, if your oil temp is too high they might burn before they cook. This is [the best instant-read thermometer](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DC8MWQ8K?asc_item-id=amzn1.ideas.2XY3YP95FMVHQ&th=1&linkCode=ll2&tag=hotthakit-20&linkId=c7c1992614d5c66a1a6afda83c13ad1f&language=en_US&ref_=as_li_ss_tl) that's worth every penny (_affiliate_). It reads temperature quickly and accurately, and I use it almost everyday for one thing or another. I never deep fry without it!

## Advance Prep Tips

**You can wrap wontons the day before,** and place them in a well sealed or well wrapped container in the fridge. Fry them as per usual when ready to serve.

**You can also freeze raw wontons.** The only thing to keep in mind is when wonton wrappers are frozen, especially thin ones, they become super brittle and the edges will break off very easily. So be careful how you store them in the freezer. Don't just throw them into a freezer bag!

I recommend freezing them flat in a tray, then you can consolidate them into a box, with some paper towel for some cushioning. And don't toss the box around once they're frozen! They take a little bit longer when fried from frozen, but not by much.

### Description

These crispy wontons has a flavourful pork filling and are served with a spicy-sweet dipping sauce. They are the perfect party appetizer and a guaranteed crowd pleaser. They can be prepped in advance and cook up in only 1 minute! 

* * *

-   3 cloves garlic
-   ½ tsp (2.5 ml) white peppercorns
-   2\-3 cilantro root or 6\-8 cilantro stems, chopped
-   250 g (9 oz) ground pork
-   1 Tbsp (15 ml) soy sauce
-   1 Tbsp (15 ml) oyster sauce
-   2 tsp (10 ml) cornstarch
-   1 tsp (5 ml) sugar
-   1 tsp (5 ml) sesame oil, optional
-   1 small green onion, finely chopped
-   About 40 wonton wrappers (see note)
-   Frying oil, as needed.

#### Dipping Sauce

_You can simply dip them in the Thai sweet chili sauce (store bought or [use this recipe here](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/sweet-chili-sauce/)), but for something a little tangier, spicier, and less sweet, try this combo!_

-   3 Tbsp (45 ml) Thai sweet chili sauce
-   2\-3 tablespoon (30-45 ml) Sriracha hot sauce

* * *

1.  Using a mortar and pestle, pound the garlic, peppercorns and cilantro roots/stems into a fine paste.
2.  If your mortar is big enough to mix everything in, you can continue the recipe in the mortar, otherwise transfer the herb paste into a mixing bowl.
3.  Add the ground pork, soy sauce, oyster sauce, cornstarch, sugar, and sesame oil (if using) and mix thoroughly using a gloved or clean hand. 
4.  Once mixed well, mix in the green onions.
5.  **Wrap the wontons** by first placing only 1 teaspoon of the filling into the middle of the wonton wrapper. Dip your finger into water and wet two adjacent edges of the wrapper. Fold the wonton into a triangle, pressing out as much air as possible, and seal it along the watered edges. You can stop here and fry them as triangles, or to make them into a "Chinese gold" shape, wet the two outside tips of the triangle, and bring them towards the center and press them together to seal. (Tip: If instructions are unclear, refer to the step-by-step photos above or watch the video tutorial.)
6.  Heat about 1.5-2 inches of frying oil in a wok or pot, and heat the oil to 325 °F (160°C).
7.  Without crowding the pot, fry the wontons for about 30 seconds to 1 minute, flipping them as needed so all sides are evenly browned. They are done when the wrappers are golden and the bubbling has mostly subsided (a sign that the wrappers are crispy.) Keep the heat low, if your oil temp is too high the wonton wrappers will burn before the filling cooks. Fish them out with a wire skimmer and drain on a paper towel-lined plate. Repeat with the remaining wontons.
8.  Let the wontons cool slightly before you eat. Meanwhile, mix up the dipping sauce by stirring the two sauces together. Start with 2 tablespoons of sriracha, and add more if you want it spicier. You can adjust the ratio of either sauces to taste.
9.  Serve while still warm and crispy. Enjoy!
