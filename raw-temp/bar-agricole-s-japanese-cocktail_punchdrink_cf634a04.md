## Thad Vogler | San Francisco

At San Francisco’s Quince, where Bar Agricole is in residence, overseeing the bar program, [the Japanese Cocktail is a standout](https://punchdrink.com/articles/japanese-cocktail-bar-agricole/). Thad Vogler uses Bar Agricole’s own [biodynamic California brandy](https://baragricole.com/products/biodynamic-california-brandy), a blend of barrels aged 8 to 15 years, for the base, complemented by housemade orgeat and aromatic bitters. The farm that grows the grapes for the brandy and for the high-proof spirit that stabilizes the bitters is the same one that grows the almonds that go into the orgeat. “So it’s a single-estate, grower-producer cocktail,” says Vogler.

##### Ingredients

Serving: 1

-   1 1/4 ounces California brandy , such as Bar Agricole Biodynamic California Brandy
-   1/2 ounce orgeat (see Editor’s Note)
-   2 dashes aromatic bitters

##### Directions

1.  Add all ingredients to a shaker with ice and shake until chilled.
2.  Double strain into a coupe glass.

##### Editor's Note

**Orgeat**  
8 ounces (227 grams) almonds  
1/2 ounce (14 grams) blanched apricot kernels (optional)  
1 1/2 cups (355 milliliters) water, plus more for soaking  
about 200 grams organic evaporated cane sugar (the exact amount will vary; see directions)  
1/4 cup (49 milliliters) brandy, such as Bar Agricole Biodynamic California Brandy  
1 teaspoon orange flower water, such as Nielsen-Massey

Lightly toast the almonds (and apricot kernels, if using) in the oven at 375°F for 15 minutes.  
Place the toasted almonds in a bowl, rinse once, then cover with cold water and soak for 30 minutes.  
Drain and discard this water. Chop the toasted nuts in a food processor until they reach a uniformly fine texture. Occasionally, stop and stir the ground nuts (aka nut meat) so the pieces on the bottom don’t turn to butter.  
Transfer the nut meat into a large bowl and add the 1 1/2 cups of water. Let stand overnight.  
The next day, pass the mixture through a fine-mesh chinois strainer. Save the strained liquid and nut meat in separate containers. Gently heat the liquid to 125°F (50°C). Add the nut meat back into the liquid, and let it stand for 30 minutes to 1 hour.  
Pass through a chinois, and again save the liquid to reheat to 125°F (50°C).  
Put the nut meat back into the warmed liquid for a third time, and let it stand for 30 minutes to 1 hour.  
Press the nut meat through a multilayered cheesecloth (or nut milk bag) to extract as much liquid as you can.  
Discard the solids, and weigh the nut milk. Multiply that number by 0.88 to find how much sugar to add. (You want a little less sugar than the amount of liquid. For example, if your liquid weighs 227 grams, you’ll use 200 grams of sugar.) Pour the strained liquid into a pot and add the sugar.  
Over medium heat (180°F), stir to dissolve the sugar, then bring the mixture to a boil, stirring occasionally until the liquid reaches the syrup stage, 230°F to 235°F. Remove from heat immediately. (Do not boil too long, otherwise the sugar will crystallize when it cools.)  
Once the almond syrup is cooled, add the brandy and orange flower water. Store the finished orgeat in the refrigerator for up to 3 weeks.
