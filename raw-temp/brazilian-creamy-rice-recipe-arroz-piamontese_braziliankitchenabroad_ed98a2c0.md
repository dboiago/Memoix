## Arroz a Piamontese: What is This “Brazilian” Creamy Rice?

This creamy parmesan rice recipe, what we call _Arroz à Piamontese_ in Brazil, is rich, luscious, and loaded with flavor. Made with a little wine, cream, butter, mushrooms, and parmesan, it’s our answer to risotto, but way easier to make.

Despite the name, this creamy rice recipe is not from Piedmont, Italy. It was actually born in Brazil, created by Italian immigrants who couldn’t find starchy rich rice to make traditional risotto with. So they did what immigrants do best: they improvised with what they had.

Settling in São Paulo and Rio de Janeiro, they reimagined risotto using everyday white rice, cream, mushrooms and plenty of cheese.

The result is this creamy, elegant-yet-homey dish that became a sensation, especially when paired with _medalhão ao molho madeira_ ([filet mignon steaks](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/filet-mignon-steaks/) with [Madeira sauce](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/madeira-sauce-recipe-molho-madeira/)), a combo that ruled Brazilian restaurant menus from the ’50s through the ’90s. (And if you grew up in Brazil, you should definitely remember this from that “fancy” restaurant your family went to for special occasions.)

This duo is iconic. It melds rich flavors in a way that feels elegant yet familiar.

[![collage of two images showing Creamy Parmesan Rice in a bowl from two different angles; one of them with the rice topped with a saucy steak](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps.png)

This rice dish is nostalgic for so many of us because it’s a symbol of celebration and adaptability, and of the delicious things that happen when cultures meet in the kitchen.

This recipe is also super flexible. I like using canned or jarred mushrooms for that slight tang. This is how my mom used to make it, and exactly where my memory goes after years of ordering this dish at restaurants for special occasions. But if you prefer fresh, you can absolutely sauté your own white mushrooms instead.

You can also control just how creamy it is. Tweak the cream, butter, and cheese to get the consistency you like. If I want it extra velvety, I bump up the cream and cheese. But when I’m pairing it with something rich and saucy, like a Madeira sauce, I keep it a little thicker, like you see in the photos.

Whether or not you grew up with this dish, you’re in for a treat. It’s rich, comforting, and somehow still feels a little fancy. Basically, it’s the kind of creamy rice you make when you want something indulgent, a little retro, and completely irresistible. As an added bonus, it will pair perfectly with pretty much anything: from steak, to roasted meats, or even on its own with a spoon.

I hope you love this recipe as much as I do!

#### REMEMBER!

**Changing a recipe can lead to failure. Substitutions or tweaks might significantly impact your results, so for the best experience, try to follow the original recipe as closely as possible.**

-   **White rice** – I use long-grain white rice, but medium-grain white rice should work just fine. The key is using a plain rice, so please do not use jasmine rice, basmati rice or brown rice.
-   **Chicken stock** – homemade or store-bought chicken broth or stock work. Just keep an eye on the salt levels, especially since parmesan is salty, too. You can use vegetable broth, as well, if that is all you have.
-   **Butter** – we use this twice. Once to sauté the aromatics and again to finish the dish. I prefer unsalted, but salted is totally fine. Just taste as you go.
-   **Onion** – for that base layer of flavor. You want to use a yellow or white onion.
-   **[Brazilian sofrito](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/brazilian-sofrito/)** – or freshly minced garlic.
-   **Parmesan cheese** – freshly grated is a must, for the dish itself and for serving in the end! The pre-grated cheese usually has anti-caking agents that mess with the texture. Use the finer side of your grater for best results.
-   **White wine** – use a dry white like **Sauvignon Blanc**, **Pinot Grigio**, or an **unoaked Chardonnay**. These offer crisp acidity and subtle flavor that won’t overpower the creamy richness of the dish.
-   **Heavy cream** – for that luscious, creamy texture.
-   **White pepper** – optional, but I love it here because it blends in without adding black flecks. But black pepper is fine, too. Your call!
-   **Sliced mushrooms in conserve** – the canned or jarred kind bring that nostalgic tang I love, but sautéed fresh mushrooms work just as well. Throw those in with the aromatics to cook instead of at the end, as the recipe specifies.
-   **Parsley** – optional, for garnish and a little color pop.

## How to Make This Creamy Rice Recipe

**Prep and measure**. Grate the parmesan using the finer side of your grater, then measure, dice and prep all the other ingredients so they’re ready when you need them.

**Sauté aromatics**. Melt the butter in a medium pan over medium heat, then add the onions and garlic (or your sofrito). Sauté just until translucent and fragrant – you don’t need to brown them, as this will alter the flavors.

_Optional:_ If using fresh mushrooms, add them now, sauté until tender, then remove and set aside for later.

[![collage of two images showing how to measure in and cook rice before assembling the creamy parmesan rice recipe](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps-3-1024x683.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps-3.png)

**Toast the long-grain rice**. Add the unwashed rice to the pan with a little salt (about ½ teaspoon [Diamond Crystal kosher salt](https://amzn.to/3TtqviE), or adjust based on how salty your stock is). Stir for a few minutes to coat each grain in the butter and aromatics.

**Deglaze and reduce**. Pour in the white wine and cook just until it’s mostly reduced – enough to lose the sharp alcohol edge but still keep its flavor depth.

[![collage of two images showing how to deglaze a pan of rice with wine from above](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps-4-1024x683.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps-4.png)

**Cook the rice**. Stir in the stock, bring to a simmer (but do not boil), reduce the heat, and cover.

Let it cook gently until the rice is **_al dente_** – firm with a little bite.

_Tip:_ If the rice isn’t quite done, add a splash of water or stock (about 1-2 tablespoons), cover again, and cook a little longer until the texture is right.

[![collage of two images showing how to add stock to the pot of rice and what the rice should look like after cooking](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps-5.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps-5.png)

**Finish the rice**. When the rice is just right, stir in the remaining butter, the heavy cream, the grated parmesan cheese, and the drained mushrooms (or the sautéed ones).

Season with fresh white pepper (or black pepper) to taste, and turn off the heat.

[![collage of two images showing how to measure in mushrooms, as well as cheese to the creamy parmesan rice](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps-6.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps-6.png)

**How to adjust the texture:** Want it creamier? Add more cream, cheese, and/or butter – but stir gently to avoid everything turning into mush.

Using finely grated cheese helps prevent clumping, as the cheese will melt faster.

[![collage of two images showing how to make the creamy rice recipe creamier by adding more cream and butter to it](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps-7.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps-7.png)

**Garnish and serve**. Add a sprinkle of fresh parsley and another dusting of parmesan if you like, then serve right away while it’s warm and creamy.

[![collage showing two images of how to garnish the final Arroz a Piamontese with additional parmesan, as well as fresh parsley](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps-8.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Creamy-Rice-Recipe-Steps-8.png)

## How to Store Leftovers:

Store any leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to **3–4 days**. Because this rice recipe has dairy, it’s best not to leave it out for long after serving.

## How to Reheat:

To reheat, place the rice in a saucepan over medium-low heat and stir in a splash of cream to loosen it back up.

You can also reheat it in the microwave – just cover and stir halfway through to make sure it warms evenly.

Don’t forget to taste and adjust the salt or cheese, if needed, before serving.

Servings 4 Servings

Calories 454 kcal

Prep Time 15 minutes

Cook Time 25 minutes

Total Time 40 minutes

-   1 cup of long grain white uncooked and unwashed
-   1 ½ cups of chicken stock
-   3 tablespoons of unsalted butter, split
-   ¼ cup of diced onions roughly half of a small onion
-   2 teaspoons of [Brazilian Sofrito](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/brazilian-sofrito/), or 2 cloves of garlic , freshly minced
-   1 cup of freshly grated parmesan cheese grated finely
-   ½ cup of a dry white wine
-   2 tablespoons of heavy cream
-   8 oz can of sliced mushrooms in conserve , drained

-   Box grater

-   Melt two tablespoons of the butter in a medium pan over medium heat.
    
-   Add the diced onions and the garlic. Sauté just until translucent and fragrant — no browning needed.
    
-   Add the unwashed rice to the pan and season with salt to taste. Stir for a few minutes to coat each grain in the butter and aromatics.
    
-   Pour in the white wine, and cook just until it’s mostly reduced.

-   Serve immediately with additional freshly grated parmesan and minced fresh parsley, if desired.
