Helen Graves' [barbecued lamb](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/barbecued-lamb-recipes) breast recipe sees the meat covered in an aromatic spice rub and cooked low and slow over hot coals, though if the weather isn't playing ball feel free to [slow-cook](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/braising-slow-cooking-recipes) in the oven. The ribs are served with a malt vinegar [sauce](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/sauce-recipes), based on the famous recipe from Alabama, but with a smattering of fresh herbs.

First published in 2017

This is a riff on southern [American](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/american-recipes) barbecue but I’ve given it a British twist. The rub is made with white pepper, mustard powder and bay leaves and the sauce is based on an Alabama vinegar sauce. I love it because you can really taste the malt vinegar. It’s a thin sauce, which you can either pour onto the ribs or (my preference) use for dipping.

### Imperial

#### Spice rub

-   1 tbsp of [ground white pepper](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/peppercorn-recipes)
-   1/2 tbsp of [onion powder](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/onion-recipes)
-   1/2 tbsp of garlic powder
-   1 tbsp of brown sugar
-   1 tbsp of [flaky sea salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes)
-   1 tbsp of [mustard powder](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mustard-recipes)
-   8 [bay leaves](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/bay-leaf-recipes), crushed in a pestle and mortar into flakes

#### Malt vinegar sauce

-   200ml of malt vinegar
-   2 tbsp of caster sugar
-   2 tbsp of [mint](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mint-recipes), leaves picked and finely chopped
-   2 tbsp of [tarragon](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/tarragon-recipes), leaves picked and finely chopped
-   4 tbsp of [mayonnaise](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mayonnaise-recipes)
-   6 dashes of Worcestershire sauce

-   Barbecue with a lid

1

These ribs are best slow cooked on a barbecue but of course can still be slow cooked in an oven at 160°C/gas mark 3 for 2.5–3 hours. If cooking on a barbecue, set up a kettle barbecue for indirect cooking

3

Place the lamb belly, skin-side down, on the opposite side of grill to the coals and pop the lid on. Set the air flow to 1/4 open at the base and leave a small crack (2mm) in the vent holes on the lid

4

Cook for 3 hours, the heat will initially be around 200°C but will drop through the cooking and settle at about 120°C for the bulk of the cooking. You should turn the belly every 45 minutes

5

To make the dipping sauce, add the sugar and finely chopped herbs to the vinegar and give it a good stir until the sugar has dissolved

-   200ml of malt vinegar
-   2 tbsp of caster sugar
-   2 tbsp of [mint](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mint-recipes), leaves picked and finely chopped
-   2 tbsp of [tarragon](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/tarragon-recipes), leaves picked and finely chopped

6

Add the mayo and whisk until it's incorporated. Add the Worcestershire sauce and give it a final whisk

-   4 tbsp of [mayonnaise](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mayonnaise-recipes)
-   6 dashes of Worcestershire sauce

7

Once the ribs are cooked, remove from the barbecue, allow to cool a little and chop them roughly. Pour over about 1/2 of the sauce, if you like, retaining the other half for dipping
