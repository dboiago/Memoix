## Cashew Chicken in Thailand

In Thai, this dish is called _gai pad med mamuang himmapan ไก่ผัดเม็ดมะม่วงหิมพานต์ ,_ and I swear that whole thing just says "cashew chicken stir fry!" It is simply a stir fry of chicken and cashews, but the details aren't particularly prescriptive, so you can find cashew chicken that use different vegetables or have different tasting sauces.

Some people might think of cashew chicken as more a Chinese dish, and in fact, this dish started out in Thailand as a Chinese dish. You can tell by the presence of sesame oil, which we don't use very much of in Thai cooking. But over time, Thai people put our spin on it and adapted it to better suit the Thai palate.

While cashew chicken is very common in North American Thai restaurants, in Thailand it's a little less common. Unlike [pad gaprao](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/holy-basil-stir-fry/) or [green curry](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/green-curry-new-2/) which can be found everywhere on almost every street corner, most of the time cashew chicken is served in sit-down restaurant that specialize in Chinese or Chinese-Thai cuisine.

## Ingredients and Notes

Here are all the ingredients for Thai cashew chicken with important notes about them. It looks like a lot, but many of these you probably already have on hand. And if anything looks unfamiliar, not to worry - I'll explain everything you need to know!

![](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/cashew-chicken-ing-1.jpg)

#### The Stir Fry

-   **Chicken thigh or breast,** boneless skinless, but into bite-sized pieces. I prefer chicken thighs for this dish as they are juicier and less prone to overcooking. If using breasts don't cut them thinner than ½-inch to prevent them from drying out too quickly.
-   **Roasted cashews**, **unsalted**. If starting with raw cashews, make sure you roast them until they're well browned, not light golden (see pic below). If starting with pre-roasted cashews, I roast them _again_ (350°F, 8-10 mins) to get them darker as I find most commercial roasted cashews are too light. Darker-roasted cashews are crunchier and nuttier, which makes a difference in this dish! (See more under [Pro Tip](#pro-tip)).
-   **Onion**, 1-inch diced. Get sweet onions if they're available, otherwise regular yellow or white onions are fine.
-   **A mix of green, red and/or yellow bell peppers**, 1-inch diced. If you don't like bell peppers you can change up the veg. Carrots and mushrooms both work really well; I personally love shimeji mushrooms.
-   **Garlic**, roughly chopped
-   **Green onions,** halved lengthwise and cut into 1-2 inch pieces. Or roughly chopped if you prefer them in small pieces.
-   **Spicy dried chilies (optional).** Though optional, they are a classic part of cashew chicken in Thailand. They serve mostly as garnishes but people can also break them up and eat them for extra heat. You can use any kind you want, I uses the generic dried chilies you find at Chinese markets.

![lightly roasted cashews vs dark roasted cashews on a baking sheet](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/extra-roasted-cashews.jpg)

Left: Store-bought roasted cashews are quite light-roasted. Right: Roasting them further gets them darker, nuttier and crunchier. And they hold up better in the saucy stir fry. If roasting from raw, aim for the colour on the right.

#### Cashew Chicken Sauce Ingredients

If you have any questions about these sauces, see my [Thai Cooking Sauces Explained](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/salty-sauces/) article. If you plan on making it often, make a big batch of sauce and it'll keep in the fridge for months!

-   **Soy sauce**. I use Thai soy sauce, but regular or light soy sauce works too.
-   [**Fish sauce**](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fish-sauce-101/)
-   [**Oyster sauce**](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/oyster-sauce-101/)
-   **Thai chili paste,** store bought (pic below) or [homemade](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/thai-chili-paste/). Thai chili paste is a sweet-savoury thick paste that is _loaded_ with umami. It's made mainly from dried chilies, shallots, garlic and dried shrimp. For store bought I like Mae Pranom Brand, but the more common Pantai works well too.
-   **Sugar**. You may or may not need the sugar as some Thai chili paste is quite sweet.
-   **Golden Mountain Sauce (optional)**, this is another type of Thai soy sauce. You can use Maggi Seasoning instead if you have it, or sub and equal amount of fish sauce.
-   **Toasted sesame oil** **(optional).** It'll still taste good without it!

## Step-By-Step

Here's a bird's eye view of the steps, but the full instructions are in the [recipe card below](#recipe). To ensure success I highly recommend watching [the video tutorial](#recipe-video) before you start! This is a very fast-cooking dish, so it's always good to know the whole process ahead of time.

![process shots for making cashew chicken steps 1-4](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/1-2.jpg)

1.  Marinade chicken in soy sauce. If using breast, add a little water.
2.  Combine all sauce ingredients together.
3.  Heat oil in a wok until very hot and spread the chicken in one layer and allow to brown.
4.  Once browned, toss the chicken until fully cooked. Remove from the pan.

![process shots for making cashew chicken steps 5-8](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/2-1.jpg)

5.  In the same wok, add the garlic and cook till golden.
6.  Add the onions and cook briefly.
7.  Add bell peppers, chicken, and the sauce mixture.
8.  Toss on high heat for about a minute until everything is well combined and heated through.

![process shots for making cashew chicken steps 9-10](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/3-1.jpg)

9.  Turn off the heat, then toss in the cashews and green onions.
10.  Enjoy with some jasmine rice!

## Pro Tips: The Little Things That Maximize Flavour

-   **Dark-roasting cashews make them crunchier and nuttier, and that makes then pop.** Some brands of commercially roasted cashews are quite light in colour, and aren't that crunchy.
-   **Marinating the chicken while you prep makes it flavourful inside and out.** The sauce is super flavourful, but it's only sitting on the outside. For the best result your chicken should also be flavourful inside.
-   **Browning chicken adds flavour without the need to fry.** Restaurants often dredge the chicken in flour and then deep fry them before tossing them into the stir fry. But if you just let the chicken brown in the wok, you'll develop that deeper "fried chicken" flavour, no frying required!
-   **Adding onions after the garlic keeps them crisper.** You might be tempted to cook onions first along with the garlic to get them soft and sweet, but here, the sauce is already sweet, and the chicken is tender, so minimally-cooked onion can better provide balance.

## How to Make Cashew Chicken Faster

If you love cashew chicken and, like my husband, want to eat it again and again but don't want to do all the prep every time, I got you. Here are a couple of ways you can prep ahead and save time when the urge for cashew chicken strikes:

**Make a big batch of cashew chicken sauce**

1.  Make 3, 4 or even 10 times the amount of sauce in this recipe. Be sure to scale all ingredients proportionally. Because sugar doesn't dissolve well in the thick sauce, I would first dissolve it in just a little bit of hot water before adding it to the rest of the sauce ingredients. Stir to mix well.
2.  Measure the final volume of the sauce before you put it away, and divide it by the number of batches to get the volume that you need per batch. Write this amount on a piece of tape and tape it to the jar so you don't forget. Keep it in the fridge, it'll last indefinitely. Stir the sauce well before using.

**OR..Start with my Universal Stir Fry Sauce**

1.  If you don't want cashew chicken THAT often, make a jar of my [universal stir fry sauce](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/universal-stir-fry-sauce/), which is super useful for all sorts of things, including this recipe.
2.  **For the recipe given below, use 3 tablespoon of the universal sauce instead of the oyster sauce, soy sauce, Golden Mountain Sauce, and fish sauce.** When you cook, you'll just need to add the chili paste, sugar and sesame oil.
