## Recipe Quick Overview - The TD;LR

**What is it?** _Gaeng ped bped yang_ \- Thai red coconut curry with Chinese BBQ duck, pineapple, tomatoes, and Thai basil.

**How to make it?** Buy a Chinese BBQ duck from an Asian supermarket or Chinese restaurant. Debone the duck and simmer the bones to extract an incredible broth. Then make a basic Thai red curry sauce with the coconut milk and duck broth, and add the duck meat, pineapple, tomatoes and Thai basil. If you're efficient, it can be done in under an hour from start to finish.

**Why you'll love it.** If you already love a basic red curry, the complexity of this curry is going to blow you away. The combination of the 5-spice roasted duck and red curry is a true cross-cultural match made in heaven, and the pineapple keeps the dish bright and fresh. There are lots of ways to prep this dish in advance.

## Cultural Context - How a Chinese Duck Met a Thai Curry

The name of the dish is _gaeng ped bped yang_ แกงเผ็ดเป็ดย่าง_._ **_Gaeng ped_ means red curry, and _bped yang_ refers to Chinese roast duck** (or Chinese BBQ duck). It might strike you as odd that Thai people would put a quintessentially Chinese dish into a quintessentially Thai curry. But this isn't something I came up with during a creative breakthrough in the shower, it is a traditional Thai dish that I've seen since I was a kid.

**There's a lot more Chinese influence on Thai food than most people realize.** There is a huge number of Chinese people in Thailand, my family included, and the migration has been going on for hundreds of years. Naturally, the Chinese brought with them many cooking techniques and ingredients that have firmly become part of Thai cuisine. Noodles, soy sauce, oyster sauce, heck the whole idea of stir frying, all came to use from the Chinese. That iconic stir fried noodle dish we call Pad Thai? It wouldn't be here without the Chinese.

Most of the time the Chinese influence on the dish is subtle enough that most people don't stop to think anything of it, but in this dish, it's glaringly obvious!

## Can I roast my own duck?

I mentioned that buying a roast duck is not a cheat, that this is a dish where you're _supposed_ to use a professionally roasted chinese BBQ duck. I know some of you DIY-minded overachievers are itching to ask, "but what if I WANT to roast my own duck?"

Let me first explain that in Thailand, roasting is not something that home cooks do. in fact, Thai cuisine does not involve roasting at all! Therefore Thai kitchens do not have ovens. This is why for this curry, you're _expected_ to buy the duck from professional Chinese chefs who have mastered the craft of roasting the duck...and who have ovens! This is a curry where Thai people combine the expertly roasted Chinese duck with the flavours of Thai red curry.

But of course you can roast your own duck, if you're so inclined! You should know that roasting a Chinese duck is not like roasting a chicken - to do it properly, it's going to take you a whole day, if not two. And if you've spent that kind of effort on the duck, you would probably want to savour the duck by itself, and not throw it into a curry!

## Step-By-Step

Here's a bird's eye view of the process. The full instructions are in the [recipe card](#recipe) below, and if this is your first time, I highly recommend [watching the video tutorial](#recipe-video) to ensure success!

![process shots for how to make a thai duck curry steps 1-4](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/1.jpg)

1.  Debone the duck and chop the meat into bite-sized pieces (if you need guidance, see below for instructions). Place the duck bones and wings into a pot, cover with water and simmer for 30-45 mins.
2.  Remove the bones and discard, reserve 1 ½ cups of the stock for the curry, freeze the rest for other soups (recipe ideas below)
3.  Bring ¾ cup of the coconut milk to a boil and add the curry paste and shrimp paste (if using).
4.  Stir frequently until the paste is thick, and coconut oil starts to sizzle out of the paste. (If the oil doesn't separate but the paste is very thick, just move on.)

![process shots for how to make a thai duck curry steps 5-8](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2.jpg)

5.  Add the remaining coconut milk, the duck stock, palm sugar and about half of the fish sauce. Bring to a boil.
6.  Add the pineapple pieces.
7.  Add the duck meat, reserving some sliced breast pieces and a drumstick for garnish if desired. Simmer for 3-4 minutes.
8.  Pierce the tomatoes with the tip of a paring knife to create vent holes, then add them to the curry and turn it off immediately. Taste and adjust seasoning with the remaining fish sauce.

![process shots for how to make a thai duck curry steps 9-10](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/5.jpg)

9.  Stir in the Thai basil leaves off heat.
10.  Plate in a wide bowl and top with the reserved duck meat. Brush some curry sauce on top for a shine. Enjoy with jasmine rice!

## How to Debone a Roast Duck

Or a roast chicken for that matter! It is much easier than it sounds. The whole process takes me less than 5 minutes all-in.

![proces shots for how to debone a roast duck steps 1-4](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/3.jpg)

1.  Cut the skin between the leg and the body.
2.  Open the leg up like a book until the thigh joint pops out of the socket which looks like a round bit. Use your knife to then cut around that joint to separate the whole leg from the body.
3.  Separate the drumstick from the thigh by slicing at about 45 degrees between the two. If you're slicing through the joint, as you should, your knife should go right through with little resistance. If it feels hard, you've hit the bone, so move your knife slightly to one side or the other until you can slice through easily.
4.  Cut the meat off the thigh bone. If you want, leave the drumsticks whole for garnish, or you can remove meat from it also.

![proces shots for how to debone a roast duck steps 5-7](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/4.jpg)

5.  Make a cut in between the breasts, all the way down to the rib cage and along the whole length of the breast. You want your knife slightly off to the side of the middle so you're not hitting the keel bone.
6.  Use your hand, with the help of the knife, to peel the breast off the bones, and once the meat is all off, cut the skin to free the breast. Repeat with the other breast.
7.  Cut the thigh meat into bite-sized pieces, and slice the breasts nicely. Set the nicer breast aside for a final garnish.

The duck stock from the bones will have a subtle flavour of the Chinese five spice, soy sauce, and a bit of sweetness. It's a great base for all kinds of soups! Here are some ideas.

-   **Make a Noodle Soup.** Make the broth from my [duck noodle soup](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/duck-noodle-soup/) recipe using the duck stock instead of the chicken stock. You can just add chicken or whatever protein you've got, no need to have another duck meal!
-   **Make a tom yum soup.** Tom yum soup goes very well with 5 spice flavour, and I didn't make it up, it's something that is done in Thailand! Take my [tom yum chicken recipe](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/tom-yum-chicken/), and use your duck stock instead of the chicken stock.
-   **Or use it in any braises or stews!** Beef stew, chilli, pulled pork, braised chicken thighs. You can't really go wrong!

## Advance Prep

There are many ways to prep ahead, so comes serving time it'll be a breeze!

-   **Debone the duck and make the duck stock in advance.** The duck stock will last in the fridge for a week, and can be frozen indefinitely. The duck meat will also last in the fridge for up to a week.
-   **Make the curry in advance,** but leave the tomatoes and basil until the end. The curry actually keeps very well in the fridge, but it's best to add the tomatoes and basil at the time of serving so they don't overcook from being reheated.
-   **If you're making your own curry paste,** the paste can be frozen.

## Storing and Reheating Leftovers

This curry actually keeps quite well in the fridge, and you will be able to enjoy the leftovers for up to a week with little compromise.

Reheating in the microwave or on the stove top is fine, but remember to stir it regularly (every 1 minute if microwaving) for even heating.

Be careful not to overheat it when reheating as the tomatoes will overcook and turn mushy. If you have more Thai basil, adding some fresh ones won't hurt!
