For many people, massaman is their favourite of all Thai curries because of the tender braised chicken, flavourful potatoes, and aromatic spices. **It's so popular that it was once voted "the most delicious food in the world** by a CNN poll! I'll show you a hack for making a quick semi-homemade massaman curry paste that will beat anything you can buy. I've been making massaman curry since I was a kid in Thailand, and I promise it'll beat anything you get at a restaurant!

## ​What is Massaman Curry?

In the context of Thai cuisine, massaman curry is unique in that it is thicker and richer than others like **[green curry](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/green-curry-new-2/)** or **[red curry](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/red-curry-chicken-squash/)**. It also uniquely uses a lot of dry spices such as cinnamon, cloves, and cumin, whereas other Thai curries rely mostly on fresh herbs. This makes the flavour of massaman more reminiscent of Indian curries. The use of potatoes, a vegetable otherwise quite rare in Thai cuisine, is also its identifying feature.

![a plate of massaman curry chicken poured over rice](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/1-1.jpg)

Massaman curry is believed to have come to Thailand via the south, from the Malay people who are Muslims. This is why in Thailand massaman curry is considered a Muslim-Thai food. Because of that, you will find massaman curries in Thailand made with chicken, beef, or even goat, but never pork, despite pork being the most popular meat in Thailand!

Massaman is typically a mild curry, so it is also one that's perfect starter curries for kids or for those with low spice tolerance. For another mild Thai curry that similarly has lots of dry spices, try **[Thai yellow curry.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/yellow-curry-chicken/)**

## Ingredients and Important Notes

Here are all the ingredients you'll need for massaman and important notes about them. For amounts, check out the [full recipe card](#recipe) below.

![ingredients for massaman curry](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/3-1.jpg)

-   **Bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs and/or drumsticks.** Trim off excess fat from chicken thighs. If you want to substitute beef, check out this luxurious **[massaman beef short rubs recipe](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/beef-massaman-curry/)**, or this [**Instant Pot massaman curry**](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/instant-pot-massaman/) which uses chuck. Pork is not typically used for massaman curry because it is a dish of Muslim origin.
-   **Coconut milk.** See my post here on **[how to choose the best coconut milk.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/coconut-milk-101/)**
-   **Massaman curry paste.** If you can find premade massaman curry paste, you can use it, but I recommend you make a quick semi-homemade paste using store bought red curry paste as a base (recipe follows). It will be much better!
-   **Palm sugar,** or substitute light brown sugar or granulated sugar
-   **Fish sauce,** see my post for **[how to choose the best fish sauce](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/fish-sauce-101/)**
-   **Tamarind paste**. Tamarind is added for a little acidity which helps balance the richness of the curry. You can use pre-made Thai tamarind paste (also labeled as tamarind concentrate) or [**make you own**](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/how-to-make-tamarind-juice/) from pulp. If you're unfamiliar, read this article **[all about tamarind](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/tamarind-everything-you-need-to-know/)**. If you can't find tamarind, a touch of Worcestershire sauce or vinegar will be fine in a pinch.
-   **Bay leaves.** Totally optional, throw them in if you've got some!
-   **Potato**. You can use any kind but it's best to avoid starchy varieties such as Russet as they fall apart easily when overcooked. I like Yukon Gold, an all-purpose potato, but waxy potatoes also work great. Some prefer **sweet potato**, but be sure to use yellow-flesh sweet potatoes and NOT orange yams which are too soft and watery.
-   **Yellow or white onion**, cut into 1 cm strips vertically.
-   **Roasted whole peanuts**. I prefer roasting my own from raw peanuts as the flavours are much fresher, but pre-roasted is fine in a pinch.
-   **[Jasmine rice](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/how-to-cook-rice/)** for serving.

#### Quick Massaman Curry Paste

![ingredients for semi-homemade massaman curry paste
](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/4.jpg)

If you can find a good brand of massaman curry paste—great, but if you have a little extra time, I recommend **making this quick, semi-homemade version using store bought red curry paste as a base**. I figured out this trick after realizing that massaman curry paste is basically red curry paste PLUS some spices, but commercial massaman pastes don't add much spices due to cost, so at home, we can make a version that's fully loaded and create a much more aromatic curry.  
\*If you like to DIY you can also make **[homemade massaman curry paste](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/massaman-curry-paste/)** from scratch.

-   **Cloves**
-   **Green or white cardamom**, in Thailand we use white cardamom, but green cardamom works just fine and they're much more widely available.
-   **Coriander seeds**
-   **Cumin seeds**
-   **Ground cinnamon**
-   **Ground nutmeg,** or freshly grated
-   **Red curry paste,** store bought. A good brand is important here. I recommend Maeploy, Aroy D, or Namjai. Thai Kitchen brand is not recommended unless you need a curry that is not spicy at all (for kids, perhaps), and you will want to use the whole jar for this recipe. You can also read about my [Red Curry Paste Review](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/curry-paste-review/) for more about how I make these recommendations.
-   **Fermented shrimp paste (optional).** Thai fermented shrimp paste (_gapi)_ is a thick purplish brown paste that adds umami and depth to Thai curries. We add it to all curries in Thailand but it is often omitted from commercial pastes to make it vegan, so for the best flavour we gotta add it back in! Note: I add shrimp paste even if the curry paste already contains it to boost the umami.

## Step-By-Step

Here's a bird's eye view of the process. If this is your first time, I highly recommend watching the full video tutorial first to ensure success. For the full recipe with ingredient amounts, see the [recipe card](#recipe) below.

![Process shots for making massaman curry steps 1-4](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/1-2.jpg)

1.  For the semi-homemade massaman curry paste: Toast the cumin seeds over medium high heat, moving constantly, until the darken slightly. Remove from heat.
2.  Toast all the other whole spices, moving them constantly, until the coriander seeds darken slightly. Remove from heat.
3.  Grind the spices into a powder in a mortar and pestle or coffee grinder. Then add the ground cinnamon and nutmeg.
4.  Add the shrimp paste and red curry paste and pound to mix. If using a coffee grinder, transfer to a mixing bowl and mix with a spoon.

![Process shots for making massaman curry steps 5-8](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/2-2.jpg)

5.  Optional step: add a little oil to a heavy bottomed pot (the one you'll use to make the curry) and heat over medium high heat until hot. Sear the skin side of the chicken without moving for about 4 minutes until browned. Remove and repeat with the remaining chicken.
6.  Pour off excess oil, then return the pot to the stove and about ½ cup of the coconut milk. Bring to a boil, scraping the bottom to release browned bits.
7.  Add the curry paste and stir to mix, reduce heat to about medium.
8.  Keep sauteing the paste for 3-4 minutes, until the oil starts to separate from the paste. If after 4 minutes the oil does not separate, you can move on; some coconut milk has been processed to prevent separation.

![Process shots for making massaman curry steps 9-12](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/3-2.jpg)

9.  Add the remaining coconut milk and stir to mix. Add bay leaves (break/bruise them first), then add all of the palm sugar, half the fish sauce, and half the tamarind.
10.  Add the chicken, along with any juices collected in the bowl. The chicken should be almost fully submerged; if not, add a little more water.
11.  Partially cover the pot and bring to a gentle simmer. Simmer for 35 minutes.
12.  After 35 minutes, the chicken should be almost tender but not quite there. Oil floating on the surface is the iconic look of massaman and there's no need to skim it, but if there's way too much oil for your taste, feel free to skim some off.

![Process shots for making massaman curry steps 13-15](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/4-1.jpg)

13.  Add the potatoes, onions and half of the peanuts, and push the potatoes down so they are just barely submerged. If there's not enough liquid, add a little more water. Don't worry about submerging the onions as they will wilt down.
14.  Simmer for 10-15 minutes until the potatoes can be pieced through with a fork easily. Turn off the heat.
15.  **Taste** and add more fish sauce and/or tamarind paste as needed. Make it strongly seasoned as it will be served over rice. Garnish with more peanuts and enjoy!

## Storage, Advance Prep and Reheating

Massaman curry keeps very well. In fact, I recommend making it one day in advance for even better flavour. In the fridge, **in an airtight container, the curry will keep for up to a week.**

**You can freeze massaman curry paste,** and I recommend freezing in single portions so it's easy to use. **Or you can also freeze the curry sauce** to make dinner even faster - so cook the paste and add the coconut milk and seasonings, but don't add the meat and veggies. Then all you have to do is thaw the sauce and add meat and veg.

**When you reheat**, microwaving is fine, but take it out and give it a stir a couple of times in between. It'll also help if you break the chicken up into two smaller chunks. **If reheating on the stovetop** I would remove the potatoes as they might overcook and fall apart during the reheat. Add the potatoes back in in the last couple of minutes.

### Description

An easy but authentic recipe for Thai massaman curry. The chicken is braised in the sauce and becomes fork tender and flavourful. It's a 1-pot dish that's Thai food beginner-friendly and is always a crowd pleaser. 

* * *

-   2 pounds (1 kg) chicken thighs, bone-in, skin-on
-   2 cups (500 ml) coconut milk, divided 
-   5 to 6 tablespoons (75 to 90 ml) Quick Massaman Curry Paste (recipe below) or store bought
-   3 tablespoons (36 g) finely chopped palm sugar or light brown sugar, packed 
-   2 to 3 tablespoons (30 to 45 ml) tamarind paste, store-bought or [homemade](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/making-tamarind-paste/)
-   2 to 3 tablespoons (30 to 45 ml) fish sauce 
-   2 bay leaves (optional)
-   10.5 ounces (300 g) waxy or all-purpose potatoes, such as Yukon Gold, cut in 1-inch (2.5 cm ) chunks 
-   ½ large yellow or white onion, cut in ½-inch (1.2 cm) strips 
-   ¼ cup (35 g) unsalted roasted peanuts 
-   Jasmine rice, for serving 

**Quick Massaman Curry Paste**

-   5 pc whole cloves
-   3 pods green or white cardamom
-   1 tsp (5 ml) coriander seeds
-   1 tsp (5 ml) cumin seeds
-   1 tsp (5 ml) ground cinnamon
-   ⅛ tsp ground nutmeg
-   4\-5 tablespoons (60-75 ml) red curry paste, store bought (see note 1)
-   1\-2 teaspoon (5-10 ml) fermented shrimp paste (optional but recommended, see note 2)

**For the Quick Massaman Curry Paste**

1.  In a small skillet, add the cumin seeds and toast over medium high heat, stirring constantly, until they are aromatic and darken slightly. Remove from the pan and set aside.
2.  To the same skillet, add the coriander seeds, cloves and cardamom pods and toast over medium high heat, stirring constantly, until the coriander seeds darken slightly and are aromatic. Remove from the pan.
3.  Transfer all the toasted spices into a mortar and pestle or coffee/spice grinder along with the cinnamon and nutmeg. Grind into a fine powder.
4.  If using a mortar and pestle, add the red curry paste and shrimp paste and pound to mix. If using a coffee grinder, transfer into a small mixing bowl, then add the red curry paste and shrimp paste and stir to mix. The curry paste can be frozen if not using right away.

**For the Curry**

1.  **Optional step: brown the chicken** (or skip to step 3). Heat a heavy-bottomed pot over medium high heat and then add a small amount of oil, just enough to grease the bottom. Add the chicken, skin side down, in 1 layer without over-crowding, and let it sear without moving for 3-4 minutes until well browned. Remove and repeat with the remaining chicken. Watch the heat so the browned bits stuck to the bottom does not burn.
2.  **Pour off the excess oil** and rendered chicken fat (you can keep it to cook other things) and return the pot to the stove.
3.  **Add about ½ cup** (**125 ml** ) **coconut milk to the heavy-bottomed pot** and bring to a boil over medium heat. 
4.  **Add the curry paste** and stir to mix well, and let the mixture thicken, stirring frequently, until the mixture is very thick and the coconut oil separates from the paste, 4-5 minutes (the oil may not separate depending on the coconut milk you’re using; if this is the case just move on). If the paste sticks to the bottom of the pot, deglaze with a bit of coconut milk and lower the heat a bit. 
5.  **Add the remaining coconut milk** and stir to mix, scraping the bottom to make sure no curry paste is stuck. Add the palm sugar, about half of the tamarind paste, and half of the fish sauce. Add the bay leaves, if using, and stir to mix well.
6.  **Add the chicken** to the sauce, along with any juices collected in the bowl, and simmer gently over low heat, partially covered, for 35 minutes. (Time to make your rice now if you haven't done so!)
7.  **Add the potatoes, onions, and half of the peanuts;** if there is not enough liquid to keep the potatoes and almost fully submerged, top it up with water (you can also top it up with coconut milk if you've got some already open). Cook the potatoes over medium heat until a fork pierces through with no resistance, 10-15 minutes depending on the size.
8.  **Taste and adjust the seasoning** with more fish sauce or tamarind paste as needed. The tamarind is added to cut the richness of the curry, but it should not make the curry taste sour. If the curry feels like it needs a "zing" add more tamarind. Garnish with the rest of the peanuts and serve with jasmine rice.

### Notes

1.  I recommend Maeploy, Aroy D or Namjai. Use Thai Kitchen only if you require a curry that has no spiciness whatsoever, and use the whole jar for this recipe. 
2.  Thai fermented shrimp paste adds umami and depth to the curry. It should be a thick purplish grey paste, and the brand most commonly available is Tra Chang. If the curry paste already contains shrimp paste, add 1 tsp, if it doesn’t you can add 2 tsp.
