Ollie Moore adds a [caramel](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/caramel-recipes)[](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/caramel-recipes) depth to plain natural [yoghurt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/yoghurt-recipes) by slowly cooking it in a water bath, which results in a thickened texture and a flavour similar to butterscotch. Served simply with a few burnished [berries](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/berry-recipes)[](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/berry-recipes)[](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/berry-recipes), it makes a simple yet healthy dessert, or perhaps a delicious alternative at breakfast.

First published in 2016

### Metric

### Imperial

-   Water bath
-   Vacuum bags
-   Chamber sealer
-   Muslin cloth
-   Blowtorch

1

Preheat a water bath to 72°C

2

Place the yoghurt in a vacuum bag and seal in a chamber sealer. Cook in the water bath for 12 hours, or until the yoghurt is thickened and a caramel-brown colour. Remove the bag and cool in a bowl of iced water

-   500g of [natural yoghurt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/yoghurt-recipes)

3

Line a sieve or colander with a muslin cloth and place over a bowl. Pour in the cooked yoghurt and allow to strain for up to an hour until it resembles a thick brown cottage cheese

4

Just before serving, whisk in just as much of the fresh natural yoghurt as needed to create a smooth texture with a light caramel colour. Divide between serving dishes

-   100g of [natural yoghurt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/yoghurt-recipes)

5

Lightly scorch the berries using a blowtorch (or under a hot grill) and serve immediately with the yoghurt and a sprig of mint cress to garnish

-   100g of [raspberries](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/raspberry-recipes)
-   100g of [blueberries](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/blueberry-recipes)
-   [mint cress](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mint-recipes), to garnish
