## Brazilian Pumpkin Compote: A Taste of Tradition

Brazilian Pumpkin Compote with Coconut, or _doce de abóbora com coco_, is a beloved traditional dessert in Brazil.

This rustic, country-style sweet is something many Brazilians associate with their childhoods. It evokes memories of grandmothers’ kitchens, mothers’ homemade treats, and cozy family gatherings.

The word **_doce_** literally means “sweet” in Portuguese and is used to describe a variety of **_doces caseiros_** (homemade sweets). These treats range from fruit preserves to rich, creamy, sticky desserts. Many — like this one — are made by slow-cooking fruit with sugar and spices.

**Doce de Abóbora com Coco** balances the natural sweetness of pumpkin with the warmth of cinnamon and cloves. The shredded coconut adds a delightful texture, too, making it different than other jams or compotes.

[![a jar of Doce de Abobora on a wooden table next to a scalloped plate holding more compote and bread slices](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pumpkin-Compote_Doce-de-Abobora-19-651x1024.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pumpkin-Compote_Doce-de-Abobora-19.jpg)

Doce de Abóbora com Coco is a true Brazilian creation, though its exact origins are debated. While **Rio Grande do Sul**, **Rio de Janeiro**, and **Minas Gerais** all claim it, it is most commonly associated with **Minas Gerais cuisine** and is cherished as a homey, traditional countryside dessert and a Festas Juninas treat.

Simple yet incredibly flavorful, this rustic dessert is pure comfort food and a taste of Brazil’s culinary heritage.

#### REMEMBER!

**Changing a recipe can lead to failure. Substitutions or tweaks might significantly impact your results, so for the best experience, try to follow the original recipe as closely as possible.**

-   **Pumpkin** — go for naturally sweet options, like **butternut squash, kabocha squash, or sugar pumpkin**. These varieties hold up well to cooking and provide a richer flavor. Avoid waterier pumpkins, like carving pumpkins, because they lack sweetness and can become too mushy. Canned pumpkin puree is also not recommended.
-   **Light Brown Sugar** — I prefer **light brown sugar** to maintain the natural color of the dish and for a slightly less sweet result. However, this is a personal preference. **Dark brown sugar** will yield a deeper, more intense sweetness. **Granulated sugar** is also a suitable option.
-   **Shredded Unsweetened Coconut** — stick to **unsweetened coconut** to prevent the compote from becoming overly sweet. The coconut adds chew and enhances the texture.
-   **Whole Spices** — we use **whole cloves and cinnamon sticks** for a warm, delicate spice infusion. Whole spices allow the flavors to develop gently without overpowering the pumpkin’s natural taste. If you prefer, you can use **ground spices**, but be cautious as they can become more intense. A little dash of **pumpkin spice** might also be a fun addition if that’s your thing!
-   **Vanilla** — I used **vanilla paste** when I photographed this recipe, but the caviar from a whole **vanilla bean** or a teaspoon of **pure vanilla extract** work just as well.
-   **Freshly Squeezed Lime Juice** — this acts as a **natural preservative** and adds a touch of acidity. It balances the sweetness and rounds out the flavors.
-   **Pinch of salt** — a small pinch of **kosher salt** enhances the overall balance of flavors and adds a subtle minerality.

**Combine the ingredients**. In a Dutch oven, add the diced pumpkin, sugar, cloves, cinnamon sticks, vanilla, lime juice, and salt. Stir to mix everything together.

There’s no need to add water to this mixture — just cover it and let the pumpkin release its moisture. This will allow the flavors to concentrate beautifully as it softens.

[![collage of two images showing how to make Doce de Abobora from above](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pumpkin-Jam_Doce-de-Abobora-com-Coco_2-1024x683.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pumpkin-Jam_Doce-de-Abobora-com-Coco_2.png)

**Cook until soft**. Cook covered over medium-high heat for about **20-30 minutes**, or until the pumpkin is soft enough to mash with the back of a spoon. You need to stir it occasionally as it cooks down.

**Reduce and thicken**. Once the pumpkin is soft, uncover the pot and continue to cook for another **20 minutes**. This will allow the excess moisture to evaporate so that the compote is not too runny.

[![collage of two images showing how to cook down Doce de Abobora in a pot](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pumpkin-Jam_Doce-de-Abobora-com-Coco_3-1024x683.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pumpkin-Jam_Doce-de-Abobora-com-Coco_3.png)

**Add the coconut**. Stir in the shredded coconut and cook for an additional **3-5 minutes**.

If you are using fresh coconut, extend the cooking time slightly to ensure longer shelf life.

[![collage of two images showing the mixing in of coconut to Doce de Abobora](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pumpkin-Jam_Doce-de-Abobora-com-Coco_4-1024x683.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Pumpkin-Jam_Doce-de-Abobora-com-Coco_4.png)

**Cool and store**. Transfer the compote to mason jars or airtight containers.

Let it cool completely before sealing and refrigerating.
