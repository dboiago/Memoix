Early versions of cornbread were eaten by Native Americans and it is still very popular in the Southern states of [America](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/american-recipes). It can be sweet or savoury and makes a great accompaniment to heavy sauces, stews and curries - or just lightly grilled with butter for an afternoon snack. If you don't have a loaf tin you can [bake](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/baking-recipes) these in muffin tins for individual servings. Try making as a snack to celebrate [American Independence Day](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/independence-day).

1

Preheat the oven to 180°C/gas mark 4. Grease a large loaf tin and dust with a coating of polenta

-   [butter](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/butter-recipes), for greasing

2

In a large bowl, sift together the polenta, flour, bicarbonate of soda and baking powder. Mix in the salt and sugar

-   175g of [polenta](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/polenta-recipes), plus extra for dusting
-   150g of plain flour
-   1/2 tsp bicarbonate of soda
-   1/4 tsp baking powder
-   50g of soft brown sugar
-   1 tsp [salt flakes](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes)

3

In a separate bowl, whisk together the buttermilk, eggs and melted butter before combining well with the dry ingredients

-   300ml of buttermilk, at room temperature
-   2 [large eggs](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/egg-recipes)
-   60g of [unsalted butter](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/butter-recipes), melted

4

Pour the batter into the loaf tin before sprinkling over a little more polenta. Bake in the oven for around 45 minutes, until a skewer inserted into the middle comes out clean

5

Once baked, remove from the oven and place on a wire rack to cool. Serve with plenty of butter
