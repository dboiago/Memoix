## Why I love This Recipe

_Oi, gente!_

Growing up in Brazil, my mom turned bananas into all sorts of things… ice cream, [cake](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/banana-bread-cake-bolo-de-farinha-de-rosca-com-banana/), you name it.

One of her favorites was a banana mousse that she topped with caramelized bananas because it’s a super easy dessert. As a kid, I used to dip _biscoito maizena_ (digestive tea biscuits) into mine, because apparently mini-chef me decided it needed some texture.

Turns out, grown-up me still agrees. So, I turned it into a parfait by adding an almond crumble that brings a buttery crunch to every bite. What started as just a mousse with a topping – like my mom used to make – is now a three-part dessert with layers, texture, and that pretty parfait look, all without losing its cozy, homey vibe.

[![a spoon drizzles caramelized bananas on top of banana mousse in a cup surrounded by other cups on a marble surface](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Banana-Parfait-10.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Banana-Parfait-10.jpg)

Unlike a Southern banana pudding with its cooked custard base made from eggs, cornstarch, vanilla pudding – or a classic frozen French parfait – this version keeps it simple with a whipped banana mousse.

The parfait vibes come purely from the layers and textures: creamy mousse, crunchy crumble, and sweet topping.

[![a hand sprinkles a crumble topping on top of banana parfait in a cup surrounded by other cups on a marble surface](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Banana-Parfait-9-683x1024.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Banana-Parfait-9.jpg)

I love that you can **make all three parts ahead** of time, so it’s great for dinner parties. Just let the crumble sit at room temperature while the topping and mousse chill in the fridge, and when you’re close to serving time, just layer into these stunning banana parfaits and go.

I also love that there are a lot of possibilities for toppings. Of course, I’m obsessed with adding these [caramelized bananas](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/caramelized-bananas/) to mine because they add an extra rich banana layer.

But [dulce de leche](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/instant-pot-dulce-de-leche/) straight from a can works beautifully, too, and going that route means one less thing to make. _I get it._ If you’re more of a chocolate person, you can melt some chocolate and shave some more on top.

Just add extra banana slices between the layers to get more fresh banana flavor and texture if you’re skipping the caramelized ones.

I hope you love this banana parfait recipe as much as I do!

_Beijinhos xx_

#### **For the Almond Crumble:**

-   **Toasted almonds:** these add a nutty richness and help create that crunchy texture. I tend to go for unsalted almonds, but you can use salted to make this layer even more interesting.
-   **Digestive cookies or biscuits:** these create the base of the crumble. I use [biscoito maizena](https://amzn.to/3KXYP4K) that you can find online or at a Brazilian market, or [Maria biscuits](https://amzn.to/3Y2heQL), which are typically available in the Latin aisle of some big supermarkets in the US. Graham crackers or Nilla wafers work great, too.
-   **Butter:** for melting and to give the crumble that toasted, golden finish. I prefer unsalted butter, but salted is OK, too. Just skip the pinch of salt the recipe calls for if you use salted.

#### **For the Banana Mousse:**

-   **Mashed bananas:** these are the star of the mousse. Make sure they’re ripe and mashed well.
-   **Heavy whipping cream:** this gives the mousse a lighter texture. Use cold cream so it can whip to stiff peaks. (You cannot whip half-and-half or milk. Please make sure you are using heavy cream or your end result will not turn out.)
-   **Sweetened condensed milk:** this adds sweetness and a little extra creaminess to the mousse.
-   **Plain Greek yogurt:** this keeps the mousse from being too rich and adds a slight tang that balances the sweetness.

#### **For the Topping:**

-   **Caramelized bananas**: this is my absolute favorite. I use my [caramelized bananas recipe](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/caramelized-bananas/) for which you’ll need bananas, sugar, butter, vanilla extract and cinnamon. You can also skip this and just use more sliced bananas and a dollop of **[dulce de leche](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/instant-pot-dulce-de-leche/), or melted chocolate** instead. Your choice!

## How to Make Banana Parfait

**Make the caramelized bananas** first so they have time to cool before you assemble your parfaits. You can find this recipe and instructions [here](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/caramelized-bananas/)!

**Prepare the crumble.** Add the almonds and digestive cookies to a [food processor](https://amzn.to/3pqUglP) and pulse until you have a coarse crumble. You can choose your texture here, so grind it finer if you want or rough, uneven pieces that’ll add more crunch.

[![almond crumble ingredients in a skillet surrounded by bananas](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Banana-Parfait-4.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Banana-Parfait-4.jpg)

**Toast the crumble.** Melt the butter in a pan over medium heat, then add the almond-cookie crumble. Stir everything together and toast for a few minutes until it’s golden brown and fragrant. All it needs is a few minutes so everything gets that perfect crunch.

Transfer the crumble to a plate or tray and let it cool completely at room temperature.

**Whip the cream.** Grab a large bowl and whip the heavy cream until you get stiff peaks. This usually takes 3-5 minutes with a [hand mixer](https://www.amazon.com/KitchenAid-KHM7210OB-7-Speed-Digital-Accessories/dp/B00GXYTW4C/ref=as_li_ss_tl?keywords=hand+mixer&qid=1571698308&sr=8-1-spons&spLa=ZW5jcnlwdGVkUXVhbGlmaWVyPUFZS0xaM0FTRDhXTE8mZW5jcnlwdGVkSWQ9QTA4MTM1MzIzRDA5M0dURE0zR0RYJmVuY3J5cHRlZEFkSWQ9QTA5NzIwMTU1SzFBWVQ0RElTOUEmd2lkZ2V0TmFtZT1zcF9hdGYmYWN0aW9uPWNsaWNrUmVkaXJlY3QmZG9Ob3RMb2dDbGljaz10cnVl&th=1&linkCode=ll1&tag=braziliankitc-20&linkId=e0600da21ba172e8afc8936cc0dadaf9&language=en_US) or [stand mixer](https://amzn.to/3SmI9EW).

Make sure your cream is cold so it whips perfectly. Once it’s whipped, pop it in the fridge to stay cold while you work on the rest.

[![collage of three images showing how to make banana mousse from scratch](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Banana-Parfait-Steps-2.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Banana-Parfait-Steps-2.png)

**Make the Banana Mousse**. In another bowl, add the mashed bananas and sweetened condensed milk. Beat on high speed for 3-5 minutes. You want the bananas as smooth as possible and everything fully incorporated.

Add the yogurt to the banana mixture and beat for another 3 minutes. This adds air and keeps the mousse light.

**Fold in the whipped cream.** Do this in two batches. Add half of the whipped cream and gently whisk it in to lighten the banana mixture. Then add the second half and fold it in carefully with a spatula.

You want to keep as much air as possible, so don’t overmix.

The mousse should be creamy and light with a texture that’s similar to a yogurt.

### Aline’s hot tips:

### **Make ahead for easy entertaining**

1.  Make the caramelized bananas and let it cool completely, then store it in an airtight container in the fridge.
2.  Make the crumble and let it cool completely, then store it in an airtight container at room temperature.
3.  Make the banana mousse and keep it refrigerated in an airtight container.
4.  Assemble the parfaits a couple of hours before serving, or right before if you want the crumble extra crunchy.

## **Assemble the Parfaits**

Best to use a glass cup so you can see the layers. You can do individual servings or use a bigger trifle jar.

Sprinkle a layer of the almond crumble at the bottom of each glass. Add a scoop of banana mousse on top. Then add a layer of the caramelized bananas.

[![three containers from above, one with mousse, one with crumble and one with a final banana parfait](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Banana-Parfait-7.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Banana-Parfait-7.jpg)

Repeat the layers until you reach the top of the cup.

Pop the assembled parfaits in the fridge until you’re ready to serve. They’re best served cold.

[![side angle of banana parfaits on a marble background, surrounded by bananas](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Banana-Parfait-16.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Banana-Parfait-16.jpg)

## How to Store:

**Assembled parfaits:** These will last about one day in the fridge without the crumble losing too much texture. After that, it’ll still taste good, but the crunch will soften.

**Separate components:** The mousse will keep in an airtight container in the refrigerator for 2-3 days. The bananas may darken a bit, but it’s still fine to eat, just not as pretty. The crumble will last longer (up to 5 days) since it’s dry. Store it at room temperature in an airtight container.

**Serving:** Keep parfaits refrigerated until you’re ready to serve so they stay cold and hold their texture.
