[Celeriac](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/celeriac-recipes) is roasted, puréed and pickled as part of an achaar in this inventive [Indian](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/indian-recipes)[](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/indian-recipes) chaat dish from Will Bowlby. With a cornucopia of contrasting textures and flavours all bouncing off one another, it's a stunning recipe that adds heaps of balanced spice to earthy, sweet celeriac.

First published in 2021

### Metric

### Imperial

#### Chaat

-   1 [small celeriac](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/celeriac-recipes)
-   1 tbsp of [Kashmiri chilli powder](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/chilli-recipes)
-   1 dash of rapeseed oil
-   1 tsp chaat masala
-   100ml of [lime juice](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/lime-recipes)
-   50g of dates, pitted and finely chopped
-   20g of [coriander stalks](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/coriander-recipes), finely chopped
-   30g of red onion, finely chopped
-   [salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes), to taste

#### Andrha spice mix

-   1 tsp [cumin seeds](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/cumin-recipes)
-   1 tsp [coriander seeds](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/coriander-seed-recipes)
-   1 tsp [fennel seeds](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/fennel-seed-recipes)
-   1/2 tsp [black peppercorns](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/peppercorn-recipes)
-   5 [curry leaves](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/curry-leaf-recipes), ideally fresh
-   1/2 tsp [turmeric](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/turmeric-recipes)
-   1/2 tsp [basmati rice](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/basmati-rice-recipes)
-   1/2 tsp fenugreek seeds
-   1 [mace](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mace-recipes)
-   1 whole dried kashmiri chilli

#### Achaar

-   1 [small celeriac](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/celeriac-recipes)
-   250ml of [mustard oil](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mustard-recipes)
-   2 tsp panch poran, a Bengali and northern Indian spice blend – if you can’t find it to buy simply mix equal quantities of fenugreek, cumin, nigella, fennel and black mustard seeds and toast until fragrant (do not blitz to a powder)
-   10g of [curry leaves](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/curry-leaf-recipes), ideally fresh
-   1 tsp [turmeric powder](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/turmeric-recipes)
-   1 tsp [Kashmiri chilli powder](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/chilli-recipes)
-   [salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes), to taste
-   caster sugar, to taste

#### Coriander chutney

-   125g of [coriander](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/coriander-recipes)
-   50g of vegetable oil
-   1 knob of [ginger](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/ginger-recipes), small, peeled
-   1 [garlic clove](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/garlic-recipes), peeled
-   1/2 [green chilli](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/chilli-recipes)
-   1 1/2 tbsp of [lemon juice](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/lemon-recipes)
-   1 pinch of caster sugar
-   1 pinch of [salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes)

#### To garnish

-   50g of papdi, (a type of cracker popular in India for chaat dishes)
-   20g of [tamarind chutney](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/chutney-recipes)
-   10g of [sev](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/noodle-recipes), (a type of fried vermicelli use in chaat dishes)

-   Food processor or blender

1

To make the Andhra spice mix, toast all the ingredients in a dry frying pan over a very low heat for 5-10 minutes, until fragrant and beginning to brown. Allow to cool, then blitz to a powder. You’ll have more than you need for this dish, but it will keep in an airtight container

-   1 tsp [cumin seeds](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/cumin-recipes)
-   1 tsp [coriander seeds](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/coriander-seed-recipes)
-   1 tsp [fennel seeds](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/fennel-seed-recipes)
-   1/2 tsp [black peppercorns](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/peppercorn-recipes)
-   5 [curry leaves](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/curry-leaf-recipes), ideally fresh
-   1/2 tsp [turmeric](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/turmeric-recipes)
-   1/2 tsp [basmati rice](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/basmati-rice-recipes)
-   1/2 tsp fenugreek seeds
-   1 [mace](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mace-recipes)
-   1 whole dried kashmiri chilli

2

Begin by making the achaar, as this can be done up to a month in advance. Peel and finely dice the celeriac, then weigh out 300g of it (any leftover can be used in the purée). Place in a saucepan with the rest of the ingredients and cook over a low-medium heat until the celeriac is just softened (around 15-20 minutes). Leave to cool completely, then transfer to a jar, seal and store in the fridge for up to a month

-   1 [small celeriac](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/celeriac-recipes)
-   250ml of [mustard oil](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mustard-recipes)
-   2 tsp panch poran, a Bengali and northern Indian spice blend – if you can’t find it to buy simply mix equal quantities of fenugreek, cumin, nigella, fennel and black mustard seeds and toast until fragrant (do not blitz to a powder)
-   10g of [curry leaves](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/curry-leaf-recipes), ideally fresh
-   1 tsp [turmeric powder](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/turmeric-recipes)
-   1 tsp [Kashmiri chilli powder](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/chilli-recipes)
-   [salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes), to taste
-   caster sugar, to taste

3

For the chaat mix, preheat an oven to 180°C/gas mark 4. Peel and dice the celeriac into 1-inch cubes, then toss with the chilli powder, oil and a pinch of salt. Place on a baking tray and cook for around 25 minutes, until cooked. Allow to cool

-   1 [small celeriac](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/celeriac-recipes)
-   1 tbsp of [Kashmiri chilli powder](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/chilli-recipes)
-   1 dash of rapeseed oil
-   [salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes), to taste

4

While the celeriac roasts in the oven, prepare the purée. Peel and dice the celeriac, then place in a saucepan with the milk and cream, seasoning with a pinch of salt and 2 tablespoons of the Andrha spice mix. Bring to the boil, then place a circle of baking paper on the surface of the liquid (known as a cartouche) and turn down to a simmer. Cook for around 20 minutes until the celeriac is soft, then strain the celeriac (reserving the liquid) and place into a blender or food processor. Blitz until smooth, adding back some of the cooking liquid if necessary

-   1 [small celeriac](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/celeriac-recipes)
-   300ml of whole milk
-   300ml of double cream
-   [salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes), to taste

5

Once the roast celeriac has cooled, mix it with the rest of the ingredients for the chaat, adjusting the amount of lime juice and chaat masala to taste

-   1 tsp chaat masala
-   100ml of [lime juice](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/lime-recipes)
-   50g of dates, pitted and finely chopped
-   20g of [coriander stalks](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/coriander-recipes), finely chopped
-   30g of red onion, finely chopped

6

To make the coriander chutney, simply blitz all the ingredients together in a food processor until they form a fine paste. Season to taste with salt and sugar. You'll probably be left with more than you use for this recipe, but it will keep for a few days in the fridge

-   125g of [coriander](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/coriander-recipes)
-   50g of vegetable oil
-   1 knob of [ginger](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/ginger-recipes), small, peeled
-   1 [garlic clove](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/garlic-recipes), peeled
-   1/2 [green chilli](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/chilli-recipes)
-   1 1/2 tbsp of [lemon juice](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/lemon-recipes)
-   1 pinch of caster sugar
-   1 pinch of [salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes)

7

To serve, ensure the celeriac purée is at room temperature (or slightly warmer), reheating if needed. Place a large spoonful of it on each serving dish, then make a well in the centre and add a teaspoon of achaar per person. Arrange the chaat next to the purée, then garnish with the chutneys, papdi and sev

-   50g of papdi, (a type of cracker popular in India for chaat dishes)
-   20g of [tamarind chutney](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/chutney-recipes)
-   10g of [sev](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/noodle-recipes), (a type of fried vermicelli use in chaat dishes)
