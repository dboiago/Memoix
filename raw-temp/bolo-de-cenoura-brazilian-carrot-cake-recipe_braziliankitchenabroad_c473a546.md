## Why I love this Brazilian Carrot Cake Recipe

_Oi, Gente!_

Ohhh the memories I have about coming home from school in the afternoon and seeing this beautiful cake cooling by the window. It was always the nicest of all treats for a perfect _lanche da tarde_ (afternoon snack). This is _the_ childhood favorite cake that every Brazilian knows and loves.

_Bolo de cenoura_ is a traditional Brazilian cake recipe, but it’s not like the one you might be envisioning. Unlike American carrot cakes, Brazilian carrot cake doesn’t have any spices, raisins or pineapple added.

In Brazil, we make a blender cake and top it with either a simple buttery chocolate sauce or delicious [classic brigadeiro](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/brigadeiro/) and chocolate sprinkles. There is no cream cheese frosting involved. Think of it as a chocolate carrot cake, but the chocolate is on top instead of inside!

This cake is _super easy_ to make, and it uses simple pantry ingredients. This isa great cake to enjoy all year round, not just at Easter. If you haven’t tried a carrot cake like this, I absolutely recommend you give it a try.

## Here’s what you’ll need to make this _bolo de cenoura_ recipe

Like I said above, this cake is made from ingredients that you probably have on hand already.

#### REMEMBER!

**Changing a recipe can lead to failure. Substitutions or tweaks might significantly impact your results, so for the best experience, try to follow the original recipe as closely as possible.**

**For the Carrot Cake:**

-   **Carrots** are the star here. You blend them raw right into the batter, which is what gives this cake its beautiful orange color and moist texture. There is no need to cook or grate them first. Just peel and chunk before blending!
-   **Eggs** help bind everything together and give the cake structure and lift.
-   **Sugar** keeps it sweet and tender. Plain white granulated sugar is what you’ll need. There is no substitute.
-   **Vegetable oil** keeps this cake incredibly moist. You could also use canola oil or another flavorless oil, like avocado oil. Avoid olive oil or coconut oil, as they’ll change the overall flavor.
-   **Vanilla extract** adds a subtle warmth to the batter. It’s not always traditional, but I love what it does here.
-   **All purpose flour** is all you need. I have not tried this with any other flours or a gluten-free flour blend and do not recommend it at this time.
-   **Baking powder** is what gives this cake its rise. Make sure yours is fresh or the cake won’t puff up the way it should.
-   **A pinch of kosher salt** balances the sweetness and brings all the flavors together.

**For the Chocolate Sauce:**

-   **Milk** gives the sauce its smooth, pourable consistency. I recommend whole milk for its fat content, which adds extra depth and richness.
-   **White sugar** sweetens the sauce and helps it crystallize as it cools, giving you that signature crackly top.
-   **Cocoa powder** is the base. Use a good quality Dutch-process unsweetened cocoa powder here.
-   **Unsalted butter** makes it rich and glossy. This is what gives the sauce that beautiful buttery finish.

## How to make Brazilian Carrot Cake

My favorite part about this recipe is that it uses a blender to crush the carrots, so we don’t have to grate them by hand.

This is such a great trick because I don’t know about you, but I absolutely hate grating just about anything.

So here’s how you make it:

**Preheat the oven** and spray your bundt pan liberally with nonstick cooking spray. _This is super important because you do not want your cake to stick!_

**Mix the dry ingredients.** In a bowl, whisk together the flour, baking powder, and salt, and set aside.

**Blend the carrots.** Peel and cut the carrots into chunks and transfer them into the blender. Pulse to shred until there are not large pieces remaining, as shown below.

[![A blender shreds carrots for brazilian carrot cake](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/bolo-de-cenoura-2-1024x779.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/bolo-de-cenoura-2.jpg)

[![Collage of images showing a blender mix carrots and sugar for brazilian carrot cake](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/bolo-de-cenoura-4-1024x823.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/bolo-de-cenoura-4.jpg)

  
Add the sugar and blend until combined, scraping the sides with a rubber spatula. Then pour in the oil and blend until creamy.

**Add the eggs.** Break the eggs into a bowl. Add a splash of vanilla, and whisk to combine.

**Pour the egg-vanilla mixture** into the [blender](https://www.amazon.com/Ninja-Professional-Countertop-Technology-BL610/dp/B00NGV4506/ref=as_li_ss_tl?keywords=blender&qid=1571369572&s=home-garden&sr=1-5&linkCode=ll1&tag=braziliankitc-20&linkId=82f625456736b9c859006d6471a13ff9&language=en_US), and pulse just 2-3 times to combine. The texture will be pretty smooth, and there will be no large lumps remaining.

_Don’t overmix here or your cake will lose its light, fluffy texture._

[![Brazilian Carrot Cake mixture in a blender](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/bolo-de-cenoura-7-1024x758.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/bolo-de-cenoura-7.jpg)

**Combine the batter.** Pour the carrot cream into the dry ingredients and fold with a spatula until just combined.

It is so bright and colorful!

[![A purple spatula mixes Brazilian Carrot Cake ingredients in a white bowl](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/bolo-de-cenoura-9-1024x755.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/bolo-de-cenoura-9.jpg)

[![Brazilian Carrot Cake batter in a bowl](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/bolo-de-cenoura-10-1024x743.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/bolo-de-cenoura-10.jpg)

  
**Bake.** Pour the batter into the prepared bundt pan and bake in the preheated oven.

It’s ready when you insert a toothpick or skewer into the center of the cake and it comes out crumbly to clean. If there’s still batter on it, bake it for a few more minutes.

**Let it cool.** Let the cake cool in the pan for about 10 minutes, then turn it out onto a wire rack or serving plate.

## **Make THE CHOCOLATE sauce**:

While the cake cools, make the chocolate sauce!

Measure all the sauce ingredients into a saucepan. Turn on the heat of the stovetop, and whisk consistently as you bring them to a simmer.

Pour the warm sauce over the warm cake and let them cool together.

You’ll notice the sauce cracks as the cake cools to room temperature. This is intentional. The sugar crystallizes again as it cools, giving you that signature crackly top.

![A woman drizzles chocolate sauce on top of a bolo de cenoura bundt cake](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Brazilian-Carrot-Cake-10.jpg)

## The other way to top your bolo de cenoura…

There are two classic ways to enjoy a Brazilian carrot cake. One is with the buttery chocolate sauce, which we already covered above. The other is with a traditional brigadeiro, used like a ganache.

To use brigadeiro as your topping, make a batch of [classic brigadeiro](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/brigadeiro/), and while it’s still warm and pourable, drizzle it over the cake. For the full experience, pour some into the center hole of the bundt, too. When you slice into it, you get a beautiful chocolate lava volcano effect. Sprinkles on top are optional, but are so fun, so why not?

My mom would make the buttery glaze more often because it’s easier. Also, we always had cocoa powder, sugar, butter, and milk at home and readily available. But when brigadeiro was involved? This was an absolute treat.

I could never EVER pick a favorite, so I always bounce between the two.

You should try both ways and let me know which is your favorite!

![Close up of a Brazilian Carrot Cake with chocolate eggs in its center](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Brazilian-Carrot-Cake-1.jpg)

## Variations and **OTHER WAYS TO BAKE THIS CAKE**

-   **Use a different pan.** If you don’t have a bundt pan, you can make this cake in a 9-inch square pan or a 8-inch round pan. The bake times may vary a little, so keep an eye on it as it bakes.
-   **Make them into cupcakes.** Line 18 cupcake tins with liners and fill them 2/3 of the way full. Bake for 10-12 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted in the middle comes out lightly crumbly to clean. Cool the cupcakes in the tin on a rack for 5 minutes, then remove and cool completely.

## How to Serve

This Brazilian carrot cake is a perfect treat for coffee breaks all year long. It’s the kind of cake that just lives on the counter and disappears slice by slice.

During **Easter**, stuff the center with little chocolate eggs for a fun surprise when you cut into it. It’s also a hit at **Halloween** because of that gorgeous orange color. Swap the sprinkles for purple, black, and orange ones and it’s the cutest thing on the table.

It’s also great for birthday parties, _lanche da tarde_, or anytime you want something sweet without too much fuss.

## How to Store:

Store leftover cake covered at room temperature for up to 2 days. After that, transfer it to the fridge, where it’ll keep for up to 5 days. The chocolate sauce might lose a little of its crackly texture in the fridge, but the flavor will still be there.

You can also freeze the cake without the sauce, wrapped tightly in plastic wrap, for up to 3 months. Just thaw at room temperature and add fresh sauce when you’re ready to serve.

## Brazilian Carrot Cake with Chocolate Sauce

**Brazilian Carrot Cake**, **bolo de cenoura** in Portuguese, is one of Brazil's most iconic cake recipes. It's soft, moist, and has the most beautiful orange color from the carrots blended right into the batter. We top it with a simple buttery chocolate sauce that crystallizes as it cools or classic brigadeiro. The best part? This Brazilian carrot cake requires no fancy technique or equipment. All you need are a [blender](https://www.amazon.com/Ninja-Professional-Countertop-Technology-BL610/dp/B00NGV4506/ref=as_li_ss_tl?keywords=blender&qid=1571369572&s=home-garden&sr=1-5&linkCode=ll1&tag=braziliankitc-20&linkId=82f625456736b9c859006d6471a13ff9&language=en_US) and a few simple ingredients you most likely already have at home.

Servings 8 slices

Calories 491 kcal

Prep Time 20 minutes

#### For the Cake:

-   2 cups all-purpose flour
-   2 teaspoon baking powder
-   1 pinch kosher salt
-   2 large carrots peeled and cut into chunks (you should have enough to fill a liquid 1-cup measurer all the way to the top)
-   1 ¾ cups granulated sugar
-   1/2 cup vegetable oil (can also use canola oil)
-   3 large eggs
-   2 teaspoon vanilla extract

#### For the Chocolate Sauce:

-   3 tablespoon whole milk
-   1/2 cup granulated sugar
-   2 tablespoons cocoa powder
-   1 tablespoon butter
-   1 pinch kosher salt

-   Vegetable peeler

#### For the Cake:

-   Grease and flour a bundt cake pan. A sheet pan is OK, too, if you don't have a bundt pan. You can also use an 8-inch square pan or a 9-inch round pan. (The baking time will change with these, though, so please keep an eye on them.)
