If you love bubble tea, you are going to love this refreshing Thai treat. "Lod chong Singapore" is made from chewy tapioca noodles in a pandan scented, iced coconut milk drink. It's a popular dessert in Thailand often sold by street carts, and it is surprisingly easy to make at home. Like most Thai desserts, it's naturally vegan, gluten free, and it can be sweetened with your sweetener of choice!

![A glass of lodchong singapore in coconut milk with a straw in the glass and pandan leaves on the side.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Lodchong-Singapore-blog.jpg)

## Why is it called Lod Chong Singapore?

_Lod chong_ _singapore_ means "Singaporean _lod chong_," with _lod chong_ being the Thai term for _cendol_, the green dessert noodles popular all over Southeast Asia. For a long time I thought that this was the Singaporean version of cendol because of the name; but turns out, the reason for "Singapore" is because the shop that popularized this dessert was situated in front of a movie theatre called "Singapore Theatre!" So, no relation to Singapore the country here! _(It is believed that they not only popularized it, but actually invented the dessert, though I am not 100% sure on this.)_

## Ingredients and Important Notes

Here are the ingredients you'll need and important notes about them. For amounts, see the full [recipe card](#recipe) below!

#### For the Bubble Tea Noodles

![A bag of pine brand mung bean starch and a bag of pine brand tapioca starch](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/12/mung-bean-tapioca-starch.jpg)

-   **Tapioca starch.** This is the main starch used to give the noodles that clear bouncy texture. This is NOT to be confused with cassava flour or tapioca flour, which are not ground as finely and cannot be used. To be sure, use tapioca starch from Thailand.
-   **Mung bean starch (optional).** While not necessary, mung bean starch helps soften the elasticity of the noodles slightly, making them a little easier to chew. If you don't have it, using all tapioca starch is totally fine. This is NOT the same as mung bean flour.
-   **Green food colouring.** Green is the classic colour for the noodles to match the pandan leaves, but you can use any other colours you want.

#### For the Coconut Milk Drink

-   **Coconut water**, **no sugar added.** We will use coconut water in the syrup, and also to thin out our coconut milk. You can technically just use water, but for added flavour, I like to use coconut water instead. Make sure you buy pure coconut water with no sugar or flavour added.
-   **Coconut milk**. Good quality coconut milk is SUPER important in Thai desserts as it is the main flavour. -   **Sugar.** Regular white sugar is fine, and you can also substitute any non-caloric sweetener as well.
-   **Salt**
-   **Ripe jackfruit, canned or fresh.** Make sure you get ripe jackfruit which is yellow and sweet! Fresh jackfruit is hard to find, but you can find canned jackfruit at any Asian stores.

## Step-By-Step: How to Make Lod Chong Singapore

![a collage of steps for making lodchong Singapore, steps 1-4](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/1-720x540.jpg)

1.  Make the syrup by simmering a pandan leaf with coconut water for 5 mins.
2.  Add the sugar and stir until dissolved; cool.
3.  Make the coconut milk broth by mixing coconut milk and coconut water.
4.  Add the pandan leaf and salt and simmer for 5 mins.

_Note: I am making the coconut milk and the syrup separately so that each person can adjust the sweetness of the final drink to taste, which is what most street vendors do._

![a collage of steps for making lodchong Singapore, steps 5-86](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/2-720x540.jpg)

5.  Combine the 2 starches. Add food colouring to hot water and and drizzle it over the starches little by little.
6.  In between drizzles, toss with a spoon to mix.
7.  Keep adding hot water and tossing until almost all the water is used up and the dough starts to clump up.
8.  Stop adding water once dough seems evenly moistened and most of the starch has clumped up, with little dry flour left.

![a collage of steps for making lodchong Singapore, steps 9-12](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/3-720x540.jpg)

9.  Squeeze the dough together into a ball
10.  Transfer to a tapioca-starch-dusted surface and knead until smooth. If it feels too dry, sprinkle a little water over it; if it feels too wet, add a little tapioca starch.
11.  Roll into a 2mm thick sheet and cut into 2-3-inch wide strips.
12.  Cut each strip into 3-4mm wide noodles. Toss the noodles immediately with some tapioca starch to separate them and prevent them from sticking together.

![a collage of steps for making lodchong Singapore, steps 13-16](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/4-720x540.jpg)

13.  Drop the noodles into a large pot of boiling water and after all the noodles have floated to the surface, cook for about 1 minute.
14.  Scoop the water into a big bowl of cold water. The noodles should be translucent if they are done.
15.  In a serving glass add some noodles, jackfruit pieces, some syrup to taste, and the coconut milk. Stir to mix, and if you have time, let it sit for about 15 minutes and the noodles will absorb some sugar and it'll taste better.
16.  Add lots of ice, wait for the ice to melt a little and completely chill the drink before enjoying with a spoon or a large bubble tea straw!

## IMPORTANT: Advance Prep & Storage

The noodles don't keep well, but there are lots of ways to prep ahead! I've detailed how to store each component below, but here is my general "best practice" if you're looking to serve these at a party.

-   **_Make the syrup and coconut milk in advance._**
-   **_Make the dough the day-of, and don't refrigerate._**
-   **_Roll out and cook the noodles within a few hours of serving._**

#### Here are more details for storing each of the component:

**Syrup & Coconut Milk.** Make the syrup and the coconut milk well in advance to allow enough time for them to cool before using. The syrup will last indefinitely in the fridge, and the coconut milk at least a week.

**The Dough.** Unfortunately the dough does not keep well. In the fridge the starch will stiffen and become brittle, making it hard to work with. You can, however, make the dough anytime on the day you're going to serve, keep it tightly wrapped at room temperature until you're ready to roll (pun totally intended!).

**The Uncooked, Cut Noodles.** Once the noodles are cut, you should cook them as soon as possible. Left out they will dry and become brittle and break; and they might also stick together, especially if you have them sitting on top of each other in a big pile. But if you need to leave them for a bit, keep them covered with plastic wrap.

**Cooked Noodles.** Assemble the dessert shortly after cooking the noodles or they will clump together if left to sit for too long. You can assemble the noodles with the syrup, jackfruit and coconut milk up to a few hours in advance, and the liquid will prevent the noodles from clumping. Add ice only when serving.

_\*Do not refrigerate the cooked noodles or the texture will harden and lose that soft chew._

## Tips for Optimal Deliciousness

Once you've cooked the noodles, even if you're ready to eat, hold off for a bit. Assemble and **let the noodles sit in the syrup and coconut milk for about 15 minutes.** The noodles will have had time to absorb some sweetness from the syrup and taste better. **Do not refrigerate** to maintain the best noodle texture.

Serve within a few hours of assembly for best texture, and remember to only add the ice when ready to serve!

-   ▢ 5 pieces sweet jackfruit, fresh or canned, julienned against the grain (see note)
-   ▢ Lots of ice

#### Make the syrup:

-   Add coconut water or water into a small pot. Tie the pandan leaf into a knot or two until it’s short enough to fit nicely into the pot and add it to the coconut water. Cover and simmer on low heat for 5 minutes to infuse.
    
-   Add the sugar and stir until dissolved. Cool completely.
    

#### Make the coconut milk:

-   Combine coconut milk, coconut water, and salt in a small pot. Tie the pandan leaf into a knot or two until it’s short enough to fit nicely into the pot and add it to the coconut milk. Cover and simmer on low heat for 5 minutes to infuse. Cool completely.
    

#### Make the noodles (please watch the video for technique!):

-   Whisk the starches together in a large, heat-proof mixing bowl.
    
-   Bring the water to a full boil, and once boiling, turn off the heat immediately (I do this in the microwave in a glass measuring cup). Add the food colouring and stir to mix.
    
-   Working with the water right away while it's still hot, drizzle roughly 3 tablespoons of the hot water over the flour, then quickly toss with a spoon to distribute the hot water. The parts where the flour touches the water will clump up immediately. Repeat with more hot water, again 3 tablespoons at a time, until all of the flour starts to look clumpy. You should use MOST of the hot water, but you may not need all of it.
    
-   Use your hand to squeeze the flour into a ball. It should be very rough but it should come together easily. If there is still dry flour, add a little more water. If it is too wet and sticks to your hand too much, you can add a little more tapioca starch, 1 tablespoon at a time.
    
-   Once a rough dough forms, transfer it onto a clean work surface dusted with tapioca starch. Dust the top of the dough lightly with more starch and then knead until a smooth dough forms, adding more starch if the dough sticks to your hands. If there are little lumps of flour in the dough, do not worry, this will all cook away.
    
-   Separate dough into 2 pieces, and cover one with a tea towel while you work with the other. Re-dust the work surface and roll the dough out into a rectangle about 2mm thick (1/16 inch). Don’t worry too much about the dimensions.
    
-   Once you have the right thickness, cut down the sheet so you have long strips that are 2-3 inches wide. Then generously dust the strips and cut them into noodles, about 3-4 mm wide.
    
-   Toss the cut noodles with a little extra tapioca starch to ensure that they do not stick together. Repeat with the remaining dough.
    
-   Bring a large pot of water to a full rolling boil, and meanwhile prepare a large bowl of very cold water. Once the water is boiling, “sprinkle” all of the noodles into the pot. Once the noodles float, keep them boiling for another 1-2 minutes or until cooked through.
    
-   To check doneness, put a piece of noodle into the cold water; it should be translucent all the way through and it should be nice and chewy with no starchiness left in the centre. If you find it too chewy, you can cook it a bit longer.
    
-   Once done, scoop the noodles out with a wire skimmer and put them immediately into the cold water. Once cool, drain them immediately and place them into a bowl. Do not let them linger in the water unnecessarily or they will swell up.
    

#### If serving right away:

-   Divide the noodles into a serving bowl or cup, add jackfruit, then add syrup to your liking (start with 2 tablespoon per serving), and about 4 tablespoon of the coconut milk. If possible, let it sit for 15 mins or so and the noodles will absorb some of the sweetness and will taste better overall.
    
    Add a lot of ice and stir well, allowing it to really chill before you eat. Eat with a big bubble tea straw or a spoon. Put out extra syrup for people to add more if desired.
    

#### If not serving right away:

-   Once the noodles are cooked, you need to keep them in liquid or they will eventually stick together into a big clump. So place the noodles into a large bowl, add the jackfruit and pour all of the coconut milk over them. Add the syrup to taste, but make it a little sweeter than you like because the ice will dilute it some. Stir to mix and keep at room temp until ready to serve, ideally no longer than 2 hours. The longer they sit the more they will swell up with liquid and the texture will be less chewy.
    
-   When ready to serve, portion the noodles out into bowls or cups and add a lot of ice and stir well, allowing it to really chill before you eat. Eat with a big bubble tea straw or a spoon. Put out extra syrup for people to add more if desired.
