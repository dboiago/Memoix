![Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/wonton-noodle-soup-14.jpg)

Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup is such a basic, no-brainer bowl of noodles that you’ll find at most Cantonese restaurants. But if you’re not near a Chinatown or Cantonese place, you’ll definitely want to give it a try at home. It’s so easy to put together!

If you’re not in the mood for a classic wonton noodle soup with shrimp and pork, check out our [simple wonton soup recipe](https://thewoksoflife.com/2013/12/simple-wonton-soup-2/ "Simple Wonton Soup") that is fantastic and just as the recipe says, very simple and sans noodle and shrimp. The Shanghai clan (Judy) also has their own version of [Shanghai small wonton](https://thewoksoflife.com/2015/08/shanghai-wonton-soup/) which is pretty authentic and delicious.

You do need some special ingredients, but the method is very simple. For the noodles, you’ll need Hong Kong “wonton noodles.”

Make sure not to confuse these with the Hong Kong style pan-fried noodles, which is what’s used to make our [Simple Spicy Pan-Fried Noodles](https://thewoksoflife.com/2013/06/simple-spicy-pan-fried-noodles/ "SIMPLE, SPICY PAN-FRIED NOODLES"), [Pan-fried Noodles with Chicken](https://thewoksoflife.com/2014/05/pan-fried-noodles-w-chicken-gai-see-chow-mein/ "PAN-FRIED NOODLES W/ CHICKEN (Gai See Chow Mein)"), and [Cantonese Soy Sauce Pan-fried Noodles](https://thewoksoflife.com/2013/12/cantonese-soy-sauce-pan-fried-noodles/ "CANTONESE SOY SAUCE PAN-FRIED NOODLES"). They look a little similar, so make sure to look at the package closely.

[![Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/wonton-noodle-soup-09.jpg)](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-noodles-wrappers/)

[![Wonton Noodle Soup, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/wonton-noodle-soup-10.jpg)](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-noodles-wrappers/#HK-style-fresh-wonton-noodles)

You’ll also need [wonton skin](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-noodles-wrappers/#wonton-wrappers). Look for the square-shaped egg version, which is what’s used for Cantonese-style wontons. You’ll likely see wonton skin that’s white, and wonton skin that’s yellow.

You’re looking for the yellow version. The white version doesn’t have egg in it, and is used in our [Simple Wonton Soup](https://thewoksoflife.com/2013/12/simple-wonton-soup-2/ "SIMPLE WONTON SOUP").

[![Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/wonton-noodle-soup-01.jpg)](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-noodles-wrappers/#wonton-wrappers)

## Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup: Recipe Instructions

Start by making the wonton filling. Add the pork, 3/4 teaspoon salt, sugar, [sesame oil](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-sauces-vinegars-oils/#sesame-oil), [white pepper](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-spices-condiments/#white-pepper), [shaoxing wine](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-sauces-vinegars-oils/#shaoxing), oil, water, and [cornstarch](https://thewoksoflife.com/rice-grains-and-flours/#cornstarch) to a bowl and whip everything together by hand for 15 minutes, or in a [food processor](http://amzn.to/2jE03By) for 5 minutes.

You want the pork to look like a paste. Mix in the chopped shrimp.

![Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/wonton-noodle-soup-02.jpg)

To make the wontons, take a [wonton wrapper](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-noodles-wrappers/#wonton-wrappers), and add only about a teaspoon of filling.

![Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/wonton-noodle-soup-03.jpg)

Use your finger to coat the edges with water (this helps the two sides seal together), and fold in half so you have a triangle shape. Bring together the two outer corners, and press to seal.

![Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/wonton-noodle-soup-06.jpg)

![Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/wonton-noodle-soup-07.jpg)

For more detailed step-by-step wonton folding photos, instructions and different ways to wrap wontons, see Sarah’s post on [How to fold wontons](https://thewoksoflife.com/how-to-fold-wontons/).

![Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/wonton-noodle-soup-08.jpg)

Continue assembling until all the filling is gone. This Cantonese wonton noodle soup recipe should make about 3 dozen wontons.

![Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/wonton-noodle-soup-11.jpg)

![Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/wonton-noodle-soup-12.jpg)

To assemble the wonton noodle soup, heat up the [chicken broth](https://thewoksoflife.com/2016/01/homemade-chicken-stock/) in a medium pot. Adjust seasoning by adding salt, to taste.

Bring a large pot of water to a boil and boil the [wonton egg noodles](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-noodles-wrappers/#HK-style-fresh-wonton-noodles) just until al dente. Divide among 6 bowls. Add the wontons and boil for about 4-5 minutes until they float and the filling is cooked through. Divide wontons among the bowls, and cover with hot broth. Serve!

![Cantonese Wonton Noodle Soup, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/wonton-noodle-soup-13.jpg)

If you want your soup a little more flavorful, you can also add a couple drops of sesame oil, chopped scallion, and white pepper over the top!

## Recipe

Prep: 1 hour

Cook: 15 minutes

Total: 1 hour 15 minutes

-   ▢ 8 oz. [ground pork](https://thewoksoflife.com/2018/01/how-to-grind-meat-without-a-grinder/) (225g)
-   ▢ 3/4 teaspoon [salt](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-spices-condiments/#salt) (plus more to taste)
-   ▢ 3/4 teaspoon sugar
-   ▢ 1/2 teaspoon [sesame oil](https://thewoksoflife.com/sesame-oil/)
-   ▢ 1/8 teaspoon [white pepper](https://thewoksoflife.com/what-is-white-pepper/)
-   ▢ 1 tablespoon [shaoxing wine](https://thewoksoflife.com/shaoxing-wine-the-key-to-authentic-chinese-cooking/)
-   ▢ 2 teaspoons oil
-   ▢ 1 tablespoon water
-   ▢ 1/2 teaspoon [cornstarch](https://thewoksoflife.com/rice-grains-and-flours/#cornstarch)
-   ▢ 6 oz. shrimp (170g, chopped)
-   ▢ 1/2 pack [wonton wrappers](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-noodles-wrappers/#wonton-wrappers) (the square-shaped egg wonton skins)
-   ▢ 12 oz. [wonton egg noodles](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-noodles-wrappers/#HK-style-fresh-wonton-noodles) (340g, see photo above)
-   ▢ 8 cups high quality, low sodium chicken broth (1.9 liters)

-   Start by making the filling. Add the pork, 3/4 teaspoon salt, sugar, sesame oil, white pepper, wine, oil, water, and cornstarch to a bowl and whip everything together by hand for 15 minutes, or in a food processor for 5 minutes. You want the pork to look like a paste. Mix in the chopped shrimp.
    
-   To make the wontons, take a wrapper, and add only about a teaspoon of filling. Use your finger to coat the edges with water (this helps the two sides seal together), and fold in half so you have a triangle shape. Bring together the two outer corners, and press to seal. Continue assembling until you’ve used up the filling (this recipe should make about 3 dozen wontons).
    
-   To assemble the dish, heat up the chicken broth in a medium pot. Adjust seasoning by adding salt, to taste.
    
-   Bring a large pot of water to a boil and boil the noodles just until al dente. Divide among 6 bowls. Add the wontons and boil for about 4-5 minutes until they float and the filling is cooked through. Divide wontons among the bowls, and cover with hot broth. Serve!
    
-   If you want your soup a little more flavorful, you can also add a couple drops of sesame oil, chopped scallion, and white pepper over the top!
