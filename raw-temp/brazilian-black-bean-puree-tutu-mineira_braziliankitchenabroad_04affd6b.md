## What is Tutu à Mineira?

**Tutu à Mineira** is the most well-known version of **Tutu de Feijão**, a traditional dish from **Minas Gerais, Brazil** — hence the name, which means “tutu prepared Minas-style.”

The word **“Tutu”** is believed to have origins in the **Kimbundu language**, spoken by the **Mbundu people of Angola**. In Kimbundu, **“Tutu”** means a **mash or thick purée**, which perfectly describes the consistency of this dish.

This type of dish made its way to Brazil during colonial times, when enslaved Africans, especially from **Angola and the Congo region**, were forcibly brought to the country. Using what they had, they adapted their cooking to local ingredients.

**Beans became the base of the dish, thickened with farinha de mandioca** (cassava flour), a staple long used by Indigenous Brazilians. The result was a **thick, hearty dish**, a tutu, that blended African traditions with local flavors leaving a lasting mark on Brazilian cuisine.

Today, **Tutu à Mineira** is a beloved comfort food, typically made with **bacon, onions, and garlic** for a rich, smoky depth of flavor. It’s often served with **rice, collard greens, and meats**, carrying with it a deep history of **Angolan influence and cultural resilience** in Brazilian cooking.

Known for its **rustic and hearty cuisine,** Minas Gerais has made Tutu à Mineira a regional staple loved all over Brazil.

## How to make brazilian black bean puree

**Purée the beans:** Add your leftover beans to a [food processor](https://amzn.to/3pqUglP) and pulse until smooth. Set aside.

**Sauté the aromatics:** Heat a pan over medium heat with a drizzle of olive oil. Add the diced bacon and cook until crispy, then remove it with a slotted spoon and set it aside on a paper towel-lined plate. In the same pan, sauté the onions in the bacon fat until translucent, then add the minced garlic and cook until fragrant.

**Combine and thicken:** Stir in the puréed beans and let them simmer. Slowly sprinkle in Brazilian cassava flour, stirring continuously over low heat until the mixture thickens into a soft purée-like consistency.

**Serve and enjoy:** Drizzle with olive oil, top with crispy bacon, and, if you like, add a splash of vinegary hot sauce for extra flavor! _Check out other serving suggestions below._

## Brazilian-style Homemade Hot Sauce recipe

Here’s how I make my quick and flavorful **homemade hot sauce**.

Just blend together…

-   1/4 cup of leftover bean liquid
-   2 tablespoons of diced onion
-   Half a hot pepper, like serrano pepper (diced, seedless or with seeds if you like it hot)
-   1 tablespoon of olive oil
-   1 tablespoon of red vinegar
-   1 tablespoon of minced fresh parsley

Pulse everything in a **mixer or food processor**, and you’re done!

Drizzle it over your tutu for an extra layer of bold flavor.
