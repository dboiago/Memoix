Some years ago, while researching Japanese barbecue, I found myself at a produce market in Tokyo. There, amid the Yubari king melons and giant Amaou strawberries, was a $70 peach.

Flabbergasted, I asked the vendor how a single peach could cost $70. He explained the philosophical differences between Japanese and American agriculture.

“An American farmer looks at a peach orchard and thinks about how many tons of peaches he will harvest,” the vendor said. “A Japanese farmer looks at his orchard and thinks that one tree will produce peaches far superior to all the rest. And on that tree, one branch will produce a peach so extraordinary, connoisseurs will line up to pay $70 for the privilege of buying it.” (Sadly, I was not among them.)

## Why Grill Peaches?

I’m not going to tell you about a $70 peach this week, but I will tell you about a peach dish that tastes like a million bucks. It involves a spice, an herb, and a piece of cooking equipment near and dear to our hearts. You guessed it: the grill.

The spice in question is cinnamon—more precisely, the rolled bark of the tree that gives us ground cinnamon: the cinnamon stick.

The herb is celebrated across Planet Barbecue for its vibrant refreshing flavor: fresh mint.

To put them together, you make mini kebabs with peach quarters and mint, using cinnamon sticks as skewers.

## The Bourbon-Brown Sugar “B3” Glaze

Like all good barbecue, these grilled peach kebabs come with a sauce—in this case, my “B3” sauce. The three Bs are bourbon, brown sugar, and butter, with a whisper of heavy cream to hold them together.

Grill over a hot fire to caramelize the outside of the peaches, leaving the inside juicy and raw.

The crowning touch? Serve the grilled peaches over ice cream.

## How to Make Cinnamon-Grilled Peaches

### Ingredients

-   4 large ripe freestone peaches
-   1 bunch large fresh mint leaves
-   8 cinnamon sticks

#### For the B3 Glaze

-   1/4 cup bourbon
-   1/4 cup brown sugar
-   1/4 cup (4 tablespoons) unsalted butter
-   2 tablespoons heavy cream
-   1 pint vanilla ice cream, for serving

**You’ll also need:** Bamboo skewer and martini glasses

### Instructions

1.  Cut each peach in half along the crease, running your knife in a  
    circular motion around the peach and cutting to the pit. Twist the  
    halves in opposite directions to separate them. Using a spoon, pry  
    out and discard the pit. Cut each peach half in half.
2.  Using a bamboo skewer, poke a starter hole through the middle of  
    each peach quarter. Skewer 2 quarters on each cinnamon stick,  
    placing a fresh mint leaf between them.
3.  Make the B3 glaze: Combine the bourbon, brown sugar, butter, and  
    heavy cream in a saucepan and bring to a boil over high heat. Let  
    the glaze boil until thick and syrupy, about 3 minutes.
4.  Set up the grill for direct grilling and heat to high. Brush and  
    oil the grill grate.
5.  Arrange the peaches on the hot grate and grill until nicely  
    browned, 3 to 4 minutes per side, basting frequently with the  
    bourbon glaze.
6.  Spoon the ice cream into martini glasses. Place 2 peach skewers in  
    each glass and spoon the remaining bourbon glaze on top. Garnish  
    each with a sprig of mint and serve at once.

## Frequently Asked Questions

What kind of peaches are best for grilling?

Use large, ripe freestone peaches. Freestone peaches are easier to separate from the pit, making them simpler to quarter and thread onto the cinnamon sticks.

Do you need to peel peaches before grilling them?

No. Leave the skins on the peaches. The skins help the peach quarters hold their shape on the grill and make them easier to handle.

How long do peaches take to grill?

Grill the peach skewers over high direct heat for 3 to 4 minutes per side. The peaches should be lightly browned and caramelized on the outside while remaining juicy inside.
