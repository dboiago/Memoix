## A Home-style Chinese Recipe

In China, the term for home-style or family-style cooking is _jiachang cai_ (家常菜). Salted pork is something you’ll find less often in restaurants.

It’s much more likely to turn up on family dinner tables in simple home-style dishes like this Braised Daikon dish, where salted pork is the star. In fact, any time salted pork is present in a dish, it takes center stage.

Other ways to use salted pork include simply steaming the salted pork on top of rice in your [rice cooker](https://thewoksoflife.com/how-to/chinese-cooking-tools/#label-rice-cooker), or chopping it into small pieces and adding it to stir-fries. I have also seen salted pork used in many different kinds of Chinese soups—even fish soups.

The beauty of Chinese salted pork is that it’s really a flavor agent. It adds tons of complexity and umami deliciousness to dishes, so I do hope this post gives you some inspiration and the freedom to improvise with your salted pork!

## Recipe Instructions

![Braised Daikon with Salted Pork & Glass Noodles, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/braised-daikon.jpg)

Heat the oil in a [wok](https://thewoksoflife.com/how-to/chinese-cooking-tools/) over medium heat. Cook the [garlic](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-chives-scallions-aromatics-peppers/#garlic), the white parts of the [scallions](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-chives-scallions-aromatics-peppers/), and the [salted pork belly](https://thewoksoflife.com/2017/01/homemade-chinese-salted-pork/) for a couple of minutes until the pork fat turns transparent.

![Braised Daikon with Salted Pork & Glass Noodles, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/braised-daikon-9.jpg)

![Braised Daikon with Salted Pork & Glass Noodles, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/braised-daikon-10.jpg)

Now add the [daikon radish](https://thewoksoflife.com/asian-vegetables-beans-melons/#chinese-radish)…

![Braised Daikon with Salted Pork & Glass Noodles, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/braised-daikon-11.jpg)

And stir to combine.

![Braised Daikon with Salted Pork & Glass Noodles, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/braised-daikon-12.jpg)

Add the [chicken stock](https://thewoksoflife.com/2016/01/homemade-chicken-stock/), cover, and simmer for 15 minutes over medium heat until the daikon is cooked through.

![Braised Daikon with Salted Pork & Glass Noodles, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/braised-daikon-13.jpg)

Meanwhile, rinse the [mung bean glass noodles](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-noodles-wrappers/#mung-bean-vermicelli) and soak in cold water for five minutes. After five minutes, drain and set aside.

![Braised Daikon with Salted Pork & Glass Noodles, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/braised-daikon-7.jpg)

After the 15 minutes of simmering have elapsed, add the [sesame oil](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-sauces-vinegars-oils/#sesame-oil), [white pepper](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-spices-condiments/#white-pepper), and salt to taste.

Mix everything well. Add the glass noodles on top of the daikon, cover, and simmer for another three minutes. Uncover and add in the green parts of the scallions.

![Braised Daikon with Salted Pork & Glass Noodles, by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/braised-daikon-14.jpg)

Give everything a quick stir to combine and serve.

## Recipe

Prep: 15 minutes

Cook: 25 minutes

Total: 40 minutes

-   ▢ 3 tablespoons oil
-   ▢ 3 cloves [garlic](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-chives-scallions-aromatics-peppers/#garlic)
-   ▢ 3 [scallions](https://thewoksoflife.com/thewoksoflife.com/chinese-chives-scallions-aromatics-peppers/#scallions) (the white and green parts separated)
-   ▢ 5 ounces salted pork belly (140g, sliced)
-   ▢ 1½ pounds [daikon radish](https://thewoksoflife.com/asian-vegetables-beans-melons/#chinese-radish) (cut into small pieces)
-   ▢ 3½ cups [chicken stock](https://thewoksoflife.com/homemade-chicken-stock/)
-   ▢ 75 grams [mung bean vermicelli](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-noodles-wrappers/#mung-bean-vermicelli) (2 small bundles)
-   ▢ ½ teaspoon [sesame oil](https://thewoksoflife.com/sesame-oil/)
-   ▢ ¼ teaspoon [white pepper](https://thewoksoflife.com/what-is-white-pepper/)
-   ▢ [Salt](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-spices-condiments/#salt) (to taste)

-   Heat the oil in a wok over medium heat. Cook the garlic, the white parts of the scallion, and the salted pork belly for a couple of minutes until the pork fat turns transparent. Now add the daikon, and stir to combine. Add the stock, cover, and simmer for 15 minutes over medium heat until the daikon is cooked through.
    
-   Meanwhile, rinse the glass noodles and soak in cold water for five minutes. After five minutes, drain and set aside. After the 15 minutes of simmering have elapsed, add the sesame oil, white pepper, and salt to taste. Mix everything well. Add the glass noodles on top of the daikon, cover, and simmer for another three minutes. Uncover and add in the green parts of the scallions. Give everything a quick stir to combine and serve.
