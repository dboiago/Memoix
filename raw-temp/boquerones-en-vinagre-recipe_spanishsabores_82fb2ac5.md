## Introduction

**Anchovies are popular throughout Spain,** but especially in Málaga, a seaside city in Andalucía, a region in southern Spain. Here, the locals are even called _boquerones_! **One of their most plentiful fish has always been the _boquerón_,** or anchovy.

In Spain, it’s easy to find pre-made _boquerones en vinagre_; you can order them at almost any bar, or get them from the fish section of the market or grocery store--but now **you can make them wherever you are with this easy recipe!**

## How to Make Boquerones en Vinagre

If you’d like to see the full ingredients and instructions, _scroll to the bottom of the post_ for the printable recipe card.

![patting dry the anchovies and freezing in a dish.](https://spanishsabores.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Boquerones-en-Vinagre-Process-Collage-1.jpg)

1.  Start by **patting the cleaned anchovies dry** with paper towel. (image 1)
2.  Place them in a shallow dish and cover them with cold water. **Freeze for three hours to** kill any anisakis. (image 2)

![soaking the anchovies and topping with parsley and garlic.](https://spanishsabores.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Boquerones-en-Vinagre-Process-Collage-2.jpg)

3.  After freezing the fish, _add the vinegar and water_ to the dish and cover with plastic. **Refrigerate for 2-3 hours** to marinate. (image 3)
4.  Drain the marinated anchovies and **cover with the chopped parsley and garlic.** (image 4)

![soaking in olive oil and serving the anchovies with bread.](https://spanishsabores.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Boquerones-en-Vinagre-Process-Collage-3.jpg)

5.  **Pour the olive oil over the anchovies** and _let it marinate overnight_ if possible (or enjoy right away if you don't have time). (image 5)
6.  **Serve the boquerones en vinagre** with a bit of bread or by themselves and enjoy! (image 6)

## Recipe FAQs

**What type of fish are boquerones?**

Boquerones are anchovies, and are a very popular fish throughout Spain, but especially in the coastal region of Andalucía.

**What is the difference between the anchoa and the boquerón?**

Both _anchoas_ and _boquerones_ are anchovies, but the name differs based on its preparation. _Anchoas_ are salt-cured anchovies that are usually sold in cans, while _boquerones_ are sold fresh, frozen, or pickled in vinegar.

**What is "boquerones en vinagre" in English?**

The literal translation of the Spanish name _boquerones en vinagre_ is "anchovies in vinegar" in English.

## Expert Tips

-   **Ask your fishmonger** to clean and fillet the anchovies for you to save time.
-   If you use **frozen and thawed anchovies,** you can skip the 3 hour freezing process.
-   Drain the anchovies from the vinegar mixture **once they turn white;** marinating them too long will make them tough.

![plate of anchovies with sliced bread.](https://spanishsabores.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Boquerones-en-Vinagre-Featured-300x300.jpg)

-   ▢ 2 pounds fresh anchovies
-   ▢ 2 cups white wine vinegar
-   ▢ ¾ cup water
-   ▢ 5 cloves garlic minced
-   ▢ 5 sprigs fresh parsley chopped
-   ▢ salt to taste
-   ▢ 1 cup extra virgin olive oil (240 ml)

-   Clean the anchovies by removing the heads, innards, and spines, then cut them into fillets.
    
-   Rinse the anchovy fillets in cold water and pat them dry with paper towels. Lay them flat in a glass baking dish and freeze for 3 hours to kill any anisakis in the fish.
    
-   Remove the anchovies from the freezer and add the vinegar and water until the anchovies are submerged. Cover tightly with plastic wrap and marinate in the refrigerator for 2-3 hours, or until they're white. Don't leave them too long, as they'll become tough.
    
-   Drain the vinegar and water, then sprinkle the anchovies with the minced garlic and chopped parsley. Pour in the olive oil; the anchovies should be covered.
    
-   If possible, let the anchovies marinate in the garlic and olive oil overnight, but you can enjoy right away if you wish!
    
-   Serve the boquerones en vinagre on their own, or with a bit of delicious bread.
