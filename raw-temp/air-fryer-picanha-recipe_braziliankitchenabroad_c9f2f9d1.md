#### REMEMBER!

**Changing a recipe can lead to failure. Substitutions or tweaks might significantly impact your results, so for the best experience, try to follow the original recipe as closely as possible.**

-   **Picanha:** Our star ingredient, obviously. If you’re shopping outside Brazil, this cut is known as **top sirloin cap** or **coulotte** in the U.S., and **rump cap** across the UK, Ireland, and Australia. (Just don’t ask for plain “top sirloin” especially in the U.S. because that’s a different, larger muscle.) Look for a whole picanha with a thick, even fat cap still attached since that fat is where so much of the flavor lives. **Here’s my biggest advice:** don’t go too big. A smaller picanha is best for two reasons. **First**, it needs to actually fit in your air fryer basket. **Second**, oversized picanhas often include parts of the neighboring muscle that aren’t true picanha, and the quality drops. A good, super tender one usually runs on the smaller side. Mine is typically around 1.4 kg (about 3 lbs.), which fits my basket perfectly and is honestly pretty ideal. If your butcher offers to trim the fat cap down, ask them to leave it on!
-   **Neutral oil:** You need just a light coat to help the crust form and the salt stick. Something with a high smoke point like avocado, canola, or sunflower is usually best. Don’t use a stronger-flavored oil, like olive oil, because it will compete with the picanha fat flavor.
-   **Sal grosso:** [this](https://amzn.to/3Qto0yT) is the Brazilian coarse salt we use for churrasco, and it’s the traditional choice for picanha. The big crystals season the surface without dissolving into the meat too fast, which is exactly what you want. If you can’t find it, any coarse salt or rock sea salt will do the job. I prefer to avoid kosher salt because it’s too thin and will melt too fast, not yielding the same results.
-   **Unsalted** **butter:** the base of our herb butter, which melts over the sliced picanha right before serving. It makes everything richer and glossier. Use unsalted so you control the seasoning.
-   **Garlic:** it needs to be fresh and finely minced, for the herb butter. It’s non-negotiable for me. Garlic and picanha belong together.
-   **Fresh chives and fresh cilantro:** these go into the herb butter, too, and bring brightness against all that rich beef flavor. If cilantro isn’t your thing, swap in fresh parsley.

## How to cook Picanha in the Air Fryer

**Take the chill off.** Pull the picanha out of the fridge at least an hour before you plan to cook. Cooking cold means the outside races ahead while the center lags behind, and you’ll fight for an even cook. Letting it come closer to room temperature sets you up for that juicy texture, even cook.

**Preheat the air fryer to 200°C (400°F).** You want it fully hot before the meat goes in. This is the same way you’d never put picanha on a cold grill. That initial blast of heat is what kicks off the browning.

[![Collage of three images showing how to season the picanha roast ](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Air-Fryer-Picanha-Recipe-Steps-1.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Air-Fryer-Picanha-Recipe-Steps-1.png)

**Oil and salt.** Rub a thin layer of neutral oil over the whole picanha, top and bottom, including the fat cap. Then season generously with sal grosso.

**Here’s the key to the coarse salt:** press it into the surface; don’t rub it. Rubbing just knocks the crystals off. Pressing helps them grip to the meat so they can season the crust as it cooks.

### _Want more of that churrasco smokiness without the grill?_

You’ve got a couple of things you could try, both optional. You can swap some of the sal grosso for a **smoked coarse salt**, or keep your grilling salt light and finish the sliced picanha with a pinch of smoked finishing salt at the end.

You can also add a tiny bit of **liquid smoke** to the butter, although I have tested it and didn’t find it made a big difference, but my husband swears he tastes a little something. So try it for yourself… just know a little goes a long way here, so go easy and tweak it until it tastes right to you.

[![Collage of three images showing how to season the fat cap on top of the Picanha roast while it sits inside the air fryer basket ](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Air-Fryer-Picanha-Recipe-Steps-2.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Air-Fryer-Picanha-Recipe-Steps-2.png)

**For the first 10 minutes, fat side up.** Place the whole salted picanha in the basket fat side up and air fry for 10 minutes.

**Meanwhile, make the herb butter.** Add the softened butter to a small bowl along with the minced garlic, chopped chives, and cilantro. Season with salt and pepper, mix it all together, and set it aside.

That’s it. It’ll be ready to melt into the meat at the end.

[![Collage of two images showing how to make a simple herb butter and a bowl on a brick surface ](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Air-Fryer-Picanha-Recipe-Steps-3.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Air-Fryer-Picanha-Recipe-Steps-3.png)

**Flip and go another 10.** Turn the picanha fat side down and cook for another 10 minutes.

By now, you’ve got a beautifully browned cap and a deeply savory crust on both sides.

[![A picanha roast in the air fryer basket after cooking ](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Air-Fryer-Picanha_Churrasco-Air-Fryer-10.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Air-Fryer-Picanha_Churrasco-Air-Fryer-10.jpg)

**Slice into three.** Move the picanha to a cutting board and cut it into roughly four pieces.

For my 1.4 kg/3lbs picanha (the one in the photos) that’s four natural sections: the tip, two in the middle, and the far end. That tip piece? Chef’s kiss. It’s the softest, most perfect part of the whole cut.

**Back in for 5 minutes.** Arrange the three pieces in the basket and return them for 5 minutes. This is also your moment to salt the freshly cut sides and the inner surfaces that didn’t get any seasoning yet. Do this to taste.

**Flip, butter, and finish.** Pull the pieces out, flip them all, and start working the herb butter into the meat. Back in for another 5 minutes, then check.

We turn and rotate the pieces between rounds on purpose, so they cook at roughly the same rate and the heat distributes evenly through the meat instead of overcooking one side. The exact cooking time will depend on the temperature you like your meat.

[![Collage of three images showing sliced picanha in the air fryer basket, surrounded by fresh herbs and butter on a tile surface ](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Air-Fryer-Picanha-Recipe-Steps-4.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Air-Fryer-Picanha-Recipe-Steps-4.png)

**Watch the tip.** Keep an eye on that little tip piece. It’s much smaller than the others, so it’ll hit your target temperature sooner, especially if you’re going for medium-rare. Don’t be afraid to pull it early and let the bigger pieces keep cooking.

**Pull early, then rest.** Here’s the part people skip, and it’s the difference between perfect picanha and overdone picanha. Take the meat out of the air fryer about 5°C (around 8°F) _before_ your target temperature. A meat thermometer is great to be sure.

Once it’s resting, the residual heat keeps cooking it, and that last few degrees happens on the board. If you wait for the thermometer to hit your number in the air fryer, you’ll sail right past it. Rest the pieces on a cutting board for 5 to 10 minutes.

**Slice and serve.** Carve against the grain, spoon over any leftover butter, and you’re done.

## How to Serve:

Picanha has serious main character energy. Keep it simple and just serve it sliced on a board. Pass it around to snack on while you watch a game or hang out with friends. Or go full indoor churrasco vibes and make all the classic sides to go with it.

A scoop of [farofa](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/farofa/) for that toasty crunch is an absolute must, and then decide if you want a bright [molho à campanha](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/brazilian-salsa/) or a [chimichurri](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/cilantro-chimichurri/) to cut through all that rich beef fat. I also love serving with _maionese de batata_, [Brazilian potato salad](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/mayo-potato-salad/), or _macarronese_, if [pasta salad Brazilian style](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/brazilian-pasta-salad-with-mayo/) is more your thing.

[![Sliced picanha on a wooden cutting board, surrounded by rice, salsa, and other fresh ingredients ](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Air-Fryer-Picanha_Churrasco-Air-Fryer-17.jpg)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Air-Fryer-Picanha_Churrasco-Air-Fryer-17.jpg)

And of course, no matter how many carbs we’re serving, Brazilians will also make sure we have some fluffy [white rice](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/brazilian-white-rice/) to go with everything. You can add [black beans](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/everyday-black-beans/) to the mix if you want more of a proper meal.

For real _boteco_ vibes, the kind you get grabbing a plate at a Brazilian bar, serve it with _arroz de brócolis_, our [garlicky broccoli rice](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/brazilian-broccoli-and-rice-arroz-de-brocolis/).

And if this gets you craving the whole experience, a full air fryer churrasco is absolutely within reach. Picanha is just the beginning.

## How to Store & Make Ahead:

Leftover picanha keeps in an airtight container in the fridge for 2-3 days, though 2 is the sweet spot. Press a little of the leftover herb butter onto the slices before you store them since that extra fat will help keep the meat from drying out.

To reheat, your air fryer is your best friend. Here’s the part that’s different from cooking it fresh: you want the picanha to go in straight from the fridge. Since the meat is already cooked, that cold interior buys you time and keeps the meat from overcooking while the outside warms back up. A few minutes at a lower temperature, around 160°C (320°F), does the trick.

I’ll be honest with you, though. Reheated picanha is never quite as perfect as fresh, so manage your expectations. Skip the microwave if you can since it tends to steam the meat and push it past doneness.

As for making ahead, picanha is really best cooked fresh, that said, you can absolutely prep the herb butter up to 3 days in advance and keep it covered in the fridge.
