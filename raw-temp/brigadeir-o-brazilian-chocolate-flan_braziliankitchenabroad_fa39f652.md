#### REMEMBER!

**Changing a recipe can lead to failure. Substitutions or tweaks might significantly impact your results, so for the best experience, try to follow the original recipe as closely as possible.**

-   **Butter:** for greasing the pan to help create a light syrup that helps unmold the flan. No cooking sprays or other oils here, please. They won’t behave the same way and will mess up the flavor _and_ the unmolding.
-   **Sugar:** for the pan alongside the butter. Same rules apply.
-   **Dark semisweet** **chocolate bar (or melting wafers):** you want to use good quality chocolate that is 50-56% cacao as this specific range is what delivers the best classic brigadeiro-like flavor. If you want a less brigadeiro-like flavor and more chocolate option, go with 70% cacao, and add 2 tablespoons of sugar to balance. While it won’t taste like classic brigadeiro, this gives a richer, deeper chocolate flavor.
-   **Heavy cream:** you want any milk cream that is between 36-40% milk fat. This is crucial because this amount of fat is what gives the flan its silkiness. Do not substitute with whole milk or a lower fat milk.
-   **Sweetened condensed milk:** this is the main ingredient in brigadeiro and one of the things that gives it it’s signature flavor, so there is no substitute.
-   **Eggs:** go for large, or at minimum, medium-sized eggs. Small eggs will not set the flan properly.
-   **Chocolate sprinkles:** go for the best quality you can find. Your flan is only as good as what goes on top of it, so don’t skimp here.

## How to Make this Brazilian Chocolate Flan recipe:

**Preheat your oven** to 175°C (350°F) if you have a fan-assisted convection oven, or 200°C (400°F) for a conventional oven. Opening the oven door to place the bain marie inside causes a significant heat drop, sometimes as much as 50°C, and with the water bath adding extra cold mass, the oven can take a long time to recover. Preheating higher accounts for that loss. Once everything is in, we will adjust the temperature to settle to the low and slow range we need for that soft, velvety texture. If the oven stays too hot throughout the bake, your flan will curdle and the texture will be off.

### Aline’s hot tips:

Most home ovens run hotter or cooler than the temperature displayed on the dial, sometimes by as much as 20-25°C. For recipes like this one, where maintaining a precise, low temperature is the difference between a silky flan and a curdled one, an oven thermometer is a worthwhile investment. It takes the guesswork out completely. This is the one I use:

[![Image of Oven Thermometer](https://m.media-amazon.com/images/I/41HW2Gr0g2L._SL500_.jpg)](https://amzn.to/4sQneK5)

[Oven Thermometer](https://amzn.to/4sQneK5)

[Buy Now →](https://amzn.to/4sQneK5)

**Prepare your bundt pan.** Using a paper towel, rub butter all over the inside of the pan. Make sure to get into every crevice! Add a few tablespoons of sugar to the pan, and shake to coat the buttered surface evenly.

We’re not making a caramel sauce like we would in a classic flan, but this butter and sugar will melt during baking and create a light syrup that will help you unmold the flan cleanly later.

Set the prepared bundt pan inside a larger baking dish or pan for the _bain marie_ hot water bath.

[![collage of three images showing how to prepare a bundt pan for a flan](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Brigadeirao-Brazilian-Chocolate-Flan-Steps-1-1024x683.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Brigadeirao-Brazilian-Chocolate-Flan-Steps-1.png)

**Melt the chocolate.** Chop or break the chocolate bar and add it to a microwave-safe bowl, then add a splash of the heavy cream to the chocolate. Microwave in 30-second increments, and stir between each, until fully melted and smooth, and set aside.

You could also do this in a medium saucepan, though I find it easier in a bowl in the microwave.

[![collage of two images showing how to make the chocolate flan in a bowl](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Brigadeirao-Brazilian-Chocolate-Flan-Steps-2-1024x683.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Brigadeirao-Brazilian-Chocolate-Flan-Steps-2.png)

**Blend the base.** Add the condensed milk, remaining heavy cream, and melted chocolate mixture to the blender, and blend until fully smooth and combined. Be sure to scrape down the sides with a spatula as needed to make sure everything is fully incorporated.

We do this before adding the eggs so we can blend as much as we need without overworking them.

[![collage of two images showing how to blend chocolate flan ingredients in a blender](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Brigadeirao-Brazilian-Chocolate-Flan-Steps-3-1024x683.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Brigadeirao-Brazilian-Chocolate-Flan-Steps-3.png)

**Add the eggs.** Once the base is perfectly smooth, open the blender, add all the eggs, close the lid, and pulse just a few times until incorporated.

Don’t overdo it here. You don’t want to overwhip the flan mixture, so once it’s combined, stop.

**Pour and prep the hot water bath.** Pour the custard into your prepared bundt pan and place it inside the larger baking pan. If you’re using a fan-assisted convection oven, add hot tap water to the larger pan until it reaches halfway up the sides of the bundt pan. If you’re using a conventional oven, use boiling water instead.

[![collage showing how to gently pour the Brigadeirão into the prepared pan before baking](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Brigadeirao-Brazilian-Chocolate-Flan-Steps-4-1024x683.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Brigadeirao-Brazilian-Chocolate-Flan-Steps-4.png)

**Cover and bake.** Cover the bundt pan tightly with aluminum foil and poke 4 small holes in a cross pattern (top, bottom, left, right) to allow a little steam to escape as it cooks. Work quickly and carefully when placing the bain marie into the oven to minimize heat loss. Bake at the pre-heating temperature for 15 minutes to compensate for any heat lost from opening the door, then reduce the temperature to 150°C (300°F) for convection/fan-assist or 175°C (325°F) for conventional, and bake for a further 1 hour and 15 minutes.

**Rest.** Carefully remove the pan from the oven. When you lift the foil to check, the flan will still have a gentle jiggle in the center. That’s correct. (_If it looks too liquidy and unset, return it to the oven for an additional 20 minutes before checking again._)

When it’s perfectly set with that gentle jiggle, leave the flan in the bundt pan, still sitting in the hot water bath, and let it rest on the counter for 1 extra hour until it’s roughly at room temperature.

After that, remove the bundt pan from the water bath, gently loosen the edges of the flan by running a thin spatula or butter knife along the sides with a light hand, cover with plastic wrap, and refrigerate for a minimum of 6 hours or overnight. The longer it sets, the better.

[![collage of two images showing the pan, after the flan has been removed, and the Brigadeirão on a platter](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Brigadeirao-Brazilian-Chocolate-Flan-Steps-5-1024x683.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Brigadeirao-Brazilian-Chocolate-Flan-Steps-5.png)

**Unmold.** When you’re ready to serve, remove the flan from the fridge. You’ll notice small pieces of hardened fat around the edges where the butter syrup solidified in the cold.

To cleanly get the flan out of the mold, place the bundt pan into a hot water bath for about 30 seconds. You’re looking for that hardened fat on the edges of the flan to turn transparent, which means it’s melting and releasing the flan from the pan. Give the pan a gentle shake or wiggle. You’ll feel the flan start to move.

Place your serving plate over the pan and confidently flip it upside down – it should release easily. Brigadeirão actually unmolds more easily than a traditional caramelized sugar flan, so don’t stress it.

[![collage of three images showing how to clean up the platter, as well as decorate the Brigadeirão with chocolate sprinkles](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Brigadeirao-Brazilian-Chocolate-Flan-Steps-6.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Brigadeirao-Brazilian-Chocolate-Flan-Steps-6.png)

Clean up any excess syrup from the plate with a paper towel, if necessary.

**Add the sprinkles.** And don’t be shy! Cover the top of the flan, the sides, and fill in the hole completely. You want it to look like a brigadeiro because that’s exactly what it is.

Slice and serve. The chocolate lover in your life will be so happy!
