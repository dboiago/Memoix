If you search the internet, you're going to find A LOT of ways to make crispy pork belly. How do you know which to follow?? Well, I have gone through many of those methods in order to actually understand what it is that creates these puffy, crispy skin, and what doesn't make a difference. So this recipe has been distilled down to the essentials, along with explanations, so that you only do what is necessary for excellent result. **It is also optimized not only for beautiful crispy, puffed skin but also juicy, perfectly cooked meat.**

## Understanding The Technique: How to Get That Crispy, Puffy, Bubbly Skin?

As I tried the various methods on the web, I discovered that many of them are either not very good, where the skin is crispy but not super puffy and bubbly. Or the skin is crispy but the meat is overcooked. Or it is unnecessarily complicated calling for steps that don't really need to be done at all. (I guess some of it is "trim ends off the roast" kind of situation, if you get my reference!). But most importantly, most of them don't explain WHY they are asking you to do all these things, which you know is not my style!

So, let me explain. **To get that crispy, golden, bubbly skin, you only need to make sure a few things happen:**

1.  **You need to poke lots of little holes in it**. This breaks the skin integrity and allows it to puff into these gorgeous bubbles. If you don't poke the skin, you will still get crispy skin with a little bit of bubbling (think Italian porchetta), but not the thick, puffy and bubbly skin like in the photo. It is important not to poke holes that are too deep, or meat juices will flow up during roasting, making the skin wet, which interferes with the crisping.
2.  **The skin needs to be DRY.** You have heard me say this many times, but crispy = no moisture. If something is moist, it by definition cannot also be crispy at the same time. (This is why anything that's crispy-outside-soft-inside can only stay like that for a short time, because the moisture inside will eventually sog out the exterior.) Pork skin has a lot of moisture in it, so we have to make sure it is as dry as possible before we go to crisp the skin. We do this by salting the skin and letting salt draw out some moisture (and also season the skin), and then drying it in the fridge for a day before roasting.
3.  **High heat.** The skin will become crispy and puffy not when the pork is roasting, but when exposed to a sudden burst of high heat. In Thailand, this is done by deep frying, the Chinese way is done by roasting. At home, we can achieve the same result by broiling, or even roasting at very high heat.

## Important Ingredient Notes

There are only a few ingredients, so getting them right will make a difference! Amounts are in the recipe card below.

![A piece of pork belly with the center cut piece missing](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/pork-belly-center-cut.jpg)

-   **Pork belly, center cut**. Not all pork belly are created equal. Where the piece comes from makes a big difference. The whole pork belly is one big rectangle, as per the pic above. And the middle part of the belly has the most meat AND an even amount of fat, so when you look at it from the side, the fat-meat layers form nice straight lines. In the pic above, you can see that the far edges have less meat, and an uneven amount of meat and fat. The middle, where there is one piece missing, has more and straighter lines of meat. The middle portion is the best for this recipe (which is why half of it is missing, cuz I already took it out to prep!)
    
    Why is this important? Well, obviously more meat and consistent amount of meat is going to taste better; we don't want to bite into big chunks of fat! But also, those straight, even lines means the skin will puff up evenly. This is because after the pork cooks and the meat contracts, if the amount of meat isn't the same throughout the piece, the pork can come out lopsided, with one side being higher than the other. When you go to crisp the skin under the broiler, the higher side may burn before the lower side puffs. Not a problem you can't fix, cuz you can tent the higher side with foil after it is done puffing, but it's best to just avoid it.
    
-   **Five-Spice powder**. In Thailand, our pork belly is typically seasoned with salt only. But the Cantonese style, and my personal preference, has a little five-spice powder rubbed onto it. You can buy 5-spice powder at any Asian grocery store, but you can also simply blitz a few whole spices together, and it'll taste even better! See my [5-spice powder recipe](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/five-spice-powder/) if you want to give it a whirl!

Other than these, you just need some salt a white pepper, although black pepper would be fine if you don't have white!

#### Ingredients for _Nam Jim Jaew_ Dipping Sauce

In Thailand, we are all about _nam jim_ or dipping sauce. And while you can enjoy crispy pork belly on its own, Thai people gotta dip! We always pair meat with _nam jim jaew_ which is tart and spicy, and it works perfectly to balance the fat and saltiness of pork belly. It's very easy to make and, trust me, this makes a WORLD of difference!

-   **Lime juice** - the most important ingredient. Lime juice's acidity is what will help cut the grease of the pork belly.
-   **Fish sauce**
-   **Palm sugar**, or sub light brown or white sugar. If using palm sugar, finely chop it so it dissolves quickly as we will not be cooking the sauce.
-   **Toasted rice powder** \- A key ingredient in _nam jim jaew_ that adds a toasty aroma and body to our dip. It's made simply by toasting raw white rice until deeply browned, then ground into a powder. You can learn more from my post on [how to make toasted rice powder.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/how-to-make-toasted-rice-powder/)
-   **Roasted chili flakes** to add spiciness, which also helps balance the fat! Called _prik pon_ In Thai, these chili flakes are ground from dried chilies that have been toasted until slightly charred for added smokiness. To keep it simple you can use any kind of chili powder instead. For more detail, see my [Thai roasted chili flakes recipe](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/homemade-roasted-chili-flakes/).
-   **Herbs of your choice** (optional). A little chopped green onions or cilantro is classic and makes the dip a little fresher, but you can also do mint, sawtooth coriander, or even dill if you like it.

To learn more about _nam jim jaew_ and 3 variations of the sauce, see my [deep dive on nam jim jaew.](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/nam-jim-jeaw/)

## What To Do With Leftovers?

Try your best to not have leftovers as this is one of those things that do NOT reheat well. The skin will go from crispy to chewy and disappointing. Also, try to serve it while it's fresh for maximum crispiness. Good news is you can roast the pork in advance, let it rest at room temp for up to 4 hours, and about 30-40 minutes before you want to serve it, put it under the broiler to crisp the skin.

### Description

This roasted crispy pork belly has perfect bubbly, puffy skin while maintaining juicy, flavourful meat. It's a simple recipe that requires no frying, no boiling, only what's necessary for that perfect crunch. Plus a tart and spicy dipping sauce to balance the richness.

* * *

-   500 g (16 oz) piece of pork belly, centre cut (see note)
-   ½ tsp 5-spice powder (I have a recipe for this one the website, or you can buy)
-   ¼ tsp ground white pepper
-   ¼ tsp table salt for the meat, plus another ¼ teaspoon for the skin

_Note: Do not make anymore than you can eat in one meal. This does not keep well and does not reheat well._ 

**_Nam Jim Jaew_ Dipping Sauce (optional)**

-   1 Tbsp (15 ml) lime juice
-   1 Tbsp (15 ml) fish sauce
-   ¾ tsp (4 ml) finely chopped palm sugar, light brown sugar, or granulated sugar
-   ½ tsp (2.5 ml) [roasted chili flakes](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/homemade-roasted-chili-flakes/) or any spicy chili powder (add more or less to taste)
-   2 tsp (10 ml) [toasted rice powder](https://hot-thai-kitchen.com/how-to-make-toasted-rice-powder/)
-   A little chopped green onion, cilantro or mint (optional)

* * *

**Prepping the Pork:**

1.  Using a meat tenderizer, a metal skewer, a thick wooden skewer, or an ice pic, poke as many holes as you can in the pork skin, being careful not to go too deep and puncture the meat.
2.  Use a sharp knife, score the skin into 1-inch strips, going only deep enough to break the skin, try not to cut too much into the fat, and definitely not into the meat.
3.  Turn the pork meat side up, and sprinkle ¼ teaspoon salt evenly over the meat. Then sprinkle ½ teaspoon 5-spice powder and rub it evenly into the meat. Finally, sprinkle ¼ teaspoon ground white pepper and press it in. Be careful not to let any of the spices get onto the skin.
4.  Wash your hands if you've got spices on it, so it won't get on the skin, then turn the pork back skin side up. Sprinkle about ¼ teaspoon of salt thoroughly over the skin; I prefer using coarser grain salt like kosher salt for this so it's easier to see that you have salted the skin evenly. Let sit for about 20 mins until you see lots of water drawn up out of the skin. Dab it dry, and brush off any remaining grains of salt.
5.  Place pork into a container and refrigerate, UNCOVERED, for at least 18 hours to dry out the skin. (I usually do it for 24, but 18 is the lowest I have personally gone and was still successful. I have seen other recipes say 12, but I have not tried this out myself).

**Roasting the Pork**

1.  Preheat oven to 350°F or 180°C, set the rack in the middle of the oven. Put pork on a rack so heat can circulate around the meat, then set it on top of foil-lined baking sheet (for easy cleaning).
2.  Roast for 50-60 mins, or until internal temp reaches a minimum of 145F. 50 minutes usually gives you juicy pork that is still beautifully pink inside. If you want well-done pork you will have to roast it for at least 60 minutes for this size, for a minimum temp of 160°F.
3.  TIP: You can take the pork to this stage up to 4 hours in advance, let it sit at room temp, and broil the skin about 40 minutes before ready to serve.

**To Crisp The Skin:**

1.  Once the pork is done, remove from the oven. If you're going to crisp the skin right away, turn the oven off and open the door for about a minute just to release the heat so the pork doesn't continue to roast while it's crisping the skin.
2.  Set the rack on the lowest or second lowest rung - you want the pork to be about 10 inches away from the broiling element.
3.  Switch to "broil" on high setting.
4.  If there's any fat pooling on the pork skin, dab it dry with paper towel, then broil the pork for 10-20 minutes or until the skin is puffed and bubbly all over. KEEP AN EYE ON IT! There may be hot spots that require you to turn the pork around half way through. Or if the pork is getting too browned on one side you can tent it with foil (sometimes the pork is not even and one side will be closer to the element than the other).
5.  \*It's important to keep an eye on it. In my oven, it took 20 mins, in Adam's oven it took only 10 cuz it's smaller and the pork was closer to the element. So it can vary a lot!
6.  Let the pork rest for at least 15 minutes before slicing.

**For the dipping sauce:**

1.  Combine fish sauce, lime juice, sugar, chili flakes and stir until sugar is dissolved.
2.  When ready to serve, stir in toasted rice powder and green onions or cilantro.
