Camarones al Mojo de Ajo translates to “shrimp in garlic sauce” in English. This beloved Mexican shrimp recipe features shrimp simmered in olive oil and butter with lots of garlic and sliced onions. It’s rich, savory, and brightened with lime and parsley.

The sauce, known as mojo de ajo, is a silky garlic butter that coats every bite of shrimp—making this dish perfect served over [cilantro lime rice](https://muybuenoblog.com/cilantro-lime-rice/), [garlic butter rice](https://muybuenoblog.com/garlic-butter-rice/), pasta, or tucked into [shrimp tacos](https://muybuenoblog.com/shrimp-tacos/). It’s not spicy, which makes it ideal for the whole family.

## How to Make Camarones al Mojo de Ajo

**Step 1: Season the Shrimp**: In a small bowl, mix paprika and salt. Season the shrimp evenly.

**Step 2: Sauté Onion & Garlic**: Heat the butter and olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Add sliced onion and garlic. Cook for about 5 minutes until fragrant and softened.

![buttery Camarones al Mojo de Ajo cooking in the skillet.](https://muybuenoblog.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/butter-shrimp.jpg)

**Step 3: Cook the Shrimp**: Add the shrimp to the pan. Cook for 6–8 minutes, turning halfway, until shrimp are pink and cooked through.

**Step 4: Finish & Serve**: Add fresh lime juice and chopped parsley. Serve hot over rice, pasta, or with [homemade corn tortillas](https://muybuenoblog.com/homemade-corn-tortillas/). Optional: sprinkle red pepper flakes for heat.

## Camarones al Mojo de Ajo Expert Tips & Tricks

**Buy frozen pre-cleaned shrimp**: Saves time and mess.

**Quick thaw tip**: Place shrimp in a colander and rinse with cold water for 15 minutes if you’re short on time.

**Don’t overcook**: Shrimp are done when they’re pink and opaque. If they curl into tight O’s, they’re overcooked.

## Storage & Heating Instructions

Store any leftover Mexican garlic shrimp in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 3 days. Do not freeze, as shrimp can become rubbery when reheated.

To reheat, warm gently in a skillet over low heat just until heated through.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How to season Mexican shrimp?**

Mexican shrimp can be seasoned with spices like smoked paprika, chili powder, cumin, garlic, and lime juice. In this recipe, we use smoked paprika, garlic, and salt for a smoky and savory profile.

**What is Mexican shrimp?**

“Mexican shrimp” can refer to wild-caught shrimp from Mexico’s coastlines, known for their high quality. It can also mean shrimp prepared in traditional Mexican styles like Camarones al Mojo de Ajo, [Camarones a la Diabla](https://muybuenoblog.com/camarones-a-la-diabla/), or [shrimp ceviche](https://muybuenoblog.com/shrimp-ceviche/).

**What are Camarones al Mojo de Ajo?**

Camarones al Mojo de Ajo are shrimp cooked in a rich garlic sauce made from butter, olive oil, and plenty of fresh garlic. It’s a classic and beloved Mexican shrimp recipe that’s packed with flavor but not spicy.

**What is mojo de ajo in English?**

Mojo de ajo translates to “garlic sauce” in English. It’s made by slowly cooking garlic in oil or butter until golden and fragrant. It’s a staple in many Latin American and Spanish dishes.
