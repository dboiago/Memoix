A coconut “palette” (pronounced pah-lit in Trinidad) reminds me of childhood days in Trinidad. I grew up enjoying this home made coconut treat, which was usually wrapped in a very thin, parchment-like wrapper. Strangely, I could vividly remember the scent of the wrapping—even more than the palette itself.  
  

[![](https://1.bp.blogspot.com/-DZqZCvQ335w/UD1fmclsEsI/AAAAAAAABvs/uuYVsgimA4o/s400/Trinidad+coconut+palette-12.jpg)](https://1.bp.blogspot.com/-DZqZCvQ335w/UD1fmclsEsI/AAAAAAAABvs/uuYVsgimA4o/s1600/Trinidad+coconut+palette-12.jpg)

The hypnotic ding ding of the palette man’s bicycle bells always provoked an instantaneous smile. Everyone (my sisters and me) would dash out of the crevices and head for the road side, shout repeatedly in our loudest voice, “stop, stop, st-o-o-o-o-p!” and frantically race back into the house to scramble spare change, usually from our daddy’s pockets, hoping to gather enough money to exchange for happiness wrapped in wax paper.  I remember the two most popular varieties being coconut and peanut.

**Trinidad Coconut Palette (popsicles)**  
Makes 10-12

## Ingredients You will Need:

-   [fresh coconut milk](https://cookingwithria.com/2011/08/how-to-make-coconut-milk.html)
-   whole milk
-   sugar, or to taste (I used raw brown sugar)\*\*
-   cinnamon
-   Dash vanilla (optional)  
      
    \*\*Sugar may be substituted with condensed milk, but I used sugar because it contains less calories which means I can enjoy this treat with a little less guilt.  
    

**1\. Make** fresh coconut milk using my instructions [here](https://cookingwithria.com/2011/08/how-to-make-coconut-milk.html).    
  

[![](https://1.bp.blogspot.com/-HWVlwnXRbEc/UD1fm6tlnYI/AAAAAAAABv0/G9xaL5EIEbo/s400/Trinidad+coconut+palette-2.jpg)](https://1.bp.blogspot.com/-HWVlwnXRbEc/UD1fm6tlnYI/AAAAAAAABv0/G9xaL5EIEbo/s1600/Trinidad+coconut+palette-2.jpg)

**2\. Pour** the strained coconut milk in a large saucepan, then **stir** in the milk, sugar, (vanilla, if using) and cinnamon, and **bring to a simmer** over medium heat.   
  

[![](https://3.bp.blogspot.com/-wf5ET4MkVNU/UD1fntzEEEI/AAAAAAAABv8/CAVkm-3Oy7k/s400/Trinidad+coconut+palette-3.jpg)](https://3.bp.blogspot.com/-wf5ET4MkVNU/UD1fntzEEEI/AAAAAAAABv8/CAVkm-3Oy7k/s1600/Trinidad+coconut+palette-3.jpg)

**3\. Lower the heat and simmer**, stirring occasionally, for 15 minutes. Keep in mind that the sweetness will diminish as the mixture cools off, taste and add more sugar if you like.   
  
**4\. Let cool** to room temperature, then **strain** through a fine-mesh sieve.  
  
5\. If using conventional molds, pour the mixture into the molds, snap on the lid, and freeze until solid, about 5 hours.  If using glasses or other unconventional molds, freeze until the pops are beginning to set (1 1/2 to 2 hours), then insert the sticks and freeze until solid, 4 to 5 hours.

#### \*\*Sugar may be substituted with condensed milk, but I used sugar because it contains less calories which means I can enjoy this treat with a little less guilt.

-   Make fresh coconut milk using my instructions here.
    
-   Pour the strained coconut milk in a large saucepan, then stir in the milk, sugar, (vanilla, if using) and cinnamon, and bring to a simmer over medium heat.
    
-   Lower the heat and simmer, stirring occasionally, for 15 minutes. Keep in mind that the sweetness will diminish as the mixture cools off, taste and add more sugar if you like.
    
-   Let cool to room temperature, then strain through a fine-mesh sieve.
    
-   If using conventional molds, pour the mixture into the molds, snap on the lid, and freeze until solid, about 5 hours. If using glasses or other unconventional molds, freeze until the pops are beginning to set (1 1/2 to 2 hours), then insert the sticks and freeze until solid, 4 to 5 hours.
    

Sub condensed milk for the sugar for a creamier, sweet popsicle/palette
