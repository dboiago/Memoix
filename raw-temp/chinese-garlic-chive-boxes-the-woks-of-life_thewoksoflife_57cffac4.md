We already have recipes like our [Chinese Chive & Egg Stir-Fry](https://thewoksoflife.com/2017/07/chinese-chives-eggs/) and [Pork Chive Dumplings](https://thewoksoflife.com/2014/12/pork-chive-dumplings/) in our regular rotation, but today’s recipe is a bit off the beaten path. These Chinese Garlic Chive “Boxes” are hard to find in restaurants, so you may not be aware that they exist!

## What Are Chinese Chive “Boxes?”

There are a few English translations for this popular treat from Northern China: Chive Pockets, Chive Cakes, and Chive Pie. The Chinese name for these is 韭菜盒子 (_jiucai hezi_), which translates to “Chive Boxes.” So that’s what we’ll call them!

While many Northern Chinese families claim to have the best Chinese Garlic Chive Box recipe, they were new to both me (being from Shanghai) and Bill (whose family is Cantonese).

Some people add ground pork and/or minced shrimp to the filling. Others like to add finely chopped cooked glass noodles. Many season their chive boxes with five spice powder or Sichuan peppercorn powder. I have also seen recipes with oyster sauce in the filling.

Also note, the chives used in this recipe aren’t the regular chives you might find in your local grocery store. They’re actually [Chinese garlic chives](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-chives-scallions-aromatics-peppers/#chinese-chives), which have thicker, meatier stalks and a garlicky flavor.

All these ingredients can add extra dimensions of flavor, and you can’t really go wrong. But because I’m one of those people who prefers vanilla ice cream and plain old-fashioned donuts, the Chinese Garlic Chive Box recipe I’ve crafted is basic and foundational. Feel free to step it up with your own creativity! (Just remember to increase the cooking time if you include any ground meat or shrimp in your filling.)

## Shortcut: Using Spring Roll Wrappers

From my research on various Chinese recipe websites, I got the brilliant idea to use spring roll wrappers to make these little chive boxes (instead of traditional dough wrappers).

The result?

Less effort, speedy execution, and a crispy outside. The best part is, even though they’re spring roll skins, you don’t have to deep-fry them! 

## A Note on Dried Shrimp

The use of [dried mini shrimps](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-dried-preserved-ingredients/#dried-shrimp)––虾皮 (_xia pi_)––in this recipe will be new to many of you. Like bonito flakes in Japanese cooking, they are used as a flavor agent. They may be tiny, but they are salted, dried, and packed with flavor.

They go well with egg dishes like [Steamed Egg](https://thewoksoflife.com/2015/01/steamed-egg/), summer squash dishes like [Zucchini Pancakes](https://thewoksoflife.com/2018/07/chinese-zucchini-pancakes/), and clear broth soups like my [Easy Fish Tofu Soup](https://thewoksoflife.com/2014/12/easy-fish-tofu-soup/).

## Chinese Chive Boxes: Recipe Instructions

Beat 5 eggs in a large bowl with 1 tablespoon [Shaoxing wine](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-sauces-vinegars-oils/#shaoxing), ½ teaspoon salt and 1 teaspoon [sesame oil](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-sauces-vinegars-oils/#sesame-oil). Set aside.

Place your wok over high heat. When it just begins to smoke, add 3 tablespoons vegetable oil to coat the surface of the wok. Add the eggs, and quickly scramble them in a circular motion for 15-20 seconds. Once the eggs begin to solidify, turn off the heat. Use the residual heat in the wok to finish cooking the eggs, using your spatula to break any large chunks into smaller pieces. Remove the eggs from the wok and cool.

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-10.jpg)

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-11.jpg)

Heat another 2 tablespoons of vegetable oil in your wok over medium heat, and add the dried shrimp.

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-12.jpg)

Cook until they turn slightly golden brown. Turn off the heat, and allow to cool.

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-13.jpg)

Wash the [Chinese garlic chives](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-chives-scallions-aromatics-peppers/#chinese-chives) clean, and pat them dry with a clean kitchen towel. Finely chop the chives into ¼ inch pieces.

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-14.jpg)

In a large bowl, add the chives, cooked dried shrimp, cooked eggs, another ½ teaspoon salt (less if you use more dried shrimp, as they’re very salty), 1/2 teaspoon sugar, ½ teaspoon [white pepper](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-spices-condiments/#white-pepper), and 2 teaspoons sesame oil. Mix everything thoroughly together. Taste and re-season if necessary.

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-15.jpg)

To fold the “boxes”, take 2 [spring roll wrappers](https://thewoksoflife.com/chinese-noodles-wrappers/#spring-roll-wrapper) (in a double layer, one on top of the other). Place a heaping ¼ cup filling in the middle, and fold the box like a package.

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-16.jpg)

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-17.jpg)

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-2.jpg)

Close the boxes with a little dab of [cornstarch](https://thewoksoflife.com/rice-grains-and-flours/#cornstarch) slurry, and place on a plate or try with the seam side down. Repeat until all your chive boxes are assembled.

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-3.jpg)

To cook them, heat up a frying pan over medium heat, and brush the pan generously with oil. Cook the chive boxes for 2-3 minutes on each side, with the frying pan covered.

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-4.jpg)

Then remove the lid and crisp up each side for another minute until the outside is golden brown and crisp to the touch.

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-5.jpg)

Remember to cook the chive boxes in batches and increase the cooking time if you include ground meat or shrimp in your filling.

Serve hot!

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-6.jpg)

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-7.jpg)

Chinese garlic chives do not freeze well and spring roll wrappers don’t reheat well, so these chive boxes aren’t suitable for the freezer. You can pre-make the filling, but all other steps should be done the day you plan to serve them.

![Chinese Garlic Chive "Boxes," by thewoksoflife.com](https://thewoksoflife.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/chinese-garlic-chive-boxes-9.jpg)

## Recipe

Cook: 30 minutes

Total: 1 hour 15 minutes
