## What is Salmon Onigiri?

Salmon onigiri is a traditional Japanese dish or food that combines [grilled](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/shiozake/) and [flaked salmon](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/salmon-soboro/) with [steamed white rice](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/how-to-cook-rice/). [Onigiri](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/onigiri/), also known as omusubi or rice balls, are a staple in Japanese cuisine and have many other [flavour variations](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/onigiri-fillings/) including tuna mayo and [umeboshi (pickled plum)](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/umeboshi/). Salmon onigiri is one of the most popular and common types of onigiri because the flavor combination works so well. The salmon used to make this type of onigiri is usually salted and is called “[Shiosake](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/shiozake/)“.

![salmon onigiri recipe - two salmon onigiri served on a rectangle plate ](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/salmon-onigiri-12.webp)

Salmon onigiri, and other [types of onigiri](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/onigiri-fillings/), are very popular items at convenience stores because they’re affordable, filling, and portable.  They were also an essential food for me during my high school years. My mother would make salmon onigiri for me while I studied and I would sit at my desk with an onigiri in my left hand and pencil in my right hand.

## Ingredients You’ll Need

To make delicious salmon onigiri, you will need the following ingredients:

![grilled salted salmon on a bamboo tray, nori sheet, and cooked rice in a bowl](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/salmon-onigiri-ingredients.webp)

-   **Cooked short grain rice**: Use short-grain white rice or rice labeled as [sushi rice](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/how-to-make-perfect-sushi-rice/). The rice may be called sushi rice but onigiri is not [sushi](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/ultimate-sushi-guide/). Sushi and [onigiri](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/onigiri/) are different because sushi is seasoned with rice vinegar and onigiri is not. The same rice can be used to make both. In order to hold the shape of rice balls, you need sticky rice. See the instruction to [cook rice Japanese way](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/how-to-cook-rice/).
-   **Salmon**: The salmon onigiri you will find in convenience stores in Japan are filled with salted salmon. To make that salted salmon, see my recipe for [salted salmon](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/shiozake/).  You can grill, bake, or pan-fry the salmon before adding it to the onigiri.
-   **Nori sheets**: These are dried seaweed sheets that are used to wrap the onigiri. Cut the nori into strips or rectangles for easy handling.
-   **Salt**: A small pinch of salt will enhance the flavors of the plain rice.
-   **Optional toppings**: Consider adding toasted sesame seeds, [furikake](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/furikake/) (Japanese seasoning), or kewpie [mayonnaise](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/japanese-mayo/) for extra flavor.

Make sure to gather all the ingredients before you start making the onigiri. If you are making salted salmon from scratch, start making the salted salmon a day before as the salmon needs to be salted overnight.

## Step by Step Instructions

Follow these step-by-step instructions to make salmon onigiri:

1.  Prepare the salmon: Salt the salmon the day before. Grill, bake, or pan-fry the salted salmon until cooked through. Alternatively, you can use leftover cooked salted salmon. Flake or break the cooked salmon to bite size and set aside.  
2.  Prepare the rice: Cook the Japanese short grain rice (sushi rice) according to the package instructions.  I usually use my rice cooker but if you don’t have a rice cooker, see this post to [cook rice without a rice cooker](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/how-to-cook-rice-without-a-rice-cooker/). Once cooked, let it cool slightly before handling.
3.  Shape the Onigiri: here are various ways to shape the onigiri either with a mold or by hand.
4.  By hand with plastic wrap – Layer a rice bowl with plastic wrap. Place 1/2 cup of cooked rice. Make a small well in the middle of the rice. Place a piece of salmon in the center. Top with another 1/2 cup of rice to cover the salmon piece. Lift up the plastic wrap and wrap around the rice ball to shape.
5.  By hand without plastic wrap – Wet your hands with water to prevent the rice from sticking. Place 1/2 cup of cooked rice. Make a small well in the middle of the rice. Place a piece of salmon in the center. Top with another 1/2 cup of rice to cover the salmon piece. Shape the rice round or triangle by squishing gently with your hands. 
6.  Using an [onigiri mold](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B001IF4XG8?asc_item-id=amzn1.ideas.1ZE9UJ0SW1NZ2&linkCode=sl1&tag=chopstchron09-20&linkId=3505c3db150b3b52ff1a92de977c6d23&language=en_US&ref_=as_li_ss_tl) – submerge the onigiri mold in a small bowl of water to wet the mold to avoid rice sticking to the mold. 
7.  Repeat this process until all the rice and salmon are used. Read more about [shaping onigiri here](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/onigiri/).
8.  Wrap the onigiri: Take a sheet of nori and cut it into strips or rectangles. Wrap each onigiri with a nori strip.  \*You can buy nori sheets that are ready cut for rice balls in Japanese grocery stores/Asian stores. If it’s not available, cut the sushi nori sheets to size. I usually cut sushi nori sheets to 1/3 of the size.
9.  Serve and enjoy: Your salmon onigiri is now ready! Serve them as a snack, [bento box addition](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/bento-menu-recipes/), or part of a larger Japanese meal. They can be enjoyed warm or at room temperature.

Here are some tips to make perfect salmon onigiri:

-   Turn the rice evenly : when the rice cooker beeps to let you know that cooking cycle is completed, turn the power off, and turn the rice evenly. The texture of the rice is crucial for the overall taste and shape of the onigiri.
-   Wet your hands: Wetting your hands with water before shaping the onigiri will prevent the rice from sticking to your hands if you are making the onigiri by hand without plastic wrap.
-   Keep the fillings moist: make sure the cooked salmon stays moist to prevent the onigiri from becoming dry.

## Variations to Try

While salmon onigiri is delicious on its own, you can also try out different variations. Here are some ideas:

-   **Mixing the salmon flakes into the rice**: Instead of putting the salmon in the middle of the rice ball, you can mix it into the rice. Flake the grilled or fried salmon and mix them into the cooked rice before shaping.
-   **[Teriyaki salmon](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/teriyaki-glazed-salmon/) onigiri**: Cooking the salmon in [teriyaki sauce](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/basic-teriyaki-sauce/) is a simple yet rich and flavorful twist to this classic onigiri.
-   [Miso glazed salmon](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/miso-glazed-salmon/) onigiri: Another flavor variation is [miso](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/how-to-make-miso/). Cooking the salmon fillet in a sweet and savoury miso glaze adds a delicious taste that goes so well with the salmon rice ball.
-   If you’re looking for more onigiri flavors, check out this roundup of [14 popular onigiri fillings](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/onigiri-fillings/) including [tempura shrimp](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/tempura/) and [yaki onigiri](https://www.chopstickchronicles.com/yaki-onigiri/)(grilled onigiri).

## FAQ

**Q : Is it “sake onigiri” or “shake onigiri” ?**

A : According to [the weathernews.jp](https://weathernews.jp/s/topics/202012/070155/), over 60% of Japanese people pronounce it as shake and about 40% say “sake”. It could be a regional thing too.  People in Hokkaido tend to say shake as the origin of the word “shake” comes from the Ainu (Japanese indigenous people) word “shakembe” which means summer food.

**Q : Can I make onigiri in advance?**

A : A: Yes, you can make onigiri in advance and store them in an airtight container in the refrigerator. However, for the best taste and texture, it is recommended to consume them within 24 hours.

**Q : Can I freeze salmon onigiri?**

A : A: Yes, you can freeze salmon onigiri for up to 1 month. Wrap each onigiri tightly in plastic wrap and place them in a freezer-safe container or bag. Thaw them in the refrigerator overnight before consuming.

**Q : Can I use other types of fish for onigiri?**

A : A: Absolutely! While salmon is a popular choice, you can use other types of fish such as canned tuna, mackerel, or cooked shrimp for your onigiri.

**Q : Can I reheat leftover onigiri?**

A : A: Yes, you can reheat leftover onigiri in the microwave for a short time to warm them up. However, be careful not to overheat them, as the rice may become dry or hard.

Looking for a tasty and easy meal idea? Try this delicious salmon onigiri recipe! Perfect for lunch or a snack, these rice balls are filled with salted salmon and wrapped in crispy nori.

Cook Time 10 minutes

Total Time 10 minutes

Servings: 2 rice balls

-   ▢ 2 cups of cooked rice
-   ▢ 1 piece of cooked shiosake
-   ▢ 1 nori sheet

#### **Preparation of Rice & Salted Salmon**

-   Prepare the sushi rice by rinsing it under cold water until the water runs clear. Then, cook the rice according to the package instructions or by using a rice cooker. \*1
    
-   Grill or pan fry salted salmon following this recipe (internal link). Break the cooked salted salmon into small flakes. Set aside.
    

#### Shaping Sake Onigiri

-   Place ½ cup of cooked rice in a rice bowl layered with plastic wrap.
    
-   Make a small well in the middle of the rice.
    
-   Place a piece of salmon in the center. Top with another 1/2 cup of rice to cover the salmon piece.
    
-   Lift up the plastic wrap and wrap around the rice ball to shape with your hands.
    
-   Toast nori sheet briefly over heat, cut them into 3 strips.
    
-   Remove the plastic wrap off the onigiri and wrap it with a nori sheet.
    
-   Repeat for the remaining ingredients.
    

\*1 prep and cooking time does not include cooking rice.
