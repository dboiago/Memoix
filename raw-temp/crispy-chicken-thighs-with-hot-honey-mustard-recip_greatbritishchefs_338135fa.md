Using steam in ovens is a centuries-old practice but the technology for combining dry heat and steam in commercial ovens (called ‘combi-steamers’ or ‘combi ovens’) wasn’t developed until around the late 1960s. Their popularity amongst professional chefs quickly grew and the tech eventually found its way into the home kitchen too. Low humidity (25%) is great for cooking roasted joints of meat, bread and fish because it uses some steam to keep the food juicy on the inside at the beginning of cooking, followed by a dry heat for a crisp outside. [AEG](https://www.aeg.co.uk/kitchen/cooking/?utm_source=Great+British+Chefs "www.aeg.co.uk")’s SteamCrisp oven is perfect for these crispy [chicken thighs](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/chicken-thigh-recipes), because it renders the fat, crisps the skin and keeps the flesh juicy. Served with a tangy, sweet and fiery hot honey [mustard](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/mustard-recipes) glaze.
