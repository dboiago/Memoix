To ensure the [cod](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/cod-recipes) in this recipe has the perfect texture, chef Robin Gill salts the fish briefly before gently poaching in brown butter for a rich finish. In [summer](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/summer-recipes)[](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/summer-recipes)[](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/summer-recipes), the [asparagus](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/asparagus-recipes)[](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/ingredients/asparagus-recipes) can be barbecued for a smoky finish, making this a perfect starter for an al fresco feast. Robin makes both the brown rice [miso](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/features/ingredient-focus-miso) paste and [bottarga](https://www.greatitalianchefs.com/features/what-is-bottarga) at his restaurant for this recipe, but they can be found at specialist food stores as well.

First published in 2016

-   Blender
-   Thermometer

1

Place the cod fillet in a dish or baking tray and sprinkle over the rock salt to cover. Leave to salt for 7 minutes

-   250g of [cod fillet](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/cod-fillet-recipes), skinned
-   100g of [rock salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes)

2

Meanwhile, place the miso, wild garlic leaves and water in a blender and blitz together until smooth. Gradually add the oil while continuing to blend and allow the mixture to emulsify (as if making a mayonnaise). When fully combined, season to taste with a little lemon juice and salt

-   30g of [miso paste](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/miso-recipes), brown rice miso if available
-   10g of [wild garlic](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/wild-garlic-recipes), leaves picked and washed, reserve the flowers for garnish
-   20ml of water
-   80ml of grapeseed oil
-   [lemon juice](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/lemon-recipes), to taste
-   [salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes), to taste

3

After 7 minutes rinse the salt from the cod under cold water and pat the fish dry with kitchen paper. Cut into 4 equal portions and set aside

4

If using a barbecue, preheat to high. Alternatively, a hot griddle pan can be used

5

To cook the cod, place the butter in a pan and cook over a high heat to create a brown butter (beurre noisette). As soon as the butter starts to foam and is lightly golden with a nutty aroma, remove from the heat and cool quickly to stop it from burning

-   100g of [unsalted butter](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/butter-recipes), cubed

6

Pour the butter into a wide pan (large enough to fit all 4 pieces of cod) and warm through gently to 60°C. Add the cod and gently poach for 5 minutes on each side, being careful to keep the temperature low

7

While poaching, barbecue or griddle the asparagus spears for 2 minutes until tender, turning regularly. Thinly slice the cooked spears into 2mm thick rounds and season with a little lemon juice, salt and some of the butter from cooking the cod

-   12 [asparagus spears](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/asparagus-recipes)
-   [salt](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/salt-recipes), to taste
-   [lemon juice](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/lemon-recipes), to taste

8

To serve, spoon a generous amount of the wild garlic miso into each serving dish. Drain the cod from the butter and pull apart into large flakes. Dress these with a little more salt, lemon juice and butter and add to the dishes

-   [lemon juice](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/lemon-recipes), to taste

9

Scatter over the asparagus slices and garnish with the reserved wild garlic flowers and a grating of bottarga

-   50g of [bottarga](https://www.greatbritishchefs.com/collections/tuna-recipes), grated
