_Tteokguk (rice cake soup) made with oysters is simple and delicious!_

Happy 2018! Did you have a bowl of [tteokguk](https://www.koreanbapsang.com/tteokguk-korean-rice-cake-soup/) (떡국) on New Year’s day? It’s a Korean tradition to eat this soup made with white oval rice cake to welcome a bright, prosperous new year. [Tteokguk](https://www.koreanbapsang.com/tteokguk-korean-rice-cake-soup/) (ddeokguk or ddukguk) is usually made with beef broth, but I made it with oysters (gul, 굴) this year. After having so much delicious foods made with oysters in Korea last fall, I’ve been longing to use them more in my cooking.

The strong briny flavor of the oysters plays off quite well with the soft, starchy rice cakes!

For soups, I like to use small oysters. Chopped up oysters don’t look as appetizing as small intact oysters. I’ve been buying frozen oyster packages from Korean markets lately, which have been pretty good. They are from Korea, one of the world’s largest exporters of oysters.

![DSC 2679 1 e1515472519373 - Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)](https://www.koreanbapsang.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/DSC_2679-1-e1515472519373.jpg "Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)")[](https://pinterest.com/pin/create/bookmarklet/?media=https://www.koreanbapsang.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/DSC_2679-1-e1515472519373.jpg&url=https://www.koreanbapsang.com/gul-tteokguk-oyster-rice-cake-soup/&description=Gul%20Tteokguk%20%20\(Oyster%20Rice%20Cake%20Soup\))

As I mentioned in my [gulguk (oyster soup)](https://www.koreanbapsang.com/gulguk-oyster-soup/) recipe, oysters are packed with essential vitamins, protein iron, omega 3 fatty acids, calcium, zinc, etc. Highly nutritional if you’re thinking of healthy eating for the new year!

Not a fan of oysters? Use clams or mussels instead. When I posted a photo of my oyster tteokguk on [my Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/koreanbapsang/), a reader commented saying he made his with both oysters and mussels. That sounds great too!

Sometimes, I parboil the rice cake slices separately in the boiling water before adding to the broth. This is a good way to keep the soup from getting too thick and starchy, if you prefer a lighter soup.

![DSC 2679 1 150x150 1 - Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)](https://www.koreanbapsang.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/DSC_2679-1-150x150-1.jpg "Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)")

-   ▢ 8 (to 10) ounces rice cake slices tteokguk tteok, 떡국떡
-   ▢ 1 3-inch square dried kelp dashima - optional
-   ▢ 1 tablespoon soup soy sauce gukganjang 국간장 or fish sauce
-   ▢ 1 teaspoon minced garlic
-   ▢ 6 (to 8) ounces small shucked oysters
-   ▢ 1/2 scallion thinly sliced
-   ▢ salt and pepper to taste

#### Optional Garnish

-   ▢ 1 egg for jidan egg garnish
-   ▢ 1/4 sheet roasted gim nori, cut into thin, short strips with kitchen shears

-   Soak the rice cakes in cold water for 10 to 20 minutes.  
    ![DSC 2684 e1515470491204 - Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)](https://www.koreanbapsang.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/DSC_2684-e1515470491204.jpg "Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)")
    
-   Clean the oysters by gently swishing around in salted water. Drain. Rinse again in clean water and drain again.
    
    ![DSC 1811 768x514 - Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)](https://www.koreanbapsang.com/wp-content/uploads/2100/11/DSC_1811-768x514.jpg "Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)")
    
-   Add 6 cups of water and the optional dried kelp to a medium size pot. Bring it to a boil, and continue to boil for 5 minutes.
    
    ![DSC 2665 768x514 - Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)](https://www.koreanbapsang.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/DSC_2665-768x514.jpg "Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)")
    
-   To make the optional egg garnish (jidan), separate the egg. Lightly beat the white by gently cutting it with a spoon. Stir the yoke with a spoon until smooth. Heat a lightly oiled nonstick skillet over medium low heat. Pour each egg part into a thin layer, tilting the skillet and/or spreading with a spoon. Cook each side briefly until no visible liquid remains. (Do not brown the egg.) Slice into small diamond shape pieces or short thin strips.  
    ![DSC 0425 e1483306268902 - Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)](https://www.koreanbapsang.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/DSC_0425-e1483306268902.jpg "Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)")
    
-   Remove the kelp if used. Add the rice cake slices, garlic and soup soy sauce (or fish sauce), and cook until very soft.  
    ![DSC 2696 e1515470435982 - Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)](https://www.koreanbapsang.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/DSC_2696-e1515470435982.jpg "Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)")
    
-   Drop the oysters in. Cook for a couple of minutes after the soup comes to a boil again. Season with salt and pepper to taste. Remove the scum. Ladle the steaming soup into individual bowls and top with the scallion and the optional egg and gim strips.  
    ![DSC 2706 e1515470462395 - Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)](https://www.koreanbapsang.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/DSC_2706-e1515470462395.jpg "Gul Tteokguk  (Oyster Rice Cake Soup)")
    

If you don't like your soup too thick and starchy, you can separately parboil the rice cake slices in the boiling water until soft, drain, and then add to the soup.
