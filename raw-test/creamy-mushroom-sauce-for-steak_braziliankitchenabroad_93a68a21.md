#### REMEMBER!

**Changing a recipe can lead to failure. Substitutions or tweaks might significantly impact your results, so for the best experience, try to follow the original recipe as closely as possible.**

-   **Steaks** – I love using this sauce on filet mignon, sirloin, or any tender cut.
-   **Olive oil** – you need this for searing the steak. You can use any other high smoke point oil for this you’d like, as these are tender cuts and will sear pretty fast.
-   **Unsalted butter** – for building up our sauce. It also helps with creaminess and mouthfeel.
-   **Fresh** **mushrooms** – use any variety you like (white button mushrooms, cremini mushrooms, or a mix).
-   **Shallot** – or swap for a small onion if that’s what you’ve got on hand.
-   **[Brazilian sofrito](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/brazilian-sofrito/)** – or some freshly minced garlic.
-   **All-purpose Flour** – I prefer flour to thicken the sauce. However, you may use cornstarch, but if you do, be sure to make a slurry prior to adding it to the dish to avoid clumps.
-   **Red Wine** – go for a dry red with a good balance of acidity and tannins. Anything with Cabernet Sauvignon, Merlot, and Pinot Noir should work and will contribute to enhance the beef’s flavors and yield a richer, more complex dish. You can also skip if needed, but you’ll miss out on that flavor profile.
-   **Beef Broth/Stock** – this is the base of the sauce, therefore use a good quality beef stock.
-   **Heavy Cream** – for that silky finish. For a thicker, richer sauce, use a full fat heavy cream. Using lower fat versions will yield different results.
-   **Thyme** – you can use fresh (which I prefer, as it brings in more pronounced flavors) but dried thyme works, as well. Want to switch things up from time to time? Swap fresh rosemary for a twist!
-   **Dijon mustard** – a little of its tanginess goes a long way to cut through some of the fat in this sauce and help bring bright equilibrium. I like dijon for that silkier finish, but you can also use whole grain, which will bring in a little fun texture, too!
-   **Salt + Pepper** – to taste.
-   **Fresh Parsley** – optional, for garnish.

## How to Make Mushroom cream Sauce for Steak

**Preheat your oven.** We start by preheating the oven to 400°F / 200°C. We’ll be working to get a nice quick sear on stovetop, but will finish cooking the steaks in the oven. For that to happen efficiently, your oven needs to be nice and hot and ready for your steaks.

**Prep** **your steaks.** While the oven preheats, season the steaks with salt and pepper and let them sit at room temp while that heats up and while you prep. This can be up to 30 minutes max.

**Sear and roast.** Rub the steaks with the olive oil, then sear them on a hot (keyword _**HOT**_) cast iron or another oven proof skillet.

The hotter the pan, the better the sear… which should take about 2 minutes per side to get a nice brown sear on both sides.

After that, transfer the skillet directly to the oven and bake for 5–10 minutes depending on the thickness of your steaks and the desired doneness.

### Aline’s hot tips:

Your steak keeps cooking as it rests, so pull it off the heat about **5°F before your target temperature**. For example, if you’re going for a rare steak (125°F), stop cooking at 120°F and let it rest to finish.

Cooking times vary based on thickness, but for **1 to 1.5-inch steaks**, here’s a quick guide:

[![graphic explaining steak temperature and doneness](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/steak-temperatures-chart-1-1-1024x576.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/steak-temperatures-chart-1-1.png)

**Remove and rest.** When your steaks reach your desired doneness, remove the skillet from the oven. Immediately transfer the steaks to a dish, and cover just loosely with foil to keep warm as you make the sauce.

[![collage of four images showing the steps for building flavors to make the creamy mushroom sauce in a skillet](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Mushroom-Sauce-for-Steak-steps.png)](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Mushroom-Sauce-for-Steak-steps.png)

**Make the sauce.** In the same skillet, melt the butter.

Then, add the sofrito or minced garlic cloves and saute until golden and fragrant.

Add in the sliced mushrooms and shallots next. Season with freshly ground black pepper and salt _(which will help the mushrooms soften up)_, and cook until they soft.

**Build the flavor.** When the mushrooms have softened, add in the mustard, and stir to combine. Then sprinkle in the flour and stir until no white streaks remain.

Pour in the wine and whisk gently but quickly to combine with the floured mushrooms and simmer until reduced.

Add the broth, cream, and the thyme, then simmer until thick and dreamy. Depending on the type of cream you use this may take around 10 minutes.

Now is a good time to taste test to check the salt before you add in the steaks back.

**Finish.** Return steaks to the pan along with any delicious juices, give it a stir to combine and then spoon the sauce over. Garnish with parsley if you’re feeling fancy and you’re all done!

Serve this mushroom sauce over your favorite steak along with some complementing sides.

## How to Store It

Store any leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 2 days. Reheat gently in the microwave, on the stove or in the oven.

I don’t recommend freezing cream and cooked steak, as it won’t be super great after thawing. That said, you can do it for up to 3 months if you don’t mind it not being perfect — just thaw overnight in the fridge before reheating.

Servings 4 Servings

Calories 360 kcal

Prep Time 15 minutes

Cook Time 15 minutes

Total Time 30 minutes

-   1 tablespoon of olive oil
-   4x 8oz fillet mignon steaks\* 4x 225g steaks
-   2 tablespoons of butter
-   8 oz of mushrooms, sliced any variety you like (white, cremini, or a mix)
-   1/2 cup shallots, diced
-   1 tablespoon [of Brazilian sofrito](https://braziliankitchenabroad.com/brazilian-sofrito/) or 4 cloves of garlic, minced
-   2 teaspoons of dijon mustard
-   1 tablespoon of all purpose flour
-   1/2 cup of red wine
-   1 cup of beef broth
-   1 cup of heavy cream
-   1 teaspoon of thyme leaves
-   salt and pepper to taste
-   fresh parsley, minced for garnish optional

-   Preheat your oven to 400°F / 200°C.
    
-   While the oven preheats, season the steaks with salt and pepper. Let them sit at room temperature for up to 30 minutes.
    
-   When the oven is preheated, rub the steaks with the olive oil, then sear them on a hot cast iron or another oven proof skillet, for 2 minutes per side, until both sides get a nice brown sear.
    
-   Transfer the skillet to the oven and bake for 5–10 minutes depending on the thickness of your steaks and the desired doneness you're after.
    
-   When your steaks reach your desired doneness, remove the skillet from the oven. Transfer the steaks to a dish, and cover them loosely with foil to keep warm as you make the sauce.
    
-   In the same skillet, add the butter and let it melt completely over medium heat.
    
-   Add in the garlic and saute until golden and fragrant, about 1-2 minutes.
    
-   Next add in the mushrooms and shallots. Season with salt and pepper, and cook until softened.
    
-   Measure in the mustard, and stir to combine. Then sprinkle in the flour and stir until no white streaks remain.
    
-   Pour in the wine and simmer until reduced. Add the broth, the cream, and the thyme.
    
-   Simmer until thick and dreamy, and taste test to check the salt. Add more if necessary.
    
-   Return steaks to the pan with any juices, stir to combine, then spoon the sauce over, and garnish with parsley if desired.
