## Easiest Matcha Cookies

If you love snowball cookies and anything matcha, these matcha snowball cookies are for you! The dough comes together quickly with minimal effort and can be made with a stand mixer or in a bowl. Matcha adds a gentle bitterness that keeps the powdered sugar coating from being overly sweet.

Unlike bold or grassy matcha desserts, the flavor here is soft and rounded. The matcha doesn’t overpower the butter, and the combination of cake flour and almond flour gives these cookies a light, crumbly, melt-in-your-mouth texture. They’re buttery without feeling heavy and truly low effort but high reward. It’s easy to make a big batch to bring to parties, give as gifts, or add to cookie boxes — they really are the perfect little treat for any occasion.

## Ingredients and Substitutions

Before we jump into the steps, here’s a quick glance at the ingredients:

-   Unsalted butter
-   Powdered sugar
-   Milk powder: optional but gives it a nice mellow milky flavour.
-   Almond flour: can be omitted and substituted with more cake flour.
-   Cake flour
-   Matcha powder: use culinary matcha powder
-   Vanilla extract
-   Pinch of salt

## How to Make Matcha Snowball Cookies

These are really quick and easy cookies to make! Here’s an overview:

1.  **Cream the butter and sugar** until smooth and fluffy.
2.  **Add vanilla and salt**, mixing until incorporated.
3.  **Fold in dry ingredients** (milk powder, almond flour, cake flour and matcha) until the dough comes together.
4.  **Chill the dough** for at least 30 minutes (up to 5 days in the fridge or 3 weeks in the freezer).
5.  **Shape into small balls** (8–9 grams each) and place on a parchment-lined tray.
6.  **Bake at 160°C convection / 170°C conventional** for 12–15 minutes, until bottoms are lightly golden.
7.  **Coat in powdered sugar** while cookies are warm but slightly cooled.

No stand mixer? No problem! You can make these in a bowl with a spatula. Follow the same steps and use a cutting motion once you add in the dry ingredients until crumbly. Then gently press and mix to form a cohesive dough.

## Lisa’s Recipe Tips

1.  **Chill the dough:** Chilling firms up the butter and allows the dough to hydrate, which helps the cookies hold together and prevents a dry, powdery texture. It also makes the dough easier to roll and keeps the cookies round instead of spreading as they bake.
2.  **Don’t overbake:** The tops should stay pale and the bottoms should just start to brown. This keeps them tender.
3.  **Dust while warm:** Coat the cookies in powdered sugar while they’re still warm (about 5–10 minutes after baking). This helps the sugar lightly melt and stick for an even coating; add a second dusting once fully cooled if you want a snowier finish.

## How to Store and Reheat Leftovers

Once completely cooled, store the cookies in an airtight container or sealed bag at room temperature for up to 3 days. If your space is warm or humid, you can store them in the refrigerator to keep the powdered sugar coating dry.

To freeze baked snowball cookies, place them in an airtight, freezer-safe bag or container. They will keep for up to 3 months. Thaw in the refrigerator or at room temperature before serving. If needed, dust with a little extra powdered sugar after thawing.

### Freezing Cookie Dough (Make Ahead)

Unbaked cookie dough balls can be frozen for up to 3 months. Freeze the rolled dough balls on a tray first, then transfer to an airtight bag or container.

To bake from frozen without thawing, place the dough balls directly onto a lined baking tray and bake as directed, adding about 1 extra minute to the bake time.

## Recipe FAQ

**Can I make larger cookies?**

Yes, but adjust baking time by a few minutes. Larger cookies will stay pale on top longer but bake more evenly if you keep them small.

**Can I omit the almond flour?**

Yes, you can replace almond flour with an equal amount of cake flour, though the texture will be slightly less tender.

### Description

_Matcha Snowball Cookies are buttery, melt-in-your-mouth treats coated in a snowy layer of powdered sugar with a subtle earthy matcha flavor. They come together quickly with just a few pantry staples and bake into the perfect holiday cookie or everyday teatime sweet. If you love soft, crumbly cookies with a fragrant green tea twist, this easy matcha snowball recipe is one you’ll want to make on repeat!_

* * *

-   3 tbsp + 1 tsp (50 g) butter
-   3 tbsp (20 g) powder sugar
-   1/2 tsp (2.5 ml) vanilla extract
-   pinch of salt if using unsalted
-   1 tbsp (8 g) milk _powder_, optional
-   3 tbsp + 1 tsp (25 g) almond flour
-   1 tsp (5 g) matcha powder
-   1/2 cup (60 g) cake flour

* * *

1.  Add the butter to the bowl of a stand mixer fitted with the paddle attachment (or use a medium bowl with a hand mixer). Beat on medium speed (KitchenAid setting 4) for about 1 minute, until smooth and creamy.
2.  Add the powdered sugar and continue mixing on medium speed until well combined. Scrape down the sides of the bowl as needed.
3.  Add the vanilla extract and a pinch of salt. Beat until smooth. Add the milk powder, almond flour, cake flour and matcha powder. Mix on low speed for a few seconds, then increase to medium speed and mix until the dough is no longer powdery and everything is evenly combined.  
    The dough should come together when pinched.
4.  Gather the dough into a ball, wrap tightly in plastic wrap, and chill in the fridge for at least 30 minutes.  
    -   _The dough can be refrigerated for up to 5 days or frozen for up to 3 weeks._
5.  Remove the dough from the fridge. Cut into 12 equal pieces (about 14 g each). Roll each piece into a smooth ball and place on a parchment-lined baking tray.
6.  Preheat oven to 180°C / 350°F (170°C/338°F convection). Bake for 15 minutes, or until the bottoms are just lightly browned. 
7.  Remove from the oven and let cool on the tray for about 10 minutes. Place powdered sugar and the warm cookies into a bag or bowl and gently shake to coat evenly. Let cool completely, then serve and enjoy!!

### Notes

If omitting the milk powder, reduce butter to 30 grams.

Cookies can be made using a hand mixer or mixed by hand. If mixing by hand, smooth the butter with a spatula and mix in the sugar. Add the dry ingredients and cut them in using the side of the spatula, scraping the bowl as needed. Press the dough together to ensure no flour remains, then pinch and continue with the recipe.
